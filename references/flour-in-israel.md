# Flour in Israel: Matching the French Types

Checked 2026-10-08. The course's formulas are written for French flours, named by type (T55, T65, T80, T150): the ash content × 100, which tells you how much bran is in the flour (lesson [02.2](../lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md)). Israeli bags rarely print a type. They are named by colour and by how much of the grain is in the flour (white, 80 %, 100 %), and the strength shows only as protein per 100 g. This page matches the three flours every Israeli supermarket sells to the French types, and tells you how to change the water so the dough behaves as the lessons expect.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Brands change their recipes, protein figures and packaging. Product facts below come from the brands' own pages as they were on 2026-10-08. Read the bag in your hand: the label is what counts.

## The matching table

Protein is "as sold" per 100 g, as Israeli labels print it. French specifications use dry matter, so divide the label figure by about 0.86 to compare: 11 g on an Israeli bag is roughly 12.8 % on dry matter. Hydration changes are relative to the [base formulas](formulas.md); hold back 2–3 points and finish by bassinage ([lesson 03.5](../lessons/module-03/lesson-05.md)).

| Israeli flour | What it is | Stands in for | Protein to look for | Hydration vs the lesson formula | Usage tip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **White flour** (קמח לבן, *kemakh lavan*) | The white centre of the grain, about 72–76 % extraction; ash about 0.48–0.58 % | **T55** (pain courant, pain de mie); also T45 for viennoiserie; T65 for tradition only if the ingredients say wheat flour and nothing else | 10.5–12.5 g (Stybel gives 11 g as the average for white wheat flour) | As written for T55 (62–65 %); for tradition start at 68 % and add up to 70–74 % if firm | For tradition dough, reject any bag listing vitamin C/ascorbic acid (E300), enzymes, added gluten or added vitamins and minerals. |
| **80 % wheat flour** (קמח חיטה 80%, often called קמח מלא 80%) | Whole-grain flour with part of the bran sifted out: Stybel describes it as containing at least 80 % of the components of the whole grain, i.e. about 80 % extraction | **T80** (farine bise): estimated ash 0.7–0.9 %, on the T65/T80 border | 12–13.5 g (Stybel's is labelled 13.3 g) | About +3 to +6 points over the white-flour version; in pain de campagne raise 68–70 % to about 70–74 % | Use it 1:1 for T80. For a T65-like colour and volume, mix half white and half 80 %. It is not a T150: do not use it alone for pain complet. |
| **100 % whole wheat flour** (קמח חיטה מלא, *kemakh khita male*) | The whole grain milled, 100 % extraction; ash about 1.5–2.0 % | **T150** (pain complet); mixed 50/50 with white flour it approximates **T110** | 12–14 g (Stybel's Ruta whole wheat flour is labelled 13.9 g) | Pain complet range (72–78 %); start at 74 % | Rest flour and water 20–30 minutes before judging the water: bran keeps absorbing. Check the bag says 100 % or "whole wheat flour" alone, not 80 %. |

Why the 80 % flour sits near T80: white flour at 72–76 % extraction has about 0.48–0.58 % ash, and whole flour at 100 % has about 1.5–2.0 %. Ash rises slowly at first and steeply only once bran comes in (above about 83 % extraction), so an 80 % flour most likely lands around 0.7–0.9 %. No bag prints the ash; a Pekar test (lesson 02.2) beside a white and a whole flour shows where yours sits.

## Reading the bag

| Hebrew | Say it | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| רכיבים | *rekhivim* | ingredients | The only place additives show |
| חלבונים | *khelbonim* | protein (per 100 g, as sold) | Strength guide; higher = more water, tighter dough |
| סיבים תזונתיים | *sivim tzunatiyim* | dietary fibre | Higher = more bran = more water |
| 80% / 100% | — | share of the grain in the flour | Higher = darker, more bran, more water |
| מועשר | *me'ushar* | enriched (vitamins, minerals added) | Fine for pain courant, not for tradition |
| ויטמין C / חומצה אסקורבית / E300 | *khumtza askorbit* | ascorbic acid | Improver: tightens dough; forbidden for tradition |
| אנזימים | *enzimim* | enzymes | Improver: forbidden for tradition |
| קמח תופח | *kemakh tofe'akh* | self-raising flour | Contains raising agents: never for bread |

Israel does not currently require flour to be fortified, but some mills add vitamins, minerals or ascorbic acid to some flours (Stybel's bread flour does), so always read the list. To learn how a new bag really behaves, run the gluten-washing test in [lesson 02.3](../lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md) and the two-hydration test in [lesson 02.4](../lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md).

## If your dough feels different

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| White-flour dough tight, springs back, tears at shaping | Israeli white flour is often stronger than T55/T65; ascorbic acid tightens it further | Longer rests before shaping; +2 points water; for tradition, an additive-free flour |
| Dough firmer than the lesson describes at the same hydration | More protein or bran absorbs more water | Bassinage of 2–4 points; write the final hydration for this bag |
| 80 % or 100 % loaf heavy, low volume | Too little water for the bran; bran cuts gluten films | +3 to +6 points water; 20–30 minute rest of flour and water; folds during pointage |
| 80 % dough ferments faster than the timings | More enzymes and minerals from the outer layers | Check the dough earlier; judge by the dough, not the clock |
| Bread rises oddly, tastes of baking powder | Self-raising flour (קמח תופח) by mistake | Start again; check the bag before weighing |

For general faults, see the [troubleshooting reference](troubleshooting.md).

1. **Sources re-verified with WebFetch on 2026-10-08** (all used in module 8): King Arthur "Classic baguettes recipe", "Classic baguettes bakealong" (https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2017/06/01/classic-baguettes-bakealong), "Baguettes 4 ways" (https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2016/02/29/baguettes-4-ways), "Scoring bread dough" (https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/05/30/scoring-bread-dough: five to seven cuts along the midline, overlapping by a third, about 1/4 inch deep), "Hearth bread recipe" (core at least 200 °F), "Dough temperature" pro reference, "How to tell if bread dough has risen enough"; American Society of Baking "Baking" (https://asbe.org/article/baking/: oven spring 5-8 min at core 35-70 °C, yeast death 60-70 °C, starch gelatinisation from 76 °C, enzyme inactivation 80-95 °C, Maillard above 105 °C, caramelisation 160 °C); INRS boulangerie risks page (burns, flour dust; it does not mention cuts); Ministry of Agriculture salt page (1.5 g from July 2022, 1.4 g from October 2023); Légifrance décret 93-1074. 4. **Consistency decisions to note in BUILD_PROGRESS.md:** (a) home pâte fermentée: a mini PC-02 dough (50 g flour, 32 g water, 0.9 g salt, 0.8 g fresh yeast) made the evening before, 1 h at room temperature then 8-12 h in the fridge; afterwards 75 g (500 g batch) or 150 g (1 kg batch) kept back at the end of pointage for the next bake. (b) Home baguette: 270 g pâton, shaped to about 38 cm or tray width minus 4-5 cm; 3 per load; 08.3 shapes six from a 1 kg PD-01 batch (twice the 06.5 batch) in two staggered sets. (c) PC-02 salt = 1.076 % of the dough; a 270 g baguette reaches 1.4 g/100 g at about 23 % baking loss. (d) Hand-kneading friction factor with four factors: 8 (= 4 × about 2 °C), following 05.3.

# 13.3 · Proofers, Retarders and Cold Rooms

Fermentation runs at the speed the temperature allows, so a bakery controls it with boxes: warm humid ones to proof, cold ones to slow or stop it, very cold ones to freeze. This lesson explains what each cabinet and room does, how a refrigeration unit actually makes cold, how to check that a cabinet holds what its display says, and how to work in a cold room safely. You build a home proofing box and log it for two hours.

## Why it matters

The apprêt is judged by the poke test, but it is controlled by the cabinet: 3 °C too warm and croissants leak butter, 30 % too dry and baguettes skin and stop rising. Deferred fermentation (lesson [04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md)) only works if the cabinet runs its programme; crème pâtissière is safe only if the blast chiller and cold room do their job (lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)). The référentiel names the fermentation cabinets, the cold room, the freezers and the blast chiller among the viennoiserie equipment (S3.4) and asks you to explain, with a simple diagram, how a mechanical refrigeration unit works and how to save energy with it (S4.3; see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md)).

## How it works

### The warm side: proofing

A proofing cabinet holds two settings: **temperature**, from heating elements under a thermostat, and **humidity**, from a heated water tray or a steam or mist generator under a hygrostat, with a fan to spread both evenly. The course's settings, from lessons [04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md) and [05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md):

| Product | Temperature | Humidity | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lean bread (baguettes, rolls) | about 25 °C (24-28 °C) | 75-80 % | fast enough for the schedule, slow enough for flavour |
| Laminated viennoiserie (CR-01) | 24-26 °C, never above about 27 °C | 75-80 % | butter must stay solid or it leaks (lesson [11.6](../module-11/lesson-06.md)) |
| Pâte levée (pain au lait, brioche) | about 25-27 °C | 75-80 % | rich, slow doughs |

Humidity matters as much as heat. Too dry, a skin forms that holds back expansion and colours unevenly; too wet, water condenses on the pieces and gives a tough crust and blisters (ASBE). Industrial pan-bread lines proof much warmer (ASBE quotes 35-37 °C); French artisan bread and viennoiserie are proofed cooler for flavour and for the butter.

**The display is not the dough.** A cabinet's sensor sits in one place; hot and cold spots, an open door and a full load change what the pieces feel. Check with your probe in a glass of water on a shelf for 15 minutes (lesson [05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)), and judge the pieces by the poke test.

### The cold side: what each box is for

| Equipment | Typical temperature | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Armoire de pousse contrôlée | cools to about 2-4 °C, holds, warms, proofs | baked at a set time without night work (cycle in lesson [04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md)) |
| Retarding (pointage retardé) in the cold room | 2-6 °C | bulk dough overnight for flavour |
| Pousse lente | about 10-16 °C | shaped pieces proofed slowly |
| Chambre froide positive, réfrigérateur | about 0-4 °C (0-3 °C for crème pâtissière) | dough, butter, eggs, creams, fillings |
| Cellule de refroidissement rapide | cools hot preparations fast | crème pâtissière from +63 °C to +10 °C in 2 hours or less (lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)) |
| Surgélateur | very cold, fast air | freezes raw viennoiserie fast, small ice crystals (lesson [12.5](../module-12/lesson-05.md)) |
| Conservateur / congélateur | about −18 °C | keeps frozen products |

A refrigerator or cold room **keeps** cold products cold; it is not built to cool a hot pan quickly. That is the blast chiller's job: a hot cream in a cold room warms everything around it and cools too slowly.

### How cold is made

![The vapour-compression cycle: in the cold room the evaporator's refrigerant boils at low pressure and takes heat from the air; the compressor raises its pressure; outside, the condenser gives the heat to the outside air and the refrigerant turns liquid; the expansion valve drops the pressure again](../../assets/m13-refrigeration-cycle.svg)

A refrigeration unit does not put cold into the room: it **pumps heat out**. A refrigerant circulates in a closed loop and changes state (INRS description):

1. **Expansion valve (détendeur):** the liquid passes a restriction; its pressure, and so its boiling temperature, drops.
2. **Evaporator (évaporateur), inside:** the cold, low-pressure liquid boils, taking the heat it needs from the room air blown through it. The room cools.
3. **Compressor (compresseur):** sucks the vapour and raises its pressure (and temperature). It is the part that uses the electricity.
4. **Condenser (condenseur), outside:** the hot vapour gives its heat to the outside air and becomes liquid again.

So everything the room loses comes out of the condenser, plus the compressor's work. That explains the energy rules: a condenser clogged with flour dust or placed next to the oven cannot shed its heat, the compressor runs longer and the room may not hold its temperature. The CNBPF oven guide (2025) says plainly: do not place ovens against refrigeration equipment. Other savings: keep doors closed and seals clean; do not load hot products (use the blast chiller); defrost the evaporator as the maker says (ice blocks the air); do not overfill (air must circulate); keep the condenser clean (by the technician or as the manual allows).

### Working safely in the cold

> [!CAUTION]
> A walk-in cold room or freezer can trap someone and chill them fast. Know where the inside release and the alarm are and check that they work; limit the time you spend inside and wear warm clothing and gloves for longer jobs; keep the floor dry and free of ice. Never block the door open with product, never lock it with someone possibly inside (INRS ED 4473).

A cabinet that drifts (display or probe outside its setting) is reported on a [non-conformity report](../../templates/non-conformity-report.md), and the products are checked: a crème pâtissière stored too warm is a food-safety question for [Module 17](../module-17/lesson-08.md), not only a quality one.

## Worked example

Friday 15:00, Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle. Two problems in the cold chain of tomorrow's production.

1. **The viennoiserie cabinet.** Shaped croissants (CR-01) are proofing; the display reads 26 °C, 80 %. The probe in a glass of water on the middle shelf reads **29 °C** after 15 minutes, and the first tray shows butter shining at the edges. Decision: set point lowered to 24 °C, door open 2 minutes to cool, the trays nearest the heater moved to the middle; apprêt judged by the poke test. Recorded: "Display 26 / probe 29 °C, 15:15; set point 24." The service technician is called for the sensor.
2. **The retarder-proofer for tomorrow's 7:00 baguettes.** Programme (lesson [04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md)): load 15:30 at about 22 °C, cool to 3 °C by 17:30, block until 2:30, warm to about 20 °C by 4:30, proof 25 °C, 80 % until 5:45. Last week the pieces were over-proofed at 5:45. The cabinet's record shows it never got below 7 °C during blocking. The head baker looks behind it: the condenser grille is grey with flour dust and the cabinet stands next to the oven.
3. **Diagnosis.** The unit could not shed its heat (dirty condenser, hot air from the oven), so the "block" was really a slow proof at 7 °C. Decision for tonight: the baguettes are retarded in the cold room instead (reading 3 °C) and proofed in the morning by hand; the technician cleans the condenser; the owner is asked to move the cabinet away from the oven. A non-conformity report is written.
4. **Saturday 5:45:** cold-room baguettes take out at 4:15, proof about 1 h 30 at 25 °C, baked at 6:00. Problem contained.

## Practice

You build a proofing box from what you have, test it empty with a glass of water, then use it for a real apprêt.

> [!WARNING]
> If you use the oven as a proofing box, it is **off**: tape a note "DOUGH INSIDE, DO NOT SWITCH ON" on the controls, and take the dough, the plastic and the water out before anyone preheats. Pour hot water into a sturdy jug or metal bowl on a stable surface, never a thin glass, and keep it away from children.

### You need

- Minimum: probe thermometer, a glass of water, a container with a lid (or bowl and cover), one of: an oven with a light, a cooler box (or large lidded plastic crate), or a microwave; a mug or jug for hot water; a timer; the [temperature log](../../templates/temperature-log.md). Optional: a cheap hygrometer.
- Professional equivalent: proofing cabinet with thermostat and humidifier; programmable retarder-proofer.

### Ingredients

For the real apprêt in steps 6-8, PD-01 rolls (or use the 13.1 doughs):

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Home (500 g flour) | Professional (10 kg flour) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bread flour | 100 | 500 g | 10,000 g |
| Water | 65 | 325 g | 6,500 g |
| Salt | 1.8 | 9 g | 180 g |
| Fresh yeast (or instant 0.5 %) | 1.5 | 7.5 g (2.5 g) | 150 g |
| Total | 168.3 | 841.5 g | 16,830 g |

### In Israel

Checked 2026-10-09.

- **Winter (kitchen 17-20 °C):** the oven off with the light on, or a cooler box (*tzidanit*, צידנית) with a jug of hot tap water refreshed every 30-40 minutes, both checked with the probe. Oven lamps differ in power: some ovens warm to 30 °C and more with only the light on, so measure before you trust it.
- **Summer (kitchen 26-32 °C):** your problem is the opposite. Bread pieces proof fast at 30 °C (about 1.4 times faster than at 25 °C, lesson [05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)); croissants above about 27 °C leak butter. Use the air-conditioned room (often 24-25 °C) as the proofer, or a cooler box with a frozen water bottle wrapped in a towel to hold 24-26 °C for viennoiserie.
- **Humidity:** coastal summer air is humid, an air-conditioned room is dry. Cover the pieces (lidded box, or film over the tray) so they do not skin. Fridge for retarding at 5 °C or below (lesson [05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)). Flour: [Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md).

### Steps

1. **Choose your box** for today's kitchen temperature (see In Israel). Put a glass of water at about 25 °C and the probe in it inside the box; close it.
2. **Empty test:** read and write the water temperature every 15 minutes for 2 hours. Write what you did at each step (hot water refreshed, door opened, light off).
3. **Adjust:** if it rose above 26.5 °C, open it, switch the light off, or use less hot water; if it fell below 23.5 °C, refresh the hot water. Note the rhythm that holds 25 °C.
4. **Write your box's card:** "Box: ___; kitchen ___ °C; holds 25 ±___ °C with ___ every ___ minutes."
5. **Mix PD-01** to 24 °C (lesson [01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)); pointage about 1 h 30 with one fold; divide 6 × 135 g; shape rolls; tray covered.
6. **Proof 3 rolls in the box and 3 on the counter**, each covered. Note the time each set passes the poke test (lesson [04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)).
7. **Bake both sets** at 240 °C with steam (lesson [07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)); compare volume and crust.

### Targets

- Box holds 25 ±1.5 °C for at least 90 minutes of the empty test, with the actions written down.
- Rolls 135 ±2 g; apprêt time of both sets recorded; no skin on the covered pieces.

### How you know it worked

Your log shows a flat line, not a saw-tooth, and you know exactly what keeps it flat. The box rolls reached the poke test at a predictable time; the counter rolls were faster in summer or slower in winter by roughly what the 7 %-per-°C rule predicts. You now own a proofer you can trust for croissants in Modules 11, 12 and 14.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Croissants leak butter in the cabinet | Cabinet above about 27 °C; display wrong; trays near the heater | Lower the set point; move trays; bake at the poke test | Check with a probe; proof at 24-26 °C |
| Pieces with a dry skin, small and dull | Humidity too low; pieces uncovered | Mist lightly or cover; bake at the poke test | Humidity 75-80 %; water tray filled |
| Wet, blistered or tough crust | Humidity too high; condensation from cold pieces in a humid cabinet | Open the door briefly; dry the surface | Lower humidity; warm the pieces gradually (warming phase) |
| Blocked pieces over-proofed in the morning | Cabinet never reached blocking temperature (condenser dirty, next to the oven, overloaded) | Bake early or reshape if possible; retard in the cold room tonight | Clean, ventilated condenser away from the oven; read the cabinet's record |
| Cold room above 4 °C at the morning check | Door left open; hot product loaded; ice on the evaporator | Close; move sensitive products; report | Blast chiller for hot preparations; defrost as the maker says |

## Review

- Proofing cabinets control temperature and humidity: bread about 25 °C, viennoiserie 24-26 °C (below about 27 °C), 75-80 % humidity; too dry skins, too wet blisters.
- Cold equipment has distinct jobs: retarder-proofer, cold room 0-4 °C, blast chiller (+63 → +10 °C in 2 h or less), blast freezer, storage freezer −18 °C.
- A refrigeration unit pumps heat out: evaporator inside, compressor, condenser outside, expansion valve; keep the condenser clean and away from the oven.
- Check the box with a probe, not its display; cold rooms need a working inside release and alarm.
- Exam-relevant (S3.4 equipment, S4.3 refrigeration): see [the CAP exam reference](../../references/cap-exam.md).

# 20.4 · Working Time, Pay and Rights

A baker's month is made of night hours, overtime before a bank holiday, a Sunday morning now and then, and a payslip that turns all of that into euros. This lesson gives the French rules for working time, night work, rest and leave, the minimum pay of a craft bakery, how to read a payslip line by line, who protects your rights, and how a contract ends. You finish by checking a timesheet and a payslip as the EP1 paper asks.

## Why it matters

Checking overtime on a timesheet, counting paid leave, naming premiums a baker is entitled to and the documents given at the end of a contract are classic EP1 applied-management questions (S5.2, S5.3; see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md#ep1-written-test-on-technology-applied-science-and-management)). At work, nobody else checks your payslip for you. A missed night premium of 25 % on 65 hours is about €200 a month at the coefficient-160 rate; a wrong coefficient costs every hour for years. And night work has health effects you already met in lesson [17.9](../module-17/lesson-09.md): the rules that limit and compensate it exist for a reason. You practise these questions under exam time in lesson [21.5](../module-21/lesson-05.md).

## How it works

### Working time

| Rule | Value (checked 2026-10-09) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Legal week | **35 h** (151.67 h a month) | Code du travail, F1911 |
| Maximum a day | **10 h** of actual work (exceptions by agreement) | F1911 |
| Maximum a week | **48 h** in one week, **44 h** on average over 12 weeks | F1911 |
| Break | **20 minutes** once 6 consecutive hours are worked | F1911; bakery convention, every 6 h |
| Overtime premium (no agreement otherwise) | **+25 %** from the 36th to the 43rd hour, **+50 %** from the 44th | F2391; the bakery convention refers to the law |
| Annual overtime quota | 220 h unless an agreement sets otherwise | F2391 |
| Paid leave | **2.5 working days** a month, **30** a year (5 weeks); reference year 1 June–31 May | F2258 |

Overtime is counted **per week**: a 44-hour week is 9 overtime hours (8 at +25 %, 1 at +50 %), even if the next week is short.

### Night work in a bakery

The bakery convention (IDCC 843, article 23) and the law work together:

- **Night period**: work between **21:00 and 6:00**. A **night worker** works at least 3 hours in it on at least 2 days a week, or at least 270 night hours a year.
- **Premium**: every employee, night worker or not, gets **+25 % of the base hourly rate for each hour worked between 20:00 and 6:00**.
- **Limits** for night workers: 8 hours a day (exceptionally 10), 40 hours a week on average over 12 weeks (44 with averaging).
- **Rest**: one extra rest day from 270 night hours a year, two from 600.
- **Health**: an information and prevention visit with the occupational health service **before** starting night work; the occupational doctor can prescribe extra tests (F2212). Lesson [17.9](../module-17/lesson-09.md) covers sleep and drowsy driving.
- **Under 18**: night work is banned (22:00–6:00 for 16-17-year-olds). Bakery is one of the sectors where the labour inspector can grant a derogation, but **never between midnight and 4:00** (L3163-2). An apprentice of 17 therefore never starts before 4:00.

### Sundays and public holidays

The bakery convention adds **+20 %** for every hour worked on a Sunday (article 28). Joint committees of employers and unions set, locally, at least 10 complementary public holidays besides 1 May; a holiday of that list that is worked is paid **double** (article 27); 1 May follows the law. Opening days and weekly closing rules for bread shops are local and not taught here: ask the employer.

### Minimum pay: SMIC and the convention's grid

No hourly rate may be below the **SMIC**: **€12.31 gross an hour, €1,867.02 a month for 35 hours**, since the arrêté of 22 May 2026 (in force 1 June 2026; service-public, verified 1 June 2026). The bakery convention sets higher minimums by **coefficient**, from 1 January 2026 (avenant 139):

| Coefficient (fabrication) | Who (article 9) | Minimum hourly gross |
|---|---|---|
| 155 | no CAP | €12.41 |
| **160** | **holder of a CAP** (or CQP Tourier) | **€12.53** |
| 170 | CAP holder after one year at 160 | €12.78 |
| 175 | CAP + CQP Tourier, or a mention complémentaire | €12.91 |
| 185 | qualified, can hold every post | €13.31 |

Both figures move: the SMIC is revalued by the State (the current amount dates from 1 June 2026, in the middle of the year); the grid is renegotiated by the branch. **Always take the higher of the two** and check both at the date of the payslip. Apprentices are paid a percentage of the SMIC by age and year (for example 18-20 years old, first year: **43 %**, €802.82 a month; F2918).

### Reading a payslip

![A simplified payslip in five zones with the example month: identification with coefficient 160 and 12.53 euros an hour; gross pay 2,375.47 euros made of base 1,900.43, overtime 271.43 and night premium 203.61; employee contributions about 21 percent, 498.85, giving net before tax 1,876.62; income tax withheld 29.25 giving net paid 1,847.37; paid leave 2.5 days a month; with a column of what to check in each zone](../../assets/m20-payslip-anatomy.svg)

The payslip (F559) must show: the employer (name, SIRET, APE code), the **collective agreement**, your job and **classification** (coefficient), the hours paid (normal and overtime with their rates), the **gross pay**, the **contributions** grouped as health, work accidents, retirement, family and unemployment, the **net before income tax**, the **tax withheld at source**, the **net paid**, the paid-leave dates and amounts, and the total employer cost. Keep every payslip **without time limit**; you can contest a payslip for **3 years**.

As an order of size, the service-public figures for the SMIC (€12.31 gross, about €9.74 net) show that employee contributions take roughly a fifth of gross pay; the exact lines depend on the employee and change.

### Who protects your rights

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **CSE** (comité social et économique) | compulsory from **11 employees**, elected about every 4 years; represents the staff to the employer |
| **Unions** (syndicats) | defend employees, negotiate the collective agreement |
| **Inspection du travail** | informs, advises, conciliates and **controls** that labour law and agreements are applied |
| **Médecine du travail** | occupational health follow-up (lesson [17.9](../module-17/lesson-09.md)) |
| **Conseil de prud'hommes** | labour court for **individual** disputes between employer and employee; a **conciliation** phase comes first |

A small craft bakery with fewer than 11 employees has no CSE: the convention, the inspection du travail and the unions are then your references.

### How a contract ends

| Way | Who decides | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **End of CDD** | the date or event in the contract | 10 % indemnity unless an exception applies (lesson [20.3](lesson-03.md)) |
| **Démission** (resignation) | the employee, CDI only | clear and unequivocal, no reason needed; notice set by the convention or custom; normally no unemployment benefit |
| **Rupture conventionnelle** | both, CDI only | agreement signed, **15 calendar days** to withdraw, approval by the administration; indemnity at least the legal one; unemployment benefit possible |
| **Licenciement pour motif personnel** | the employer | needs a **real and serious cause** (misconduct, inadequate work, unfitness…) and the procedure (L1232-1) |
| **Licenciement pour motif économique** | the employer | reason not linked to the person: economic difficulties, technological change, reorganisation, closure (L1233-3) |
| **End of trial period** | either side | simple, with the notice periods of lesson [20.3](lesson-03.md) |

Whatever the way, the employer gives three documents: the **certificat de travail** (proves the job and dates; you are free to work elsewhere), the **reçu pour solde de tout compte** (lists every sum paid at the end: last pay, leave not taken, indemnities) and the **attestation France Travail** (needed to claim unemployment benefit when you are entitled to it). All three are due whatever the reason the contract ends.

## Worked example

Karim Benali, ouvrier boulanger, CAP holder, coefficient 160, CDI at Au Pain de la Halle. His October timesheet: Tuesday to Saturday, start 3:00, **39 hours of actual work a week**, of which **3 hours between 3:00 and 6:00 each day** (15 night hours a week). The bakery pays overtime monthly on an average of 52 weeks ÷ 12 months = 4.333 weeks.

**1. Rate.** Coefficient 160: €12.53 (convention, from 1 January 2026), above the SMIC of €12.31. Correct.

**2. Base pay.** 151.67 h × 12.53 = **€1,900.43**.

**3. Overtime.** 4 h a week (36th to 39th hour, all at +25 %): 4 × 4.333 = 17.33 h a month. 17.33 × 12.53 × 1.25 = **€271.43**.

**4. Night premium.** 15 h × 4.333 = 65.00 h a month between 20:00 and 6:00. 65 × 12.53 × 0.25 = **€203.61**.

**5. Gross pay.** 1,900.43 + 271.43 + 203.61 = **€2,375.47**.

**6. Net.** Contributions, here an illustrative 21 %: €498.85, so **net before tax €1,876.62**. Tax withheld at the rate sent by the tax office (example 1.5 % of a taxable net of €1,950.00): €29.25. **Net paid €1,847.37**.

**7. Status and rights.** 3 night hours on 5 days a week: Karim is a **night worker** (3 h on at least 2 days). Over a year, 15 h × about 45 working weeks ≈ 675 night hours: above 600, so **2 extra rest days**. He needed a prevention visit before starting nights. 39 h a week and 8 h a day are within the limits.

**8. Leave.** After a full month: **2.5 working days** of paid leave acquired; after a full reference year, 30.

**9. A December Sunday.** He works 6 h on Sunday 20 December: +20 % → 6 × 12.53 × 0.20 = **€15.04** on top of the hours' pay (and night premium for any hour before 6:00).

## Practice

You check a four-week timesheet, a payslip and an end-of-contract file, as in the EP1 paper.

### You need

- Calculator, this lesson, 75 minutes.
- Professional equivalent: your own timesheet or hours notebook, your payslips and the convention collective on Légifrance. Templates: [payslip check](../../templates/payslip-check.md) and [rights card](../../templates/rights-card.md).

### Steps

1. **Timesheet.** An employee records: week 1, 37 h; week 2, 38 h; week 3, 44 h; week 4, 39 h. The employer's sheet shows overtime at 25 %: 2, 3, 9, 4 and none at 50 %. Check each week.
2. **Payslip.** Check this October extract for an ouvrier boulanger with a CAP who has been paid at coefficient 160 in this bakery for 3 years (coefficient shown: 160):

| Line | Base | Rate | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salaire de base | 151.67 h | 12.53 | 1,900.43 |
| Heures supplémentaires 25 % | 8.67 h | 15.66 | 135.77 |
| Majoration nuit | 43.33 h | 3.13 | 135.62 |
| Majoration dimanche | 0 | | 0 |

3. **Minimums.** A 19-year-old first-year apprentice receives €700 gross a month. Is it enough?
4. **End of contract.** List the three documents given to an employee who resigns, and say what each one is for.
5. **Premiums.** Name three premiums a baker can receive in a craft bakery and the rate of each.
6. **Leave.** An employee has worked 7 full months since 1 June. How many working days of leave has he acquired?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

**1.** Week 1: 2 h at 25 % — correct. Week 2: 3 h at 25 % — correct. Week 3: 9 h, but only the 36th to 43rd hours are at 25 % (8 h); the 44th is at 50 % — **not correct**: 8 h at 25 % and 1 h at 50 %. Week 4: 4 h at 25 % — correct. Total: 17 h at 25 % and 1 h at 50 %.

**2.** After one year at 160, a CAP holder moves to **coefficient 170** (article 9): €12.78 an hour, not €12.53. Base should be 151.67 × 12.78 = €1,938.34; overtime and night rates must be recalculated on €12.78 (12.78 × 1.25 = €15.98; 12.78 × 0.25 = €3.20). The arithmetic of each line is right; the coefficient is wrong. Report it to the employer in writing; the payslip can be contested for 3 years.

**3.** 18-20 years, first year: 43 % of the SMIC, €802.82 a month at the SMIC of €1,867.02. **€700 is too low**; the difference is due.

**4.** Certificat de travail (proves the job and dates, shows you are free to work elsewhere); reçu pour solde de tout compte (lists all sums paid at the end); attestation France Travail (for unemployment benefit; due whatever the reason for leaving, even though a resignation normally gives no entitlement).

**5.** Night: +25 % for each hour between 20:00 and 6:00; Sunday: +20 %; overtime: +25 % / +50 %; a complementary public holiday worked: pay doubled.

**6.** 7 × 2.5 = 17.5, rounded up to **18 working days** (a fraction of a day is rounded up).

</details>

### Targets

- Every week of the timesheet checked with the 25 % / 50 % boundary at the 43rd hour.
- The payslip error found and the right figures calculated.
- Exercises 3-6 right.

### How you know it worked

Given a timesheet and a payslip, you can say in a few minutes whether the coefficient, the rate, the overtime split, the night and Sunday premiums and the leave counter are right, and whom to ask if they are not.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know 35 h, 10 h a day, 48 h and 44 h on 12 weeks, and the 20-minute break.
- [ ] I split overtime at the 43rd hour: +25 % then +50 %.
- [ ] I know the bakery's night (+25 %, 20:00–6:00), Sunday (+20 %) and holiday (×2) premiums and the night-worker status.
- [ ] I check my rate against the convention's grid and the SMIC and take the higher.
- [ ] I can read a payslip from gross to net paid and list the end-of-contract documents.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overtime all paid at 25 % in a 46-hour week | 25 % / 50 % boundary ignored | 8 h at +25 %, the rest at +50 % | Count overtime per week; keep your own hours notebook |
| Same coefficient after years of work | Classification not reviewed | Ask in writing for the coefficient that fits (article 9) | Check your coefficient each January with the new grid |
| No night premium for a 4:00 start | Premium thought to be only for "night workers" | Every hour 20:00–6:00 earns +25 %, whatever the status | Record start times; check the night line every month |
| Apprentice of 17 asked to start at 2:00 | Minor rules unknown | Refuse politely and report to the maître d'apprentissage or CFA | Never between midnight and 4:00, even with a bakery derogation |
| Rate below SMIC after a mid-year SMIC rise | Old grid still used | Pay at least the new SMIC from its date | Check the SMIC on service-public at each revaluation |
| Leaving without documents | Employer forgot or delays | Ask for the three documents in writing | Know them before your last day |

## Review

- 35 h legal week; at most 10 h a day, 48 h a week and 44 h on 12 weeks; overtime +25 % (36th–43rd hour) then +50 %.
- Bakery convention: night 21:00–6:00 with +25 % for every hour 20:00–6:00, night-worker status and extra rest; Sunday +20 %; complementary holiday worked paid double; minors never between midnight and 4:00.
- Pay at least the higher of the SMIC (€12.31 since 1 June 2026) and the coefficient's minimum (160 for a CAP: €12.53 since 1 January 2026); paid leave 2.5 days a month.
- A payslip goes from gross to contributions to net before tax to tax withheld to net paid; keep it for life.
- Contracts end by end of term, resignation, rupture conventionnelle or dismissal; three documents are always due. See [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md) for how EP1 assesses this.

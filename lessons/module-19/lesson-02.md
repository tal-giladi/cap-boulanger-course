# 19.2 · Briefing Sales Staff on a Product

The customer never meets the baker: what she hears about your bread is what the sales assistant remembers of what you said. This lesson turns a technical sheet and a tasting into a pitch card (fiche argumentaire) the shop can use, teaches the French sentences to brief a seller in two minutes, and sets the honest limits on names and claims. You write a pitch card for a bread you baked and test whether it survives being repeated.

## Why it matters

Giving sales staff a product argument is a competency of its own (C4.2), and the référentiel says exactly what it must contain: the product's **composition**, the **foods it goes with** (association mets et pain) and **how it keeps** (conservation), built from the technical sheet and the sensory analysis sheet; see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md). Lesson [15.5](../module-15/lesson-05.md) placed the product brief inside the hand-over and left the argument to this lesson; lesson [02.11](../module-02/lesson-11.md) warned that a seller repeats what you say. Both are true in money: a seller who can say why the tradition costs more sells it, and a seller who says "sans gluten" or "fait maison" about the wrong product exposes the bakery to a misleading-practice complaint.

## Key terms

| French | Say it | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| argumentaire (de vente) | *ar-gü-mahn-TAIR* | the product argument: facts and benefits sales staff use |
| fiche argumentaire | *FEESH ar-gü-mahn-TAIR* | pitch card: one page per product for the shop |
| composition | *kohm-poh-zee-SYOHN* | what the product is made of and how |
| association mets et pain | *ah-soh-syah-SYOHN MAY ay PAN* | which foods a bread goes with |
| conservation | *kohn-sair-vah-SYOHN* | how long and how the product keeps |
| fiche d'analyse sensorielle | *FEESH dah-nah-LEEZ sahn-sor-YEL* | tasting sheet with descriptors and intensities (lesson [15.3](../module-15/lesson-03.md)) |
| allégation | *ah-lay-gah-SYOHN* | a claim ("riche en fibres", "sans gluten"), many controlled by law |
| mie crème / croûte croustillante | *mee KREM / kroot kroos-tee-YAHNT* | cream-coloured crumb / crisp crust |
| acidulé | *ah-see-dü-LAY* | lightly sour (a levain bread's taste) |

## How it works

### Seven blocks, each from a document

A pitch card is not advertising. Every line comes from a document the bakery holds, so the seller can repeat it without risk (blank card: [pitch card](../../templates/pitch-card.md)):

![Structure of a fiche argumentaire in seven blocks: name, composition and method, taste and texture, pairings, keeping, allergens, and the seller's sentence; each block comes from the technical sheet, the tasting sheet, storage knowledge or the allergen table, with no claim the bread cannot prove](../../assets/m19-pitch-card.svg)

| Block | Comes from | Example (pain de campagne CA-01) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Name | the shelf label and the law (lesson [09.1](../module-09/lesson-01.md)) | Pain de campagne au levain |
| 2 Composition and method | technical sheet | T65 wheat and 15 % rye, liquid levain, long fermentation, baked dark |
| 3 Taste and texture | your tasting sheet (lesson [15.3](../module-15/lesson-03.md)) | thick toasted crust, cream crumb, light acidity, cereal aroma |
| 4 Goes with | tasting and house practice | cheese board, charcuterie, soup |
| 5 Keeps | how it stales (lesson [07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)) | 2-3 days in its paper bag; freeze sliced |
| 6 Allergens | written allergen table | gluten (wheat, rye) |
| 7 Seller's sentence | blocks 1-5 in under 30 words | below |

Pairings are suggestions, not facts: base them on your own tasting and the house's habits, and keep them short. Common French bakery pairings: baguette and tradition with everything, especially soft cheeses; campagne and levain breads with cheese boards, charcuterie and soups; rye-rich breads with seafood and butter; seeded or nut breads with cheese; pain de mie for toast and croque-monsieur; brioche and pain au lait for breakfast and foie gras.

### Honest names

The seller says the name on the label, and the name is a legal statement (lesson [09.1](../module-09/lesson-01.md)):

| Name | When the seller may say it | What to say instead otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| Tradition (pain de tradition française) | only wheat flour, water, salt, yeast and/or levain, permitted flours within limits, no additive, never frozen (décret 93-1074 art. 2) | "baguette" |
| Maison / fait maison | kneaded, shaped and baked entirely at the place of sale (art. 1); for viennoiserie, made here from raw ingredients | the product's plain name |
| Au levain | crumb pH ≤ 4.3 and ≥ 900 ppm acetic acid, yeast at most 0.2 % of the final-mix flour (art. 3-4); proved by laboratory analysis | "pain de campagne" without the mention |
| Campagne, complet, aux céréales | free names, but they must not mislead (L121-2) | — |

### Honest nutrition words

Customers ask "is it healthier?". The seller gives facts, not promises (lesson [02.11](../module-02/lesson-11.md)):

- **Pain complet** is made with wholemeal flour (T150): it has more fibre than white bread. The words "**source de fibres**" or "**riche en fibres**" are claims: allowed only from **3 g** and **6 g** of fibre per 100 g respectively (Regulation 1924/2006), which needs an analysis or a reliable calculation from the flour's data.
- **Salt:** say the number if you have it ("about 1.3 g per 100 g"), never "low salt": "pauvre en sel" means at most 0.12 g of sodium (about 0.3 g of salt) per 100 g, which no ordinary bread reaches.
- **Sans gluten** is never said about anything made in a wheat bakery: the claim requires 20 mg/kg or less of gluten, and spelt, kamut, rye, barley and oats contain gluten too (lesson [19.3](lesson-03.md)).
- No health promise ("good for diabetics", "helps digestion"): refer health questions to the customer's doctor.

### The two-minute brief

At the hand-over (lesson [15.5](../module-15/lesson-05.md)) the baker gives the new product's card and says it aloud; the seller repeats it back. Repeating back catches what got lost.

| French | Say it | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Nouveau produit aujourd'hui : un pain de campagne au levain. | *noo-VOH proh-DWEE oh-zhoor-DWEE: uhn PAN duh kahm-PAN-yuh oh luh-VAN* | New product today: a sourdough country loaf. |
| Il y a quinze pour cent de farine de seigle. | *eel-YAH kanz poor SAHN duh fah-REEN duh SEH-gluh* | There is 15 % rye flour. |
| La croûte est épaisse et bien cuite, la mie est crème, un peu acidulée. | *lah KROOT ay tay-PESS ay byan KWEET, lah MEE ay KREM, uhn PUH ah-see-dü-LAY* | The crust is thick and well baked, the crumb cream-coloured, a little sour. |
| Il accompagne très bien le fromage et la charcuterie. | *eel ah-kohm-PAN-yuh treh BYAN luh froh-MAHZH ay lah shar-kü-TREE* | It goes very well with cheese and charcuterie. |
| Il se garde trois jours dans son sac en papier. | *eel suh GARD trwah ZHOOR dahn sohn SAK ahn pah-PYAY* | It keeps three days in its paper bag. |
| Pas au frigo ; au congélateur, tranché. | *pah zoh free-GOH; oh kohn-zhay-lah-TUHR, trahn-SHAY* | Not in the fridge; in the freezer, sliced. |
| Il contient du gluten : blé et seigle. | *eel kohn-TYAN dü glü-TEN: BLAY ay SEH-gluh* | It contains gluten: wheat and rye. |
| Tu peux me le redire comme à un client ? | *tü PUH muh luh ruh-DEER kom ah uhn klee-YAHN* | Can you say it back to me as to a customer? |

## Worked example

Thursday, Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle launches the campagne CA-01 on liquid levain (lesson [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md)). The owner asks you for the pitch card and a brief to Léa, who opens the shop.

1. **Name.** The label says "Pain de campagne au levain". Check the evidence: the sheet uses levain only, no yeast; the laboratory report on the launch loaves shows pH 4.2 and 1,050 ppm acetic acid. pH 4.2 ≤ 4.3 and 1,050 ≥ 900: "au levain" is allowed (art. 3). Campagne is a free name and honest for a 15 % rye loaf.
2. **Composition** from the sheet: T65 wheat 85 %, rye T130 15 %, water 70 %, salt 1.8 %, liquid levain; slow mix, long fermentation, baked about 40 minutes until dark.
3. **Taste** from yesterday's tasting of three loaves (0-10 scale): toasted 7, acid 4, cereal 6, crumb firmness 4, crust crispness 7. Descriptors kept: "croûte épaisse, grillée", "mie crème", "légèrement acidulé", "goût de céréales".
4. **Pairings** chosen after tasting with the house's cheese and a soup: cheese board, charcuterie, vegetable soup.
5. **Keeping:** a levain crumb stales and moulds more slowly; [07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md) gives 2-3 days in a paper bag, refresh a few minutes in a hot oven; not in the fridge; freeze sliced once cool, up to about three months (King Arthur Baking).
6. **Allergens** from the table: gluten (wheat, rye); no other allergen in the ingredients. The rye flour's supplier sheet carries no "may contain".
7. **Seller's sentence (24 words):** « Notre pain de campagne au levain, avec du seigle : croûte grillée, mie crème légèrement acidulée. Parfait avec le fromage. Il se garde trois jours. »
8. **Brief and check.** You say it to Léa; she repeats: « C'est un pain au seigle, sans levure, il se garde une semaine. » Two errors: "pain au seigle" suggests mostly rye, and "a week" doubles the keeping time. You correct both and leave the card at the till.

## Practice

You write a pitch card for one bread you have baked, taste it to fill the taste block, brief a "seller" and check what they repeat.

### You need

- Minimum: one loaf from a recent bake (campagne CA-01 from [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md), tradition TR-01 from [Module 9](../module-09/lesson-01.md), or pain courant PC-02 from [Module 8](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) or one you bake today from its lesson; its technical sheet; the tasting sheet of lesson [15.3](../module-15/lesson-03.md); a bread knife and board; paper and pen; a phone to record; a family member or friend to play the seller.
- Professional equivalent: the house technical sheets, the sensory analysis sheet from a tasting panel, the written allergen table and supplier sheets, a laminated pitch card per product at the till.

> [!WARNING]
> Use a bread knife on a stable board and cut away from your hand. If your tasters have food allergies, show them the ingredient list first: wheat and rye contain gluten, and your other bakes may carry milk, egg, sesame or nuts.

### Ingredients

No new formula: use your loaf and its sheet. For the pairing test, two or three small foods you would serve with it (a cheese, a soup, butter).

### In Israel

Checked 2026-10-09.

- **French names mean nothing legally in Israel.** A "baguette" (באגט, *baget*) on an Israeli shelf can be anything long and white; *lechem machmetzet* (לחם מחמצת, sourdough bread) is used without any pH or yeast test, and "tradition" is not protected. When you explain your bread to Israeli friends, say what the French name **requires** in France ("in France, *tradition* by law means only flour, water, salt and yeast or levain, no additive, never frozen"), not just its translation.
- **Write the card twice:** in French for the CAP, then the seller's sentence in English or Hebrew for your Israeli tasters. If the Hebrew sentence needs more words, the French one was probably too vague.
- **Pairings at home:** test with what you eat: labneh, a salty white cheese (*gvina bulgarit*, גבינה בולגרית), tahini or a lentil soup are fine for practice; on the CAP card, write French pairings (cheese board, charcuterie, soup).

### Steps

1. **Read the technical sheet** and copy block 2: flours, ferment, method, baking.
2. **Check the name** against lesson [09.1](../module-09/lesson-01.md): write the legal condition and whether your loaf meets it. At home you cannot prove "au levain" (no lab): write "pain de campagne", "levain" only as a method word in block 2.
3. **Taste** with one or two people using the [15.3](../module-15/lesson-03.md) sheet: crust crispness, crumb firmness, toasted, cereal, acid, salty, on 0-10. Keep the 3-4 strongest descriptors.
4. **Pairing test:** taste the bread with two or three foods; keep the two best for block 4.
5. **Keeping block:** from [07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md), write how long and how; add the freezing advice.
6. **Allergens:** list every allergen in the ingredients and in the labels of what you bought.
7. **Write the seller's sentence** in French, under 30 words. Say it aloud and time it: under 10 seconds.
8. **Brief your "seller"** in two minutes using the phrase table (French, then English if they do not speak French). Ask them to repeat it as to a customer. Record it.
9. **Compare** their version with your card: list every change. Rewrite the card so the most-lost item is first.

### Targets

- One pitch card with all seven blocks filled, every line traceable to a document or your tasting.
- Seller's sentence under 30 words and under 10 seconds aloud.
- Brief under 2 minutes; the seller's repeat contains the name, one composition fact, one taste word, the keeping time and the allergen.
- No forbidden claim: no "sans gluten", no "au levain" without proof, no "riche en fibres" without the number.

### How you know it worked

Your seller can answer "what is it, what does it taste like, what do I eat it with, how long does it keep" without the card after one brief. If they changed a number or a name, your card or your delivery was unclear: that is the result to fix, not the seller.

### Self-check

- [ ] Every block of my card comes from the sheet, my tasting or a lesson.
- [ ] I used a legal name only after checking its condition.
- [ ] My nutrition words are facts with numbers, not claims.
- [ ] I said the brief in French and the seller repeated it back.
- [ ] I fixed the card after hearing what got lost.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seller tells customers the campagne keeps a week | Brief given orally, no card | Correct it with the seller today | Card at the till; seller repeats back at every new product |
| "Tradition" sold to a customer from the pain courant basket | Seller does not know the difference | Explain the legal difference in one sentence | Brief names with their condition; separate, labelled baskets |
| Customer complains the "pain au seigle" is mostly wheat | Name chosen by habit, not composition | Use "campagne" or say the rye share | Name from the sheet; say the percentage on the card |
| Seller says "c'est sans gluten, c'est de l'épeautre" | Spelt confused with gluten-free | Stop; show the allergen table | Card states "contient du gluten" for every wheat, spelt or rye bread |
| Pitch card full of adjectives ("délicieux", "unique") | Advertising instead of facts | Replace with descriptors from the tasting | Write blocks 2-5 from documents before the sentence |

## Review

- A pitch card has seven blocks: name, composition, taste, pairings, keeping, allergens and one seller's sentence, each from the technical sheet, the tasting sheet or the allergen table.
- The référentiel's three musts for C4.2: composition, association mets et pain, conservation.
- Tradition, maison and au levain only when the decree is met; campagne and complet are free names that must not mislead.
- Nutrition words are facts with numbers; "source de fibres" from 3 g, "riche en fibres" from 6 g per 100 g; never "sans gluten" in a wheat bakery.
- Brief in two minutes and have the seller repeat back; C4.2 is assessed in the written test: see [the CAP exam reference](../../references/cap-exam.md).

# 19.1 · Talking to Your Manager and Team

Every problem in a bakery reaches the person who can fix it through someone's mouth: a warm tub of cream, a mixer that grinds, a tray of croissants that did not rise. This lesson teaches you to report those problems to the chef in French, quickly and with the facts, to choose the right register for the person in front of you, and to brief a colleague at a hand-over. You write three reports and say each one aloud.

## Why it matters

Three of the four "communicate" competencies of the CAP are about talking inside the bakery: reporting non-conformities in received goods (C4.1), using language suited to the situation (C4.3) and reporting non-conformities and malfunctions during production (C4.4). They are assessed in the written test from documents such as a delivery note and in the practical test while you work; see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md). In real work a report decides money and safety: a delivery signed without remark becomes the bakery's problem, a fault in a machine's guard that nobody mentions is an accident waiting, and a batch nobody flags goes on sale. French labour law makes the alarm part of the job: a worker must alert the employer **immediately** to a situation that looks seriously and imminently dangerous, or to a defect in a protection system (Code du travail L4131-1). Lesson [21.6](../module-21/lesson-06.md) practises these reports as timed EP1 questions.

> [!NOTE]
> This is the first lesson of a module you practise rather than read. Watching role plays is not doing them: say every phrase aloud, record yourself and listen back. The Academy cannot hear or grade your French; compare your recordings and reports with the targets and the rubric yourself, and remember that self-assessment does not replace the official exam.

## How it works

### Who you talk to

Lesson [01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md) set out the people in a craft bakery. The line you report along is short: the **ouvrier boulanger** or **apprenti** tells the **chef boulanger** (head of production), who decides or passes it to the **chef d'entreprise** (owner), who deals with suppliers, technicians and authorities. The sales staff (**personnel de vente**) sit beside that line: they get your hand-over brief and send back customers' questions and complaints.

![Reporting chain in a craft bakery: the ouvrier or apprentice reports delivery problems at once before the driver leaves, machine faults immediately after stopping and tagging the machine, and product non-conformities before the product reaches the shop, to the chef boulanger, who decides or passes it to the chef d'entreprise; the sales staff exchange the hand-over brief and customer feedback with the fournil](../../assets/m19-reporting-chain.svg)

### The four parts of every report

A good report is the same shape whether it lasts ten seconds at the oven or fills a [non-conformity report](../../templates/non-conformity-report.md):

1. **Facts and figures** (*les faits*): what, where, how many, measured. "The cream is at 9 °C", not "the cream is warm".
2. **What you did** (*ce que j'ai fait*): set aside, labelled, machine stopped, reservation written.
3. **What you propose** (*ce que je propose*): refuse, call the technician, switch product, re-bake.
4. **The manager decides** (*vous décidez*): you ask; you do not hide a problem or settle it alone beyond your role.

Then you write it down. The spoken report gets the decision fast; the written one keeps the evidence for the supplier, the insurer or an inspection, and for the cause hunt of lesson [16.1](../module-16/lesson-01.md).

### Timing: before the problem spreads

| Problem | When you report | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery (C4.1) | at once, **before the driver leaves** | after a clean signature the goods are the bakery's; the reservation must be on the bon de livraison |
| Machine fault or danger (C4.4) | **immediately**, after stopping the machine and tagging it "hors service" | L4131-1; the next person must not restart it |
| Product non-conformity (C4.4) | **before the product reaches the shop**, and before the next batch repeats the error | a set-aside tray costs a tray; a sold one costs customers |

For deliveries, the bakery's numbers come from its hygiene plan; the CNBPF guide gives a target of 0 to +4 °C for very perishable chilled products with a tolerance of +3 °C, so **above +7 °C the product is refused**, and frozen products are refused above **−15 °C**; any product past its use-by date or in damaged packaging is refused too. You do not decide the refusal alone if your house rule says the chef decides: you measure, keep the product apart and report while the driver is still there.

For machines, your job is to **stop, tag and report**, never to open a guard or "have a look" at the motor. The use and safety manual and the risk assessment (document unique) say who may intervene (lesson [13.1](../module-13/lesson-01.md)).

### Registers: tu, vous and fournil words

The référentiel asks you to recognise the registers of language and to adapt to the person. In practice:

| Register | Example | Use it with |
|---|---|---|
| Familiar (familier) | « T'as vu, la pâte, elle relâche grave. » | close colleagues, never customers, never in writing |
| Everyday (courant) | « La pâte relâche, je fais un rabat. » | the team and the chef: clear and professional |
| Formal (soutenu) | « Je me permets de vous signaler un retard de livraison. » | a supplier, an inspector, a written message |

**Tu or vous.** Many fournils say "tu" between bakers. Start with **vous** to the chef, the owner, suppliers and every customer, and switch only when invited (« On se tutoie ? »). **Fournil words** (pâte qui relâche, coup de feu, frasage) are precise inside the team; with sales staff and customers use plain words (lesson [19.4](lesson-04.md)). Non-verbal language counts too: a report made while walking away, without looking at the chef, sounds like an excuse.

### Briefing a colleague

A hand-over brief (*passation de consignes*) follows the production order, not your memory ([hand-over brief](../../templates/hand-over-brief.md) template): what is **done**, what is **in progress and when it is due**, what is **waiting where** (container, cold room, shelf, label), and what **went wrong**. Example, at 13:30:

> « Pour la relève : les pâtons de tradition sont en pointage retardé, chambre froide, bacs 3 et 4, sortie demain 4 h. La poolish pour les baguettes est prête à 14 h. Le laminoir est hors service, le technicien passe à 16 h : les croissants de demain sont tourés à la main. »

(For the next shift: tradition doughs in retarded bulk, cold room, tubs 3 and 4, out at 4:00 tomorrow. The baguette poolish is ready at 14:00. The sheeter is out of order, technician at 16:00: tomorrow's croissants are turned by hand.)

## Worked example

Tuesday, 6:40, Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle. You are the commis on reception. The dairy delivery arrives: 2 × 5 L crème fleurie 35 %, 10 kg beurre de tourage, 6 L lait entier. The bon de livraison lists "crème 35 % ×2, beurre tourage 10 kg, lait ×6".

1. **Measure.** Probe between two packs: butter 3 °C, milk 4 °C, cream **9 °C**. Cream DLC in 6 days; one cream tub dented but sealed.
2. **Compare with the house rule.** Very perishable: target 0/+4 °C, limit +7 °C (CNBPF). Cream at 9 °C is over the limit: **non-conformity**. Butter and milk conform.
3. **Act within your role.** Cream set apart on the reception table with "ne pas utiliser", not in the cold room with the accepted goods; driver asked to wait.
4. **Report, spoken (20 seconds):** « Chef, j'ai un problème sur la livraison de crème : elle est à neuf degrés, la limite est à sept. Je l'ai mise de côté. Le beurre et le lait sont conformes. Le livreur attend : je fais une réserve et on refuse la crème ? »
5. **The chef decides:** refuse both cream tubs. You write on the bon de livraison before signing: « Crème 35 % ×2 refusée, température 9 °C à réception (limite 7 °C), 6 h 42. » Driver signs the reservation too.
6. **Written report** on the [non-conformity report](../../templates/non-conformity-report.md): type received goods; product and lot number; facts (9 °C, probe, time); action (set aside, refused, reservation); probable cause ("truck cold chain"; you do not accuse, you state); suggestion (ask the supplier for a credit note and a delivery before 9:00 for the crème pâtissière, or use the UHT cream in stock for today).
7. **Knock-on brief:** the tourier is told at once that the crème pâtissière for the pains aux raisins waits for the replacement cream: « Pas de crème fraîche avant neuf heures : on fait la crème pâtissière avec la crème UHT du stock, le chef est d'accord. »

## Practice

You write three reports (one delivery, one machine, one product), say each aloud in French, and brief an imaginary colleague. If something really goes wrong in your kitchen this week, use it instead of the scenario.

### You need

- Minimum: three printed or copied [non-conformity reports](../../templates/non-conformity-report.md), a phone to record audio, a probe thermometer, the [delivery check](../../templates/delivery-check.md) template for scenario A, this lesson's phrase table.
- Professional equivalent: the house reception procedure and log, the delivery note and supplier contacts, the maintenance log (cahier d'entretien), the "hors service" tags, the shift hand-over sheet.

### Ingredients

None to bake. For scenario A you measure one real chilled product from your fridge (milk, cream or butter) to practise the probe placement.

### In Israel

Checked 2026-10-09.

- **You are practising French from Israel.** Say the reports aloud in French first, then once in English or Hebrew to check you understood what you said. French courses for adults are offered by the Institut français d'Israël in Tel Aviv, Haifa and Nazareth; a conversation partner from the large French-speaking community (many French-speaking families live in Netanya, Ashdod, Jerusalem and Tel Aviv) can play the chef.
- **Practise the probe at the supermarket door.** Israeli supermarkets deliver chilled products too: measure the milk or cream (*shamenet*, שמנת) from a home delivery the moment it arrives and write the reading as if it were a reception check.
- The French rules stay the subject: the 0/+4 °C target and +7 °C limit you report against are the French bakery guide's, not a local standard.

### Steps

1. **Scenario A, delivery (C4.1).** A flour delivery: the bon de livraison says "T65 tradition 25 kg ×10"; you count 9 sacks, one torn and damp at the bottom. Fill in the delivery check, then a non-conformity report, and write the reservation sentence for the delivery note in French.
2. **Scenario B, machine (C4.4).** During the second mix the spiral mixer smells hot and the bowl stops turning while the spiral keeps going; you stop it with the emergency stop. Write the report: what you saw, heard, smelled; what you did (stop, tag, unplug only if the house rule allows); what you did **not** do; what you propose for the dough in the bowl.
3. **Scenario C, product (C4.4).** At 6:20, on the second croissant batch, 14 of 40 croissants are flat, with butter on the tray (lesson [16.5](../module-16/lesson-05.md)). The proofer showed 31 °C. Write the report with the count, the probable cause and the proposal (sell the 26 good ones, not the 14; proofer set point checked; next batch).
4. **Say each report aloud** in French in under 30 seconds, using the phrase table. Record it.
5. **Listen back** with the self-check below; record again until every report has the four parts.
6. **Hand-over brief.** Write and record a 45-second brief for the afternoon baker covering scenarios B and C and one dough in progress.
7. **Register switch.** Rewrite scenario C's report as a short written message to the owner (formal register, vous, no fournil slang).

### Targets

- Three written reports, each with facts and numbers, action taken, probable cause and proposal; no blank field in the template.
- Each spoken report under 30 seconds, in French, with at least one number and one question for the decision.
- The delivery-note reservation in one French sentence: product, quantity, the defect, the time.
- One hand-over brief under 45 seconds: done, in progress with times, waiting where, problems.

### How you know it worked

Someone who knows nothing about the morning could act on your written report alone. In your recording, the chef would know within ten seconds what is wrong and what you want decided; there is no "euh, en fait, je crois que…" before the fact. Your machine report says clearly that you stopped the machine and touched nothing inside it.

### Self-check

- [ ] Every report starts with the facts and a number, not an opinion.
- [ ] I said what I did and what I propose, and I asked for the decision.
- [ ] The delivery reservation is written before signing and names product, quantity, defect and time.
- [ ] I stopped, tagged and reported the machine; I did not repair it.
- [ ] I used vous with the chef and the owner, and plain words in the formal message.
- [ ] My hand-over brief tells the next person where things are and when they are due.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier refuses a credit note for warm cream | Delivery note signed with no reservation | Report to the chef with your reading and the time; keep the product apart | Measure and write the reservation before signing, every time |
| Chef asks three questions before understanding the problem | Report started with a story, no number | Restart: product, measure, what you did | Use the four-part order; rehearse the first sentence |
| Machine restarted by a colleague and fails again | Not tagged, not reported, or reported only orally | Stop it, tag it "hors service", tell the chef | Tag plus written report in the maintenance log |
| Faulty batch reaches the shop | Set aside without telling the sales staff | Withdraw it from the shelf; tell the chef | Report before the hand-over; mark the tray clearly |
| Tension with a colleague after a report | Familiar or accusing words ("t'as encore raté…") | Restate the facts calmly, without blame | Describe the product and the measure, not the person |

## Review

- Report in four parts: facts and figures, what you did, what you propose, then the manager decides; write it on the non-conformity report afterwards.
- Delivery problems before the driver leaves, with a reservation on the bon de livraison; machine dangers immediately after stopping and tagging (L4131-1); product faults before they reach the shop.
- Vous to the chef, the owner, suppliers and customers until invited; fournil words inside the team, plain words outside it.
- A hand-over brief says what is done, what is due when, what waits where and what went wrong.
- C4.1, C4.3 and C4.4 are assessed in the written and practical tests: see [the CAP exam reference](../../references/cap-exam.md).

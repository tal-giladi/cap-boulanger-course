# 15.4 · Checking Weights and Quantities

A customer can see a pale crust, but cannot see that a baguette is 15 g light or that the order is two pieces short: the baker has to check. This lesson gives you the weight-control sheet a bakery uses after every batch, the decisions it leads to, and the French rules that sit behind weights: what the law fixes, what it does not, and what it expects from your scale. You fill in a weight-control sheet for your own batch.

## Why it matters

The référentiel gives you the order and the technical sheets and expects "the quantities produced are in line with the order" (C3.2); when they are not, you report it (C4.4). Weight is also money and trust: a pâton 10 g heavy on 48 baguettes a day gives away about 175 kg of dough a year; a pâton 10 g light sells less than the customer expects. And because salt stays in the bread while water leaves, the weight you measure is also how you check the salt limit (lessons [06.3](../module-06/lesson-03.md) and [08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)). How weights and quantities count in the production test is in [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md).

## How it works

### Count first, against the order

Before any weighing, count every product against the order form and the production sheet: pieces baked, pieces set aside (dark, misshapen, broken), pieces available. If the order is short, the sales staff and the manager need to know **now**, while another load can still be baked or the customer warned, not at 11:00 when the restaurant calls.

### The weight-control sheet

| Step | What you do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Target | Write the baked target: the announced weight, or pâton × (1 − measured loss) | you need a reference to judge against |
| 2. Sample | Weigh at least 5 cooled pieces (10 for a large batch) taken from different places: first and last trays, each deck, front and back | one place hides oven and dividing patterns |
| 3. Average | Sum ÷ number of pieces; compare with the target | is the pâton weight and the bake right? |
| 4. Spread | Lightest and heaviest piece; count the pieces outside the tolerance | is the dividing even? (lesson [04.3](../module-04/lesson-03.md)) |
| 5. Loss | (pâton − average) ÷ pâton × 100 | is the bake right? (lesson [06.3](../module-06/lesson-03.md)) |
| 6. Salt | Salt per 100 g of the lightest piece = pâton × salt % of dough ÷ cooled weight × 100 | the lightest piece is the saltiest |
| 7. Decision | Release, set aside, adjust or report (table below) | the sheet exists to decide something |

The salt % of dough for the course's sheets: PC-02 1.076 %, TR-01 1.044 %, CA-01 1.048 %, CO-01 0.958 %, PM-01 (pain de mie) 0.879 %, VI-01 1.004 %. The limits are 1.4 g per 100 g for pain courant (and, as good practice, tradition), 1.3 g for wholemeal and cereal bread, 1.1 g for pain de mie (lesson [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)).

**Which tolerance?** At dividing, the **tolérance de poids** is set on the pâton, for example ± 5 g on 350 g. After baking, the course rubric uses ± 3 % of the baked target for 2 points and ± 5 % for 1 point ([quality rubric](../../templates/quality-rubric.md)). A bakery writes its own tolerance on the technical sheet; what matters is that it is written, measured and acted on.

### Deciding from the sheet

| What the sheet shows | Likely meaning | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Average on target, all pieces in tolerance | dividing and baking right | release; file the sheet |
| Average on target, some pieces outside | dividing uneven, or one oven position | set the pieces aside; check the divider or the bench scale and the oven map |
| All pieces light by about the same amount | pâton too light, or bake too long or too hot | adjust the pâton or the bake for the next batch; report if the order is affected |
| Salt over the limit on light pieces | high baking loss concentrates the salt | shorten or cool the bake; report |
| Count short of the order | pieces lost or set aside | tell sales staff and the manager at once; bake more if possible |

### What French law says about weights

*Checked 2026-10-09; rules change, so check the current texts before relying on details.*

- **No legal weight for a type of bread.** A baker is free to choose the weight and the price of a baguette. In a 2020 answer to a written question in the Assemblée nationale, the government recalled that the rules on displaying bread prices date from 1978 (a placard for each category of bread on display, and a poster in the window for the main traditional breads) and do not regulate the weight of bread.
- **What you announce, you must deliver.** If a placard, a label or an order states a weight, that weight is a promise. Deceiving a customer about the quantity of the goods delivered is the offence of *tromperie* (Code de la consommation, article L441-1). Practical rule: set the pâton so that the **average** is a few grams **above** the announced weight and no piece is clearly light.
- **Prepackages have a tolerance table.** Products packed in advance with a fixed quantity printed on them (a bag of sliced pain de mie "500 g", a pack of six rolls "300 g") fall under décret n° 78-166, from 5 g up. The **average** content of a lot must not be below the nominal quantity, and only a small share of packs may fall short by more than the maximum tolerated shortfall:

| Nominal quantity | Maximum tolerated shortfall |
|---|---|
| 50-100 g | 4.5 g |
| 100-200 g | 4.5 % |
| 200-300 g | 9 g |
| 300-500 g | 3 % |
| 500-1,000 g | 15 g |
| 1-10 kg | 1.5 % |

- **Scales used for sale are regulated.** A scale used to sell by weight or to make prepackages must be a certified trade model (not a kitchen or bathroom scale), have a **carnet métrologique** within a month of being put into service, and pass a **periodic verification** by an approved body: every 2 years for scales up to 30 kg used only for direct sale, every year when you make prepackages. A green sticker shows a valid verification; a red sticker means the scale must not be used. The tare (paper, bag, tray) is always deducted. Production scales that weigh ingredients are not trade scales, but check them yourself with a known weight (lesson [01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md)).

What to write on the shelf and the label (name, price, allergens) is covered in lesson [02.11](../module-02/lesson-11.md) and in [Module 17](../module-17/lesson-06.md).

## Worked example

Saturday, 6:55, Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle. The shop's placard announces "Baguette 280 g". Order for the morning: 48 baguettes PC-02 (350 g pâtons) and 8 bags of sliced pain de mie labelled 500 g, packed at 6:30 for a café.

**1. Count.** Baguettes: 48 baked, 1 dropped at unloading: **47 available**. Tell the sales staff now: 47 for the shelf, one more from the 7:30 load. Pain de mie: 8 bags. Order otherwise covered.

**2. Baguette sample.** Ten cooled pieces from both decks: 284, 279, 276, 281, 270, 283, 278, 285, 280, 277 g.
- Average 2,793 ÷ 10 = **279.3 g**: 0.7 g under the announced 280 g.
- Spread 270-285 g. Bakery tolerance ± 3 % of 280 g = 271.6-288.4 g: **one piece out** (270 g).
- Loss (350 − 279.3) ÷ 350 = **20.2 %**.
- Salt in the lightest piece: 350 × 1.076 % = 3.77 g in 270 g = **1.40 g per 100 g**: right at the limit.

**3. Pain de mie bags (prepackage, 500 g).** 503, 498, 507, 489, 501, 496, 510, 492 g. Maximum tolerated shortfall at 500 g: 15 g, so a bag under 485 g is defective: **none**. But the average is 3,996 ÷ 8 = **499.5 g**, below the 500 g on the label: the lot does not meet the average rule.

**4. Decisions.**
- The 270 g baguette is set aside (staff or a lower-price shelf, as the manager decides). The divider sheet shows that piece came from the end of the dough (the last press): check the divider's last cut.
- The average is under the announced weight: raise the pâton. Aim at 283 g: 283 ÷ (1 − 0.202) = 354.6 → **355 g** from Monday, written on the technical sheet.
- The pain de mie: weigh each slice pack before closing and aim at 505-510 g; re-pack the two lightest bags with an extra end slice if the manager agrees.

**5. Report (C4.4).** "Contrôle poids 6:55: baguettes moyenne 279,3 g pour 280 g annoncés (10 pièces), une pièce à 270 g retirée (dernière pesée de la diviseuse). Proposition: pâton 355 g, vérifier la dernière division. Pain de mie 500 g: moyenne 499,5 g sur 8 sachets, aucun sous 485 g; objectif de remplissage 505-510 g. Une baguette tombée: 47 au lieu de 48 à 7:00, complétée à 7:30."

## Practice

You fill in a weight-control sheet for your own batch and, for comparison, for a bought pack with a printed weight. Then you do four exercises.

> [!CAUTION]
> If you bake for this lesson, follow the oven and steam safety of lesson [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md): dry oven gloves, preheated metal tray for steam, stand back.

### You need

- Minimum: a batch of at least 5 pieces of one product (rolls of 60 g are ideal: 10 from 500 g of flour), your bake log with the pâton weights, a scale (1 g), a calculator, and one bought pack with a printed net weight. Copy the sheet below into your notebook.
- Professional equivalent: a certified bench scale at the control station, the bakery's weight-control sheet filed with the batch record, the divider's settings.

### Ingredients

None new. If you bake for this lesson: PD-01 or PC-02 on 500 g of flour (lesson [08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)), divided into rolls of 60 g ± 2 g.

### In Israel

Checked 2026-10-09.

- **A bought pack to check.** A bag of pitas (פיתות, *pitot*) or rolls (לחמניות, *lakhmaniyot*) with a printed net weight (משקל נטו, *mishkal neto*) is a good exercise: weigh each piece and the whole bag without the plastic, and compare with the label using the French table above as the training reference. You are practising the method; Israeli rules are not the subject.
- **Check your scale.** A home scale is not a trade scale. Tare a jug and pour in 500 mL of water measured with a measuring jug: it should read close to 500 g. Do it once a month and write it in your log.
- **Heat and loss.** In a hot, dry kitchen bread keeps losing weight on the rack: weigh at a fixed time after baking (1 hour) and write the room temperature.

### Steps

1. **Count** your pieces against what you planned; write the difference and why.
2. **Write the target:** pâton × (1 − your measured or planned loss), or the printed weight for the bought pack.
3. **Weigh** every piece after 1 hour of cooling (at least 5).
4. **Calculate** the average, the spread, the deviation of the average from the target in g and %, and the number of pieces outside ± 3 %.
5. **Calculate** the loss and the salt per 100 g of the lightest piece with your sheet's salt % of dough.
6. **Decide** with the decision table and write the decision. A printable version of the sheet is the [weight-control sheet](../../templates/weight-control-sheet.md).
7. **Write** a three-line report as if to a manager: facts, action, proposal.
8. **Do the bought pack** the same way (count, each piece, the total without packaging, average against the printed weight).
9. **Exercises:** answer the four below.

| Ordered / baked / set aside / available | |
| Pâton, baked target, tolerance | |
| Weights of the sample (g) | |
| Average, lightest, heaviest | |
| Pieces outside tolerance | |

**1.** Croissants: 60 g of dough, planned baked weight 52 g, tolerance ± 3 %. Sample: 52, 53, 50, 52, 54, 51, 52, 53 g. Average? Pieces outside tolerance? Decision?

**2.** A bakery packs six rolls in a bag labelled 300 g. Five bags weigh 302, 296, 305, 290, 301 g. Does the lot meet the prepackage rules? Which bag is defective?

**3.** A shop announces "baguette 250 g". Measured loss: 21 %. You want the average 3 g above the announced weight. Which pâton (rounded up to the next 5 g)?

**4.** A home tradition baguette (TR-01, salt 1.044 % of dough) from a 270 g pâton weighs 225 g after cooling. Salt per 100 g? Conforming to the pain courant limit used as good practice for tradition?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

**1.** Sum 417 g, average **52.1 g**: on target. ± 3 % of 52 g = 50.4-53.6 g: **two pieces out**, 50 g (light) and 54 g (heavy). The average is right, so the bake and pâton weight are fine; the spread points to uneven cutting (triangle sizes, lesson [11.5](../module-11/lesson-05.md)). Set the 50 g piece aside if it looks small; check the template and the sheet thickness.

**2.** Maximum tolerated shortfall for 300 g: 9 g (300 g sits in the 200-300 g band; the 300-500 g band gives 3 % = 9 g too), so under **291 g** is defective: the **290 g** bag. The average is 1,494 ÷ 5 = **298.8 g**, under 300 g: the lot fails the average rule as well. Fill heavier (aim at about 305 g) and re-check.

**3.** Baked target 253 g; pâton = 253 ÷ (1 − 0.21) = 253 ÷ 0.79 = 320.3 g → **325 g**. Dividing by 0.79, not multiplying by 1.21 (that would give 306 g, too light).

**4.** 270 × 1.044 % = 2.82 g of salt; 2.82 ÷ 225 × 100 = **1.25 g per 100 g**: under 1.4 g. Loss (270 − 225) ÷ 270 = 16.7 %.

</details>

### Targets

- Pieces counted against the plan; target written before weighing.
- At least 5 pieces weighed after cooling; average, spread, pieces outside ± 3 %, loss and salt calculated.
- One decision and a three-line report written.
- The bought pack checked the same way; four exercises answered and checked.

### How you know it worked

From your sheet alone, someone else could say whether your batch was on weight, whether the dividing was even, whether the salt was within the limit and what you decided. Your exercise answers match the reference answers, including dividing (not multiplying) by the yield in exercise 3.

### Self-check

- [ ] I counted before I weighed and noted the difference from the plan.
- [ ] I weighed a sample from different places, after cooling, against a written target.
- [ ] I calculated average, spread, loss and the salt of the lightest piece.
- [ ] I wrote a decision and a factual report.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average right, complaints about small pieces | Spread ignored | Weigh the whole batch; set light pieces aside | Record the lightest piece and pieces outside tolerance every time |
| All pieces light by 8-10 g | Pâton too light, or a longer, hotter bake | Check the divider setting and the bake log | Pâton = target ÷ (1 − measured loss), rounded up |
| Weights differ between morning and afternoon checks | Weighed at different times after baking | Re-weigh at the standard time | Weigh at a fixed time (for example 1 hour) |
| Bagged lot fails although no pack is very light | Average under the nominal quantity | Re-weigh; top up the lightest bags | Fill to a target a few grams above the nominal quantity |
| Order short discovered when the customer calls | Count not done or not passed on | Bake more; tell the customer | Count against the order before the shop opens; tell sales staff |
| Scale reading drifts | Scale not level, tare forgotten, out of verification | Level, tare, check with a known weight | Certified scale, carnet up to date, monthly check |

## Review

- Count against the order first; tell sales staff and the manager at once if it is short.
- A weight-control sheet: target, sample from different places after cooling, average, spread, pieces outside tolerance, loss, salt of the lightest piece, decision.
- France sets no legal weight per bread type, but a weight you announce must be delivered (tromperie sur la quantité): keep the average above it.
- Prepackages: average at least the nominal quantity, and only a few packs short by more than the tolerated shortfall (15 g at 500 g, 9 g at 200-300 g); scales for sale are certified and verified.
- Weights and quantities in the production test: [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md).

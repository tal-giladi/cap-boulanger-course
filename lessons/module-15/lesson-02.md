# 15.2 · Interior Quality: Crumb and Alveolation

The crumb is the record of everything that happened inside the dough: how it was mixed, how long it fermented, how it was shaped and how far it was baked. This lesson teaches you to cut a product the same way every time, to read its alveolation, colour and texture against what that product should show, and to record it so that two bakes can be compared. You cut and rate the crumb of your own bread.

## Why it matters

A customer who opens a baguette and finds a tunnel, or a pain de mie with a damp grey band, will not come back for the crust. The référentiel asks you to characterise a quality bread and identify its main defects with their causes (S3.1.6) and to check finished products so that defects are identified (C3.2). The crumb is also your best diagnostic tool: lessons [08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md), [09.4](../module-09/lesson-04.md) and [11.7](../module-11/lesson-07.md) used it product by product; this lesson gives one method for all of them. In INRAE's study of baguettes, the trained panel judged the crumb first, before the crust and the taste.

## Key terms

| French | Say it | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| mie | *MEE* | crumb: the inside of the bread |
| alvéolage | *al-vay-oh-LAHZH* | crumb structure: the size, number and spread of the holes |
| mie serrée | *mee seh-RAY* | tight crumb: many small holes close together, dense |
| mie ouverte / alvéolée | *mee oo-VAIRT / al-vay-oh-LAY* | open crumb: large, irregular holes |
| mie élastique | *mee ay-lah-STEEK* | springy crumb that comes back after pressing |
| mie collante / pâteuse | *mee koh-LAHNT / pah-TUHZ* | sticky, doughy crumb |
| tunnel | *tü-NEL* | long hole under the crust, usually from shaping |
| coupe transversale | *KOOP trahns-vair-SAL* | cross-section: a cut across the bread |

## How it works

### Cut the same way every time

1. **Cool fully first:** at least 1 hour for a baguette, 2-3 hours for a large loaf or pain de mie, 30-60 minutes for viennoiserie. The crumb keeps setting as it cools; cut warm, a well-baked bread looks gummy.
2. **Use a serrated knife** and saw gently; never press down, or you crush the cells you want to see.
3. **Cut in the same places:** across the middle (cross-section), and for long breads a lengthwise cut of about 10 cm from the middle. Viennoiserie: lengthwise through the middle, tip to tip (lesson [11.7](../module-11/lesson-07.md)).
4. **Look straight on, in good light,** then feel and smell.

### The five things a crumb shows

| Criterion | How to judge it | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Structure (alvéolage) | size, number, shape and spread of the holes; walls thin and shiny or thick and dull | hydration, fermentation, shaping, the process (tradition vs courant) |
| Colour | white, creamy, cream to ivory, grey, dark | oxidation at mixing (white = over-mixed, lesson [03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)), flour type, levain |
| Elasticity | press with a finger: the crumb springs back, or stays dented | gluten network and bake |
| Moisture | touch: moist and soft, or dry and crumbly, or damp and sticky | hydration, bake time, cooling, staling |
| Regularity | holes even across the whole section, or a dense zone, a tunnel, a band | shaping, proofing, oven heat |

### What each product should show

There is no single "good crumb": a tradition with a pain de mie's crumb is a fault, and the reverse too.

### Recording a crumb so you can compare it

Words like "quite open" drift from week to week. Three simple records make crumbs comparable:

- **Hole count in a window.** Cut a 4 × 4 cm square out of a card. Lay it on the cut face, in the middle of the section, and count the holes of 5 mm or more inside it, and note the largest. A tradition will show a few large holes and many small ones; a pain de mie almost none of 5 mm. The number only means something compared with your own bread of the same formula.
- **Press test.** Press the crumb about 1 cm with a fingertip and count: back within about 2 seconds (elastic), slowly, or stays dented.
- **Tracing.** Lay baking paper on the cut face and trace the outline and the biggest holes with a pencil; keep it in your log next to the bake's data. A photo does the same job if it is always taken from the same distance and light.

### From crumb fault to stage

| Crumb fault | Usually from | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Dense, tight crumb, small volume | under-fermented or under-proofed; dough too cold; weak or under-mixed dough | dough temperature below target; short apprêt; poke test notes ([04.7](../module-04/lesson-07.md)) |
| Large holes under the top crust, flat loaf, dense bottom | over-proofed; the structure collapsed | long apprêt; warm room; sour smell ([04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)) |
| Tunnel under the crust | air or flour trapped in a fold at shaping | shaping notes; flour on the bench ([08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)) |
| Grey, damp, gummy band at the base | under-baked; cut warm; too much water | core temperature below about 93 °C; time of cutting ([07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)) |
| White, cottony crumb, bland taste | over-oxidised: mixed too long and too fast | mixing time and speed ([03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)) |
| Dry, crumbly crumb | too little water, over-baked, or stale | hydration; bake time; day of baking ([07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)) |
| Uneven: dense on one side | uneven shaping, or uneven heat in the oven | position in the oven; shaping |

Correct the earliest stage first, change one thing at a time, and if the cause is equipment or a raw material, report it ([non-conformity report](../../templates/non-conformity-report.md)). The general table is in [Troubleshooting](../../references/troubleshooting.md).

## Worked example

Tuesday, 10:30. The head baker asks you to cut-test one piece of each product from the morning bake before the afternoon order is mixed.

| | Tradition (300 g) | Pain de mie (1,050 g pâton) | Campagne (590 g pâton) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooling before cut | 1 h 30 | 2 h 30 | 2 h |
| Holes ≥ 5 mm in 4 × 4 cm | 11, largest 14 mm | 0 | 6, largest 9 mm |
| Colour | cream, shiny walls | pale, even | slightly grey |
| Press test | back in 1-2 s | back in 1-2 s | back slowly |
| Regularity | even | tunnel 1 cm under the top along the whole loaf | grey, damp band of about 1 cm at the base |

1. **Tradition:** matches the expected crumb. Score 2 on both crumb lines of the rubric.
2. **Pain de mie:** fine and regular, as it should be, but with a tunnel along the top. The four balls in the tin (lesson [10.5](../module-10/lesson-05.md)) were each tight; the tunnel runs along the whole length at the same height. Evidence: the shaping note says the balls were "rolled in flour to stop sticking". Cause: flour trapped between the balls and the dough skin, which did not weld. Crumb structure 1, texture 2.
3. **Campagne:** the grey, damp band at the base and the slow press test point to under-baking. Evidence: the bake log shows the campagne came out of the lower deck after 28 minutes instead of about 40 because the deck was needed for the rolls; core temperature was not taken. Crumb structure 1, texture 0.
4. **Decisions:** the pain de mie is sold (the tunnel is a look defect, not a safety one). The under-baked campagne is withdrawn: the head baker decides. Report (C4.4): "Campagne du 14/10: mie grise et humide à la base sur la pièce testée, cuisson 28 min au lieu de 40 (sole occupée). Pièces retirées de la vente. Proposition: plan de cuisson revu, température à cœur contrôlée."
5. **Change for tomorrow:** pain de mie balls shaped on a lightly oiled bench, no flour; campagne baked its full time, core temperature checked at about 96-98 °C.

## Practice

You cut and rate the crumb of at least two different products: one of your own bakes, and one bought bread of a different type for comparison. If you have two bakes of the same formula (for example from the project of [module 8](../module-08/lesson-01.md) or [9](../module-09/lesson-01.md)), cut both and compare them with the same method.

> [!CAUTION]
> Cut with a serrated bread knife on a stable board, your fingers on top of the loaf and away from the blade's path. Saw gently; a hard crust can make the knife slip.

### You need

- Minimum: your cooled bread, a bought bread of another type, a serrated bread knife, a board, a ruler, a 4 × 4 cm window cut in card, baking paper and a pencil (or a phone camera on a fixed spot), the [quality rubric](../../templates/quality-rubric.md), your [bake log](../../templates/bake-log.md).
- Professional equivalent: the daily cut test of one piece per product and per batch, recorded on the batch record, with faults reported to the head baker.

### Ingredients

None new: you judge bread you baked or bought.

### In Israel

Checked 2026-10-09.

- **A good comparison set.** Buy two breads with very different crumbs: a sliced white or light bread from the supermarket (לחם לבן פרוס, *lekhem lavan parus*) for a fine, regular, soft crumb close to pain de mie, and a bakery country loaf or sourdough (לחם מחמצת, *lekhem makhmetset*) for an open, irregular crumb. Read the ingredients on the bag: industrial sliced bread often contains oil, sugar and emulsifiers that make the crumb softer and finer than a lean French dough could be.
- **Whiter crumb.** Israeli white flour (קמח לבן, *kemakh lavan*) may contain ascorbic acid; with long, fast kneading the crumb can come out whiter than a T55 baguette. Judge "creamy" against the rubric, not against memory ([Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md)).
- **Heat and staling.** In a hot kitchen bread dries and stales faster on the counter: cut-test the same day, at the same time after baking, and keep the comparison bread wrapped until you cut it.

### Steps

1. **Cool** each bread fully (at least 1 hour for baguettes, 2-3 hours for large loaves); write the time.
2. **Cut** each one across the middle with the serrated knife (and lengthwise for 10 cm on long breads).
3. **Look:** describe structure, colour and regularity in one line each.
4. **Count** the holes of 5 mm or more in the 4 × 4 cm window, in the middle of the section; note the largest.
5. **Press** the crumb about 1 cm: back in about 2 seconds, slowly, or dented?
6. **Touch:** moist, dry or sticky? Look at the base for a grey, damp band.
7. **Trace** the section on baking paper (or photograph it from a fixed spot) and file it with the bake's data.
8. **Compare** each crumb with the table of expected crumbs, and score the two crumb lines of the rubric (structure; colour and texture).
9. **Diagnose** any fault with the "from crumb fault to stage" table and find the evidence in your log; write one change for next time.

### Targets

- Every bread cut only after full cooling, in the same places.
- Hole count, largest hole and press test recorded for each bread.
- Two crumb lines of the rubric scored for each bread, with one sentence of justification.
- Any fault traced to a stage with a piece of evidence from your log.

### How you know it worked

Your notes let someone who has not seen the bread tell a tradition crumb from a pain de mie crumb, and tell which of your two bakes had the more open crumb, from the numbers alone. Your fault diagnosis names a stage and a record ("dough at 21 °C instead of 24 °C"), not a feeling.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crumb crushed and dense along the cut | Smooth knife pressed down | Cut another slice with a serrated knife, sawing | Serrated knife; let the blade do the work |
| Crumb looks gummy but the loaf sounded hollow | Cut before full cooling | Wait and cut again later | Cool 1-3 hours depending on size |
| Every bread judged "too dense" | Compared with an open tradition crumb | Compare with the product's own expected crumb | Use the product table |
| Hole counts vary wildly on one loaf | Window placed in different spots | Always in the centre of the cross-section | Same place, same size window |
| A fault "fixed" but back next bake | Several things changed; no evidence used | Go back to the log | One change at a time, recorded |
| Under-baked crumb sold | No cut test before sale | Withdraw the batch; tell the manager | Cut-test one piece per batch; core temperature |

## Review

- Cut only after full cooling, with a serrated knife, in the same places; look, then press, then touch.
- Judge structure, colour, elasticity, moisture and regularity against what that product should show: tradition open and cream, pain de mie fine and regular, croissant honeycomb.
- Record the crumb so it can be compared: holes of 5 mm or more in a 4 × 4 cm window, the press test, a tracing.
- Dense = under-fermented or cold; big holes under a flat top = over-proofed; tunnel = shaping; grey gummy base = under-baked or cut warm; white and bland = over-mixed.
- The quality of finished products in the production test: [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md).

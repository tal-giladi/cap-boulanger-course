# 08.3 · Shaping Baguettes

Shaping turns a relaxed, pre-shaped piece into a baguette: a tight, even cylinder with a sealed seam and pointed ends. This lesson explains what each movement does to the dough and how to reach the right length for a bakery oven and for yours. You shape six home baguettes of 270 g and set them to proof.

## Why it matters

The référentiel asks you to explain how weighing and shaping methods affect the dough and the bread (S3.1.3). Shaping is where a baguette gets its tension, and tension decides oven spring, the way the scores open and the regularity of the crumb. A slack baguette spreads and its cuts stay flat; an over-tight one tears on the side. Shaping by hand is also the skill that takes the most repetition: a professional shapes a baguette in well under a minute, every one alike. Your first ones will not be alike, which is why this lesson asks for six.

| clé | *KLAY* | the seam where the dough is sealed; it goes underneath in the oven |
| pointes | *PWANT* | the tapered ends of a baguette |
| tension / serrage | *tahn-SYOHN / seh-RAHZH* | the tightness of the outer skin given by shaping |
| couche | *KOOSH* | floured baker's linen in which baguettes proof between folds |
| planchette (à baguettes) | *plahn-SHET* | narrow transfer board used to roll a baguette out of the couche |
| façonneuse | *fah-soh-NUHZ* | moulder: machine that rolls and lengthens baguette pieces |

## How it works

### What shaping does

The pâton after détente is relaxed and full of gas. Shaping does three things to it:

1. **Removes the largest bubbles** (flattening), so the crumb is regular and no tunnel runs under the crust.
2. **Builds tension**: each fold stretches the outside of the dough over the inside, and sealing the seam locks it. The tight skin holds the gas during apprêt and guides oven spring upwards and through the scores.
3. **Gives length and an even thickness** (rolling out), so the baguette bakes evenly from end to end.

![Six panels: flatten the relaxed piece, fold the far third and seal, fold over the thumb and seal with the heel of the hand, roll outwards from the centre seam down, taper the ends, check length: about 38 cm for a 270 g home baguette and 55 cm for a 350 g bakery baguette](../../assets/m08-baguette-shaping.svg)

### The movements

1. **Flatten.** Piece top side down on a very lightly floured bench; press gently with the fingertips into a rectangle. Pop only the large bubbles: you want to keep most of the gas.
2. **First fold.** Fold the far third to the middle; press the edge down with the fingertips.
3. **Second fold and seal.** Fold the top over your thumb down to the near edge and seal along the whole length with the heel of the hand. Now you have a tight cylinder with the seam (clé) along its length.
4. **Roll out (allongement).** Seam down. Both hands, slightly cupped, start in the centre and roll back and forth while moving apart towards the ends. Do it in 2-3 passes, not one: the gluten needs a moment between passes. Use little or no flour on the bench: the dough needs a little grip to lengthen.
5. **Taper.** Finish each end with more pressure under the outer edge of the hands to form the points.
6. **Check.** Even thickness, straight seam, the right length. Do not re-roll a torn piece several times: it only tears more.

**How much tension?** Enough that the skin is smooth and taut but not torn. Weak or very wet dough needs a firmer seal; strong dough (common with Israeli white flour) needs gentler folds and a longer détente. The moulder (façonneuse) in a bakery does the rolling for you, but the hand-shaped baguette, with less pressure, keeps a more open crumb.

### Length: bakery and home

| | Bakery (PC-02) | Home |
|---|---|---|
| Pâton | 350 g | 270 g (lesson [06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md)) |
| Length after shaping | about 55 cm | about 38 cm, or your tray width minus 4-5 cm |
| Dough per cm | about 6.4 g/cm | about 7.1 g/cm |
| Oven | deck oven, loaded with a loader | home oven tray, usually 3 per tray |

The home baguette is shorter, but its thickness is close to the bakery's, so it bakes in a similar time. A baguette shorter and thicker than this bakes more like a bâtard; a longer, thinner one dries and over-colours in a home oven.

### Into the couche: apprêt begins

Each baguette goes into a well-floured couche, seam **up** (it is turned over onto the transfer board, so it is seam down in the oven), with a fold of linen standing up between pieces so they keep their shape and do not touch. The couche wicks a little moisture from the skin, which helps scoring. At home, baking paper on a tray with folded tea towels rolled between the pieces works: shape seam down, since you will not turn them over. Cover, and note the time: apprêt runs about 1 h 15 at 25 °C and 75-80 % relative humidity in a bakery proofing cabinet; lesson [08.4](lesson-04.md) decides when it is finished.

Shape **in the order you divided**: the first pieces are the most fermented, so they are shaped first and baked first.

## Worked example

Saturday 14 November, 4:20, PC-02 batch: 24 baguette pieces of 350 g, détente done (lesson [08.2](lesson-02.md)). The shop needs them at 7:00; the 20 rolls are shaped after them.

1. **Set up.** Couche floured on two boards (12 baguettes each), planchette, small flour dish, scraper. Bench dusted very lightly.
2. **Rhythm.** 24 baguettes in about 25 minutes: about one a minute, including setting it in the couche. You shape them in division order: the first piece cut at 3:42 is the first shaped at 4:20.
3. **Check every fifth piece** with the ruler on the bench: 54, 56, 55, 53, 57 cm. All within about ± 2 cm of 55 cm: even enough to bake together.
4. **One piece tears** at the second pass of rolling. The détente of the first pieces was fine, so this is over-handling: you finish it at 50 cm without forcing it and note it; it will be the shortest in the load and is checked at the weight control after baking.
5. **Apprêt.** Boards into the proofing cabinet at 4:45, 25 °C, 78 % RH. Planned end: about 6:00, oven ready at 6:00 (lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)). The rolls follow at 4:50, proofed slightly cooler so they are ready for the second load.
6. **Record.** Shaping 4:20-4:45, lengths 53-57 cm, one torn piece (over-rolled), apprêt start 4:45. If the baguettes come out uneven, these notes separate a shaping fault from a proofing fault.

## Practice

You shape six baguettes of 270 g from a 1 kg PD-01 batch, twice the 500 g batch you costed in lesson [06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md), and proof them in two staggered sets of three so each set fits your oven. Practise the movements first with a small piece of dough or play dough if you have it.

> [!WARNING]
> Baking is at 250 °C with steam. Dry oven gloves, steam only into a metal tray preheated in the oven (never water into a hot glass dish), pour and step back. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers and cover the lame after use.

### You need

- Minimum: as for lesson [08.2](lesson-02.md), plus a large bowl for 1 kg of flour, a ruler or tape measure, two trays with baking paper, 4 clean tea towels (or a linen couche), plastic sheet to cover, flour for dusting.
- Professional equivalent: floured couche on boards, planchette, moulder (façonneuse), proofing cabinet at 25 °C and 75-80 % RH.

### Ingredients

PD-01 on 1 kg of flour, direct (no pâte fermentée needed; with your pâte fermentée you can make PC-02 instead, 150 g of it on 1 kg):

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Flour T55 | 100 | 1,000 g |
| Water at the calculated temperature | 65 | 650 g |
| Fine salt | 1.8 | 18 g |
| Fresh yeast (or 5 g instant dry) | 1.5 | 15 g |
| **Total** | **168.3** | **1,683 g** |

Six pâtons of 270 g = 1,620 g; the 63 g left covers process losses.

### In Israel

- **Oven size.** Many Israeli home ovens are 60 cm wide models (built-in or freestanding), whose trays can be narrower than 45 cm. Measure your tray before you shape and shape to tray width minus 4-5 cm: on a 42 cm tray that is about 37-38 cm. If your tray is under 40 cm, divide 6 × 135 g and shape demi-baguettes of about 25 cm, or place the baguettes diagonally (checked 2026-10-08).
- **Flour.** White flour (קמח לבן, *kemakh lavan*) as the T55 ([Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md)). If the pieces fight back and tear, the flour is strong: give 10 more minutes of détente and shape with lighter folds rather than adding water at this stage.
- **Warm, humid kitchen.** The apprêt can be ready in 45 minutes at 28-30 °C. Shape the second set 20-25 minutes after the first, as planned below, and keep it in the coolest room you have so the oven is free when it is ready.
- **Couche.** A linen couche is sold by some baking-supply shops and online (search for "couche"); heavy, plain cotton tea towels well rubbed with flour do the same job at home (checked 2026-10-08).

### Steps

1. **Measure your tray** and write your target length (about 38 cm).
2. **Mix** PD-01 by hand to 24 °C ± 1 °C (water with three factors, lesson [05.2](../module-05/lesson-02.md)). Knead 12-14 minutes for this larger batch.
3. **Pointage** about 1 h 15 with one fold at 40 minutes, judged by the signs (lesson [08.2](lesson-02.md)).
4. **Divide** 6 × 270 g ± 2 g. **Pre-shape** loose cylinders of about 15 cm. Détente 20-30 minutes, covered.
5. **Shape set 1 (three pieces)** following the six movements. Measure each one. Place them on paper seam down with a rolled towel between pieces (or seam up in a floured couche). Cover. Write the time. Preheat the oven to 250 °C with the baking tray and the metal steam tray inside.
6. **Shape set 2** 20-25 minutes after set 1, in the same way, and proof it somewhere slightly cooler.
7. **Look at the six** side by side before they proof too much: lengths, thickness, straight seams, points. Write which one is best and why.
8. **Score and bake** each set when ready (lesson [08.4](lesson-04.md)); if you have not read it yet: three cuts along the length, 250 °C with steam, 20-25 minutes until deep golden, set 2 after set 1.
9. **Cool** on a rack, weigh each baguette after 1 hour, and fill in the [bake log](../../templates/bake-log.md).

### Targets

- Six baguettes within **± 2 cm** of your target length; thickness even by eye along the whole length.
- Seams straight and sealed (no opening during apprêt); no torn surface.
- Each piece shaped in under 2 minutes by the sixth one.
- Apprêt start time written for both sets.

### How you know it worked

After apprêt the baguettes are still round in section, not flat, and the seam has not opened. In the oven they rise upwards and open at the scores rather than tearing at the side. Cut across, the crumb is even from end to end with no tunnel under the top crust.

### Self-check

- [ ] I flattened only the large bubbles and kept most of the gas.
- [ ] I folded twice and sealed the whole seam with the heel of my hand.
- [ ] I rolled from the centre outwards in two or three passes, with little flour.
- [ ] I shaped in division order and measured every baguette.
- [ ] I can say which of my six is best and what I did differently with it.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baguette tears while rolling out | Détente too short; strong flour; rolled out in one pass | Stop, cover, rest 5 minutes, finish gently | Longer détente; 2-3 passes; lighter pre-shape |
| Baguette will not lengthen, slides on the bench | Too much flour on the bench | Brush the flour off; a lightly damp hand | Almost no flour for rolling out |
| Seam opens during apprêt or in the oven | Seam not sealed; flour in the seam | Pinch closed before proofing | Seal with the heel of the hand along the whole length |
| Thick middle, thin ends (or the reverse) | Hands kept in one place; uneven pressure | Re-roll the thick part gently once | Start in the centre and move the hands apart evenly |
| Flat, wide baguettes after apprêt | Too little tension; dough too soft; no support between pieces | Bake at once | Tighter folds and seal; couche folds or rolled towels between pieces |
| Large hole under the top crust | Gas pocket left at flattening; flour in the folds | — | Flatten evenly; brush off flour before folding |
| Baguettes too long for the tray | Length not checked against the oven | Curve them slightly or bake on the diagonal | Measure the tray; shape to tray width minus 4-5 cm |

## Review

- Shaping removes large bubbles, builds tension with folds and a sealed seam, and gives an even length.
- Six movements: flatten, fold the far third, fold and seal with the heel of the hand, roll out from the centre, taper, check.
- Bakery: 350 g to about 55 cm; home: 270 g to about 38 cm or your tray width minus 4-5 cm, with a similar thickness.
- Shape in division order, place in a floured couche (seam up) or on paper (seam down) with support between pieces, cover and note the apprêt start.
- Hand shaping is a production-test skill: see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md).

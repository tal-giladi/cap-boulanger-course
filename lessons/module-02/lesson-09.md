# 02.9 · Seeds, Other Flours, Malt and Improvers

Flour is rarely just ground wheat: millers and bakers correct it with small amounts of ascorbic acid, malt, bean flour, gluten or enzymes, and bakers enrich breads with seeds and other cereals. This lesson sorts these products by legal category and by what they do to the dough, shows why tradition bread forbids almost all of them, and ends with label reading and a seed soaker.

## Why it matters

A correcting product (*produit correcteur*) can rescue a weak or "lazy" flour, but it can also hide a poor process and make bread white and tasteless. Each one has a precise effect on elasticity, extensibility, fermentation, volume, crumb or crust, so you must know which one does what before you reach for it. And because pain de tradition française allows no additives, picking up the wrong bag is a legal non-conformity, not just a technical one. Seeds and other flours raise their own questions: water absorption, gluten dilution and allergens (sesame, mustard, lupin and nuts are regulated allergens).

## Key terms

| French | Say it | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| produit correcteur / améliorant | *pro-DWEE ko-rek-TUHR / ah-may-lyo-RAHN* | correcting product / bread improver (often a commercial blend) |
| additif | *ah-dee-TEEF* | additive: a substance with an E number, declared on the label |
| adjuvant | *ad-zhew-VAHN* | adjuvant: an ingredient added to correct flour (gluten, bean, soy or malt flour, deactivated yeast) |
| auxiliaire technologique | *ok-see-LYAIR tek-no-lo-ZHEEK* | processing aid: acts during manufacture, inactive in the finished bread (e.g. fungal amylase) |
| acide ascorbique (E300) | *ah-SEED as-kor-BEEK* | ascorbic acid (vitamin C), the oxidising additive used in bread flour |
| farine de fève / de soja / de malt | *fah-REEN duh FEV / duh so-ZHAH / duh MALT* | bean / soy / malt flour |
| levure désactivée | *luh-VOOR day-zak-tee-VAY* | deactivated (dead) yeast, a dough relaxer |
| graines / trempage | *GREN / trahm-PAHZH* | seeds / soaker (seeds soaked before mixing) |
| épeautre, sarrasin | *ay-POH-truh, sah-rah-ZAN* | spelt (a wheat), buckwheat (not a wheat, no gluten) |

## How it works

### The three legal categories in the référentiel

| Category | Product | What it does | Effect on the dough and bread |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Additive** | Ascorbic acid (E300) | oxidised during mixing into dehydroascorbic acid, which helps gluten proteins link | more tenacity and elasticity, better gas retention, more volume and tolerance; too much: tight dough, tears at shaping and scoring |
| **Adjuvants** | Wheat gluten | adds gluten-forming protein | stronger dough, more water absorption, more volume; used for wholemeal, seeded and frozen products |
| | Bean flour (farine de fève) | brings an enzyme (lipoxygenase) that oxidises flour fats and pigments | whiter crumb, a stronger dough; in excess: bland bread, very white crumb |
| | Soy flour (farine de soja) | same enzyme, more active; plus fat and protein | whiter crumb, softer; in excess: over-oxidation, loss of aroma |
| | Malt flour (wheat malt) | brings alpha-amylase from sprouted grain | more sugar for the yeast, better rise and crust colour; corrects flours with a high falling number |
| | Deactivated yeast | dead yeast cells release glutathione, which breaks some gluten bonds | relaxes the dough: more extensible, shorter mixing; for tough doughs and laminated dough |
| **Processing aid** | Fungal amylase (from *Aspergillus oryzae*) | breaks starch into sugars; inactivated earlier in the oven than cereal amylase | regular fermentation, colour and volume with less risk of a gummy crumb; not active in the finished bread |

An INAO specification for pain courant flour notes that the regulatory limit for ascorbic acid in French bread flour is 300 ppm (300 mg per kg of flour); good flours use far less (the Label Rouge flour caps it at 100 ppm). The same document lists other additives that ordinary bread may contain (for example lecithin E322, mono- and diglycerides E471, and lactic or acetic acids against "ropy" bread): up to 14 in pain courant. In practice an artisan bakery uses a flour already corrected at the mill, and adds little or nothing.

### Pain de tradition française: the exception

Décret 93-1074 (article 2) allows **no additive** in pain de tradition française, and only three adjuvants within strict limits, as a share of the total flour: **bean flour 2 %, soy flour 0.5 %, wheat malt flour 0.3 %**. So:

- no ascorbic acid, no gluten, no deactivated yeast, no emulsifiers in tradition;
- a flour labelled "farine de tradition française" is made to respect this;
- a commercial "améliorant" almost always contains E300 and is forbidden in tradition.

The full legal text and the other bread names (maison, levain, campagne) are taught in [lesson 09.1](../module-09/lesson-01.md).

### Seeds, grains and other flours

| Product | Effect on dough | Practical rule |
|---|---|---|
| Seeds (sunflower, flax/linseed, sesame, poppy, pumpkin) | dilute and cut the gluten → less volume; absorb water from the dough if added dry → dry, crumbly crumb that stales fast | usually 10–20 % of flour; **soak** first (or toast then soak) and count the soaker water |
| Flakes (oats, barley) | absorb a lot of water | soak or scald |
| Rye flour | no gluten network, sticky, high absorption (lesson [02.2](lesson-02.md)) | acidify (levain); blend with wheat |
| Spelt (épeautre) | a wheat with weaker, more extensible gluten | mix less, ferment gently |
| Buckwheat (sarrasin), maize | no gluten | blend with wheat; small shares only |

A **soaker** (*trempage*) is seeds + water (often equal weights, more for flax, which forms a gel) left for several hours or overnight in the fridge. Soaked seeds bring moisture into the bread instead of stealing it. Toasting seeds before soaking adds flavour. [Lesson 10.7](../module-10/lesson-07.md) bakes a seeded loaf with a soaker.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Sesame, mustard, lupin, nuts and all gluten cereals (wheat including spelt, rye, barley, oats) are among the 14 regulated allergens. A seeded dough can contaminate the next batch through the mixer, the bench and the trays: plan the order of work and cleaning ([lesson 17.6](../module-17/lesson-06.md)).

## Worked example

You receive three products. Can each be used for pain de tradition française?

| Label | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Farine de blé T65. Ingrédients : farine de blé, farine de fève (1,5 %), farine de blé malté" | **Yes** | bean flour under 2 %, malt flour (wheat) allowed up to 0.3 %; no additive |
| "Améliorant panification. Ingrédients : farine de blé, gluten de blé, dextrose, agent de traitement de la farine : acide ascorbique, enzymes" | **No** | ascorbic acid is an additive; gluten is not among the permitted extras |
| "Farine de blé T55. Ingrédients : farine de blé, gluten de blé, farine de blé malté, agent de traitement de la farine : E300" | **No** for tradition; **yes** for pain courant | E300 and gluten |

Now the seeds. A formula for 2 kg of flour includes 15 % seeds (300 g) and a soaker with 100 % water.

1. Seeds = 2,000 × 15 / 100 = 300 g; soaker water = 300 g.
2. The seeds absorb most of the soaker water; you keep the dough's own hydration (say 66 % = 1,320 g) and add the soaker at the end of mixing.
3. Total dough ≈ 2,000 + 1,320 + 300 + 300 + salt 36 + yeast 30 = 3,986 g.
4. Mix the dough to a good gluten first, then add the soaker at slow speed only until evenly distributed: seeds added early cut the developing gluten.

## Practice

> [!WARNING]
> If you bake the rolls: oven at 230 °C, oven gloves, steam tray preheated on the lowest shelf. If anyone eating them is allergic to sesame or other seeds, leave those seeds out.

### You need

- Minimum: scale (1 g), two small bowls with lids, a sieve, kitchen paper, your phone to photograph labels, [bake log](../../templates/bake-log.md). For the optional bake: the equipment of lesson 02.4.
- Professional equivalent: technical sheets of each improver and flour (fiches techniques) kept in the bakery's documentation.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed seeds (sunflower, flax, sesame, pumpkin) | 2 × 40 g | one portion for the soaker, one kept dry |
| Water | 40 g (+ 10 g if flax is more than a quarter) | for the soaker |
| Optional bake: T55 flour 2 × 200 g, water 2 × 130 g (65 %), salt 2 × 3.6 g (1.8 %), fresh yeast 2 × 3 g (1.5 %) | | dough A with dry seeds, dough B with the soaker |

### In Israel

- **Seeds:** sesame (שומשום, *shumshum*), flax (זרעי פשתן, *zar'ei pishtan*), sunflower (גרעיני חמנייה, *gar'inei khamaniya*) and pumpkin seeds (גרעיני דלעת, *gar'inei dla'at*). Buy them raw and unsalted, not the roasted, salted snack seeds (checked 2026-10-09).
- **Labels to photograph:** look for improver words on flour and bread labels, such as ascorbic acid (חומצה אסקורבית / E300) and enzymes (אנזימים, *enzimim*); the table in [Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md) lists them. Malt is לתת (*letet*).
- **Soaker in summer:** at 26-32 °C a soaker left out overnight can sour. Soak 1-2 hours on the worktop, or overnight in the fridge.
- **Optional bake:** white flour (קמח לבן, *kemakh lavan*) as the T55; water as in the lesson [01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md) estimate, with fridge water in summer.

### Steps

1. **Labels.** Photograph and copy the ingredient list of: (a) a supermarket flour, (b) a packaged bread or a bread improver (shops, online sheet), (c) a "tradition" baguette's allergen/ingredient information or a tradition flour bag. For each, list every additive (E number), adjuvant and processing aid you recognise, and decide: allowed in tradition, yes or no?
2. **Soaker.** Weigh 40 g of seeds and 40 g of water (50 g if flax-rich) in a bowl, cover, and leave at least 4 hours at room temperature or overnight in the fridge.
3. Pour the soaker into a sieve, let it drain 2 minutes, dab with paper, and weigh the seeds. Calculate how many grams of water 40 g of seeds absorbed.
4. **Optional bake.** Make two 200 g-flour doughs as in lesson [02.4](lesson-04.md) (65 % water). Fold 40 g dry seeds into dough A and the drained soaker into dough B after kneading. Ferment, shape into two 180 g rolls each, proof, bake 18–20 min at 230 °C with steam.
5. Taste both the same day and the next day.

### Targets

- Three labels fully copied and classified.
- Seed absorption measured to 1 g; most seed mixes absorb roughly half to all of their weight.

### How you know it worked

You can point to "E300" on a label and say why it strengthens the dough and why it is forbidden in tradition. Your soaked seeds weigh clearly more than 40 g. If you baked, the dry-seed rolls have a drier crumb that stales faster, while the soaker rolls stay moist.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dough very tight, tears when shaped and scored | Too much ascorbic acid or improver; strong flour | Longer détente; less tension when shaping | Dose by weight; check the flour is not already corrected |
| Very white, bland crumb | Over-oxidation: soy/bean flour plus long intensive mixing | — | Less intensive mixing; less or no bean/soy flour |
| Pale crust, slow fermentation | Flour short of amylase (high falling number) | Longer fermentation | Flour with malt flour or fungal amylase |
| Sticky dough, gummy crumb | Too much malt flour (cereal amylase) | Bake longer | Dose malt carefully (0.3 % max in tradition) |
| Seeded bread dry and crumbly the next day | Dry seeds took water from the crumb | — | Use a soaker and count its water |
| Tradition batch made with an improver | Wrong bag or habit | Sell it under another name (pain courant) and report | Separate storage; tradition flour and improvers labelled and apart |

## Review

- Additive: ascorbic acid E300 (strengthens, more volume). Adjuvants: gluten (strength), bean and soy flour (whiter crumb, oxidation), malt flour (sugars, colour), deactivated yeast (relaxes). Processing aid: fungal amylase (sugars, colour, inactive in the bread).
- Pain de tradition française: no additives; only bean flour ≤ 2 %, soy flour ≤ 0.5 %, wheat malt flour ≤ 0.3 % of the flour.
- Seeds and other flours dilute gluten and absorb water: soak seeds and count the water; watch the allergens.
- Exam-relevant (S2.4): see [the CAP exam reference](../../references/cap-exam.md).

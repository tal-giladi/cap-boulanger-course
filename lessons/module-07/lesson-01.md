# 07.1 · The 18 Stages from Order to Clean Bench

Lesson 01.2 showed the bakery's day as one flow; this lesson breaks it into the full set of 18 production stages, from reading the order to leaving a clean bench. For each stage you learn what it produces, why it exists and how it is checked, and you finish by writing the stage list for your next bake.

## Why it matters

A boulanger is not judged on one good loaf but on getting every product of an order right, on time, every day. That only works if no stage is skipped and each stage is checked before the next one starts: a forgotten salt check at weighing cannot be fixed in the oven. The référentiel lists the stages of bread-making from the organisation documents to cooling and staling (S3.1), and organising your own work (C1.2) is assessed in the production test through the sequence and the timing of your tasks (see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md)).

> [!NOTE]
> This is the first lesson of a practical module, so the usual reminder: watching someone bake does not make you able to bake. Do the practice at home. The Academy cannot grade photos, so you compare your result with the targets and the rubric yourself, and that self-assessment does not replace the official practical exam.

## How it works

### Five phases, 18 stages

The course counts 18 stages. Textbooks and bakeries group them differently (some write 10 or 12, merging pre-shaping into dividing or cooling into baking); the number matters less than never skipping one.

![The 18 production stages in five columns: Organise (order, technical sheet, organigramme, requisition, mise en place), Dough (weighing, mixing, pointage), Shape and proof (dividing, pre-shaping, détente, shaping, apprêt), Bake (scoring, loading with steam, baking), Finish (cooling and control, cleaning). A bar shows fermentation running without a pause from the end of mixing to the oven](../../assets/m07-production-stages.svg)

| # | Stage | What it produces | Why it exists | Check before moving on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Commande (order) | what, how many, what weight, by when | fixes the goal and the deadline | quantities and deadline read back |
| 2 | Fiche technique and calculations | weights for this batch | the bakery's standard, scaled ([Module 6](../module-06/lesson-01.md)) | dough total ≥ order + allowance |
| 3 | Organigramme | a timeline of every stage and machine | mixer, bench and oven never needed twice at once | works back from the deadline |
| 4 | Bon d'économat (requisition) | raw materials taken from store | stock and lot numbers known | use-by dates, quantities, temperatures |
| 5 | Mise en place | clean station, tools, containers | no searching once the dough is alive | hygiene, everything within reach |
| 6 | Pesée (weighing) | each ingredient weighed; water at its calculated temperature | exact formula; dough on target ([Module 5](../module-05/lesson-02.md)) | weights ticked off; salt and yeast apart |
| 7 | Pétrissage (frasage, kneading) | a developed dough | gluten network ([03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md)) | consistency, window test, dough temperature |
| 8 | Pointage | a fermented mass with strength and flavour | gas, acids, strength ([04.2](../module-04/lesson-02.md)) | volume, feel, time against temperature |
| 9 | Division | pieces of the right weight | every product the same size ([04.3](../module-04/lesson-03.md)) | weight within tolerance |
| 10 | Boulage / préfaçonnage | pre-shaped pieces | an even start for shaping | regular tension |
| 11 | Détente | relaxed pieces | gluten relaxes so it can be lengthened | piece stretches without pulling back |
| 12 | Façonnage | final shape with surface tension | form, volume and look of the bread (lesson 07.3) | length, regularity, seam closed |
| 13 | Apprêt | proofed pieces ready for the oven | gas to the right volume ([04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)) | poke test |
| 14 | Grignage | controlled cuts | the loaf opens where you choose (lesson 07.4) | cut angle, depth, number |
| 15 | Enfournement with buée | bread loaded into a steamed oven | oven spring, thin shiny crust (lesson 07.4) | oven temperature, steam, spacing |
| 16 | Cuisson and défournement | baked bread | crumb set, crust coloured (lesson 07.5) | colour, sound, core temperature |
| 17 | Ressuage, control, storage | cooled bread, weighed, presented or packed | steam leaves; product checked before sale | weight, look, quantity; storage conditions |
| 18 | Nettoyage | clean, tidy station | hygiene, safety, next shift | cleaning plan done, tools away |

### Paper first, dough second

Stages 1-5 happen before the first gram is weighed. They cost nothing to change: a mistake caught on paper is a crossed-out line. From stage 7 onwards the dough is alive and every mistake costs time or product.

### The clock starts at mixing

Fermentation does not stop between stages 7 and 16: the yeast keeps working while you divide, rest, shape and score, until heat in the oven kills it at about 46-50 °C in the dough ([04.1](../module-04/lesson-01.md)). That is why:

- **You cannot pause the dough for a missing tool.** Mise en place (stage 5) exists so that nothing is searched for during stages 7-15.
- **Dividing and shaping go fast and in order:** the first piece divided is the first shaped and the first loaded, so the whole batch stays at the same stage.
- **The only way to slow the clock is temperature.** Fermentation changes by about 7 % per °C (lesson [04.2](../module-04/lesson-02.md)): a cooler place or the retarder can buy time; waiting on the bench cannot.
- **The oven must be ready before the dough is.** Preheating is planned backwards from the end of apprêt, not started when the bread looks ready.

### After the oven: the most sensitive stages

Baking kills most microorganisms in the crumb, so the cooled bread is the most sensitive product in the bakery: it will not be heated again ([01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md)). Bread also keeps changing after the oven: steam leaves, the crumb sets as it cools, then it slowly stales (lesson 07.5). Stage 17 is therefore a real stage, not "waiting": racks, not bags; weighing and visual control before the shop; dated, covered storage for anything not sold today.

### Cleaning is stage 18 and happens all day

Cleaning is the last stage on paper but it runs in the gaps: bowls and tools washed during pointage, the bench scraped and wiped during apprêt, the floor done at the end. Flour dust is the first cause of occupational asthma in France and poorly cleaned floors and surfaces add to the exposure ([INRS](https://www.inrs.fr/metiers/commerce-service/boulangerie-patisserie/boulangerie-patisserie-risques.html)): scrape and wipe or vacuum, never blow flour off a bench with an air line or brush it into the air.

## Worked example

The order of 14 November from lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md): 24 baguettes (350 g) for the shop at 7:00 and 20 rolls (60 g) for a 7:30 delivery, PC-02, one two-deck oven. You write the stage list with times and checks, working back from 7:00.

| # | Stage | Time | Check written on the list |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | Order, sheet, organigramme | Friday 16:00 | 9,600 g needed, batch 9,844 g; start weighing 2:30 |
| 4 | Requisition | Friday 16:15 | T55 5.4 kg (lot number), salt 97 g, yeast 81 g (use-by date), pâte fermentée 810 g in the fridge |
| 5 | Mise en place | 2:15 | hands washed, hair covered, bench clean, scale tared, tubs, couches, racks ready |
| 6 | Weighing and water | 2:30 | water temperature calculated from today's readings |
| 7 | Mixing | 2:45 | dough 23-25 °C, smooth, window test |
| 8 | Pointage | 2:55-3:40 | 45 min at 24 °C; volume and feel |
| 9-10 | Dividing, pre-shaping | 3:40-4:00 | baguettes ±5 g, rolls ±2 g |
| 11 | Détente | 4:00-4:20 | covered; pieces stretch easily |
| 12 | Shaping | 4:20-4:45 | baguettes 55 cm, seam closed |
| 13 | Apprêt | 4:45-6:00 | 25 °C, 75-80 % RH; poke test |
| — | Oven preheat | 5:00 | 250 °C by 6:00, checked with the oven thermometer |
| 14-15 | Scoring, loading | 6:00 | 5 cuts per baguette; steam at loading |
| 16 | Bake, unload | 6:00-6:25 | colour, sound; rolls in at 6:25 |
| 17 | Cooling, control | 6:25-7:00 | racks; weigh 5 baguettes; count 24 and 20 |
| 18 | Cleaning | during pointage and apprêt; 7:30 | mixer bowl and tools at 3:00; bench at 5:00; floor after the delivery |

**What happens when a stage is skipped.** On the next Saturday a new apprentice follows the same list but bags the 20 rolls for the restaurant at 6:50, 7 minutes out of the oven, to "save time". At 7:30 the rolls arrive soft-crusted and damp, with condensation inside the bag. The ressuage (stage 17) was not waiting time: it is when the steam leaves the bread. The non-conformity is reported, the rolls are replaced from the next batch, and the stage list now says "rolls bagged no earlier than 7:15".

## Practice

You write the complete stage list for your next bake, then use it during the bake and tick each check. The example bake is PD-01 as eight flat rolls (lesson [01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)); you can use any bread from the earlier modules.

### You need

- The PD-01 technical sheet (lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)), the [production sheet template](../../templates/production-sheet.md), the [organigramme template](../../templates/organigramme.md), a pencil, a timer.
- For the bake: the equipment of lesson 01.7, a probe thermometer and an oven thermometer.
- Professional equivalent: the bakery's fiche de fabrication for each product, pinned at the station.

> [!WARNING]
> If you bake, the oven is at 230 °C with steam. Use dry oven gloves; steam only into a preheated metal tray (never a glass dish), pour and stand back.

### Steps

1. **Write the 18 stages** in a column, from memory first, then compare with the table above and correct.
2. **Fill in each line for your bake:** the time (start and end), the target (weight, temperature, length, time) and the check (how you will know). Work backwards from the time you want to eat or serve the bread.
3. **Mark three things on the list:** the moment the clock starts (end of mixing), when the oven goes on (your real preheat time from lesson [05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)) and two cleaning slots inside the waiting times.
4. **Bake from your list** (optional, recommended). Keep it beside you, tick each check, and write the real time next to the planned one.
5. **Review.** Under the list write the two stages where plan and reality differed most, and why.

### Targets

- All 18 stages in the right order, each with a time, a target and a check.
- A start time worked back from your deadline, including preheating.
- At least two cleaning tasks placed inside pointage, détente or apprêt.
- If you baked: real times within 15 minutes of the plan at each stage, or the reason written.

### How you know it worked

Someone else could bake from your list without asking you anything: each line says what to do, by when, and how to check it. During the bake you never stopped to look for a tool, the oven was hot before the rolls were ready, and the bench was clean when the bread came out.

### In Israel

- **Summer times shift.** In a 26-32 °C kitchen without air conditioning (מזגן, *mazgan*), pointage and apprêt run well ahead of the sheet's times (about 7 % faster per °C above the target): write a "check from" time on the list, not a fixed end time, and plan to proof in the air-conditioned room.
- **Plan the oven around the heat.** A home oven warms the kitchen by several degrees; in summer switch it on only as late as your preheat time allows and keep the proofing bread out of that room.
- **Flour.** Which bag to use for PD-01: [Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md) (white flour, קמח לבן).

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dough ready, oven still cold | Preheat planned forward from the dough, not backwards from the apprêt | Move the pieces somewhere cooler; bake when the oven is truly hot | Preheat time written on the stage list from the oven's real heating time |
| Tool or container searched for during dividing | No mise en place | Cover the dough, fetch it, keep the order of the pieces | Stage 5 checked off before mixing |
| Last pieces over-proofed, first ones right | Dividing and shaping slow or out of order | Bake the most advanced first | Work in order; first divided, first shaped, first loaded |
| Soft crust, damp bread, condensation in the bag | Ressuage skipped | Unbag, dry on a rack | "Bag no earlier than…" written on the list |
| Flour dust in the air at the end of the day | Bench brushed or blown clean | Stop, let it settle, wipe | Scrape and wipe or vacuum; clean in the gaps |

## Review

- 18 stages in five phases: organise (1-5), dough (6-8), shape and proof (9-13), bake (14-16), finish (17-18).
- Each stage produces something and has a check; act on a failed check before the next stage.
- From the end of mixing to the oven, fermentation never pauses: only temperature changes its speed.
- Ressuage and cleaning are real stages: the cooled bread is the most sensitive product in the bakery.
- Exam-relevant (S3.1 stages, C1.2 organisation): see [the CAP exam reference](../../references/cap-exam.md).

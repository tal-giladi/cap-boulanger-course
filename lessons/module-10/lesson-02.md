# 10.2 · Épi and Petits Pains

The épi is a baguette cut with scissors into an ear of wheat, and petits pains are the small rolls a bakery sells by the dozen to restaurants and families. Both come from the baguette dough you already make; what is new is a scissor cut that must be quick and regular, and small pieces that must all look alike. You make one épi and nine rolls in three shapes from one home batch.

## Why it matters

The épi is the most recognisable decorated baguette in a French bakery, and rolls are a large share of the trade with restaurants. Both are part of what the production test asks from the pain courant or tradition dough (the list and the marking are in [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md)). Both are also unforgiving: a hesitant scissor cut drags and tears the dough, and on a tray of rolls a single one that is larger, flatter or paler than the others is the first thing anyone sees. Small pieces also behave differently from baguettes: they proof faster, bake faster, lose more weight and so carry more salt per 100 g.

## How it works

### The épi: a baguette that is cut, not scored

An épi is shaped exactly like a baguette (lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)): same pâton, same tension, same length. Instead of a lame it gets scissors.

![Left: side view of a proofed baguette with scissors held low, about 30-45 degrees, cutting three quarters through with the bottom quarter attached. Right: top view of five leaves laid alternately left and right every 6-8 cm. Below: five 60 g roll shapes: round with a cross, long with one cut, small fendu, small tabatière, small épi with three leaves](../../assets/m10-epi-and-rolls.svg)

1. **Dust the top** lightly with flour and lay the baguette on the loader or the baking paper, seam down.
2. **Hold the scissors low**, about 30-45° to the dough, opened wider than the baguette, starting 6-8 cm from one end.
3. **One swift, clean cut** about **three quarters** of the way through. The bottom quarter stays attached: it is the "stalk" that holds the épi together. A cut right through gives separate pieces; a cut only half through gives leaves that barely move and close up in the oven.
4. **Lay the cut piece to one side** at once, turning it a little so its cut face opens upwards.
5. **Repeat every 6-8 cm**, laying each piece on the **opposite side** to the last. A 38 cm home baguette gives about 5 leaves; a 55 cm bakery baguette 6-7.

**When to cut.** In many bakeries the épi is cut on the loader at the end of apprêt, like a score, just before it goes into the oven: the leaves stay sharp and open. King Arthur Baking's recipe cuts before the final proof instead; the leaves then proof into rounder, softer shapes. Both work. Cut at the end of apprêt, the épi must be loaded at once, like a scored baguette; cut before, the dough must not be over-proofed, or the thin stalk tears when you move it. Deeper cuts look finer but are more fragile.

**Baking.** As a baguette: steam at loading, about 20-25 minutes at 240-250 °C (lesson [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)). The thin leaves colour faster than a baguette's body: take the épi out when the stalk is deep golden, before the leaf tips burn.

> [!CAUTION]
> Kitchen scissors used on dough are sharp and close to your other hand. Hold the baguette steady with the other hand well behind the line of the cut, cut away from your fingers, and dip or oil the blades so they do not drag. Clean and dry the scissors after use and store them closed.

### Petits pains: many small pieces, all alike

**Dividing.** 60 g is a usual roll weight in this course (PC-02 rolls, lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)). At ± 2 g a roll is within ± 3 %; at ± 5 g it is ± 8 %, and the difference shows on the tray. In a bakery a hydraulic divider (diviseuse) cuts one press of dough into equal pieces, for example 1,200 g into 20 pieces of 60 g; at home the scale does it, piece by piece.

**Rounding two at a time.** Each hand cups one piece on an unfloured bench and moves in small circles: the bench grips the dough and the skin tightens (the same drag as a boule, lesson [10.1](lesson-01.md)). A tight round is the start of every roll shape, so the rolls of one tray proof evenly.

**Shapes** (see the diagram). After a short détente of 10-15 minutes:

| Shape | How | Cut |
|---|---|---|
| Rond | re-round tightly | one cut or a cross |
| Long | flatten, fold in three, roll to 12-14 cm with pointed or blunt ends | one long cut, blade low |
| Fendu | rond or long, a groove pressed with a thin rod (a wooden spoon handle), floured; proof groove down, turn up | none |
| Tabatière | tight rond, a third rolled to a 2-3 mm flap, edge oiled, folded over; proof flap down, turn up | none |
| Petit épi | a short roll of 15-18 cm cut into 3 leaves | scissors |

### Why rolls behave differently

- **They proof faster.** A small piece warms through quickly and has a short distance for gas to hold: at the same dough temperature a tray of rolls is often ready 15-20 minutes before the baguettes shaped with it. Shape them last, or proof them a little cooler (lesson 08.3's worked example).
- **They bake faster:** about 14-16 minutes at 240-250 °C with steam, and they are done when evenly golden, bottoms hollow.
- **They lose more weight.** More crust per gram means more water evaporates: about 22-24 % for a 60 g roll against about 20 % for a baguette (lesson 08.5). Salt does not evaporate, so **salt per 100 g rises**: a PC-02 roll at 1.076 % salt in the dough reaches 1.4 g per 100 g at about 23 % loss. Over-baking a roll pushes it over the limit for pain courant.

## Worked example

Saturday, 6:00. A restaurant orders **36 rolls of 60 g in three shapes** (12 ronds, 12 longs, 12 fendus) for 7:30, and the shop wants **4 épis of 350 g**. All PC-02 (total 182.3 %), process losses 2 %, flour rounded up to the next 100 g.

1. **Dough.** Rolls 36 × 60 = 2,160 g; épis 4 × 350 = 1,400 g. Total **3,560 g**; with 2 %: 3,631.2 g.
2. **Flour.** 3,631.2 ÷ 1.823 = 1,992 g → **2,000 g**. Water 1,280 g, salt 36 g, fresh yeast 30 g, pâte fermentée 300 g; total 3,646 g, which covers 3,631.2 g.
3. **Divide.** The 4 épi pieces first (they are shaped first), then 36 rolls by hand at 60 g ± 2 g; checked: every tenth piece re-weighed.
4. **Shape.** Épis as baguettes, in the couche seam up. Rolls rounded two at a time, détente 10 minutes, then 12 of each shape on two trays per shape, spaced about 4 cm so they do not touch when they rise.
5. **Apprêt.** The rolls go in the proofing cabinet 2 °C cooler than the épis, so both are ready together.
6. **Loading.** Épis rolled onto the loader seam down, cut with scissors (6 leaves each), deck at 250 °C, steam, about 22 minutes. Roll trays in the deck oven above with steam, about 15 minutes; the ronds and longs are scored on the tray, the fendus turned groove up as they are set on the tray.
7. **Control.** Ten rolls weighed after cooling: average 46.4 g, so a loss of (60 − 46.4) ÷ 60 = 22.7 %. Salt: 60 × 1.076 % = 0.646 g in 46.4 g = **1.39 g per 100 g**: conforms, just. The tray from the back left corner averaged 45.6 g (24 % loss, 1.42 g): over the limit. The note for the chef: "plaque arrière gauche trop cuite: 1 minute de moins ou tourner la plaque".

## Practice

From one home batch of pain courant dough you shape one épi of 270 g and nine rolls of 60 g in three shapes (three of each), and bake the rolls and the épi in two loads.

> [!WARNING]
> The oven is at 240-250 °C with steam. Use dry oven gloves and long sleeves; steam only into a metal tray preheated on the lowest shelf (never water into a hot glass or ceramic dish), pour 100-150 mL of hot water, close the door and step back. Cut the épi with the scissors pointing away from your other hand, and score rolls with the lame moving away from your fingers; cover the lame and close the scissors after use.

### You need

- Minimum: as for lesson [10.1](lesson-01.md), plus sharp kitchen scissors, two baking trays with baking paper, a thin rod (wooden spoon handle) for fendus, a small dish of flour, a ruler.
- Professional equivalent: hydraulic divider (diviseuse), couche on boards, oven loader for épis, perforated trays for rolls, deck oven with steam.

### Ingredients

PC-02 on 500 g of flour (lesson [08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)), or PD-01 direct (water 65 %, no pâte fermentée):

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Home batch | Professional batch (worked example) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour T55 | 100 | 500 g | 2,000 g |
| Water at the calculated temperature | 64 | 320 g | 1,280 g |
| Fine salt | 1.8 | 9 g | 36 g |
| Fresh yeast (or 2.5 g instant dry) | 1.5 | 7.5 g | 30 g |
| Pâte fermentée | 15 | 75 g | 300 g |
| **Total** | **182.3** | **911.5 g** | **3,646 g** |

Divide 1 × 270 g (épi) + 9 × 60 g (rolls) = 810 g. Keep 75 g as your next pâte fermentée.

### In Israel

- **Flour and water:** white flour (קמח לבן, *kemakh lavan*) as the T55 ([Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md)); water with four factors and a hand friction factor of 8; in a 30 °C kitchen blend tap and fridge water (lesson [05.2](../module-05/lesson-02.md)).
- **Rolls proof very fast in summer:** at 28-30 °C a tray of 60 g rolls can be ready in 30-35 minutes. Shape the rolls first only if the oven is already hot; otherwise shape the épi first and proof the rolls in the coolest place you have.
- **Two trays, one oven:** most home ovens bake best one tray at a time. Rolls first (ready first, 14-16 minutes), then the épi; reheat the steam tray between loads by leaving it in the oven.
- **Scissors:** ordinary sharp kitchen scissors work; long blades make the cut in one stroke (checked 2026-10-08).

### Steps

1. **Mix, ferment, keep pâte fermentée** as in lesson 10.1, steps 1-2 (dough 24 °C ± 1 °C; pointage about 45-60 minutes with one fold).
2. **Divide** 270 g for the épi and 9 × 60 g ± 2 g. Pre-shape the 270 g piece as a loose cylinder; round the rolls two at a time. Cover; détente 20 minutes for the épi piece, 10-15 minutes for the rolls.
3. **Rolls:** shape three ronds, three longs (12-14 cm) and three fendus (or tabatières). Set them 4 cm apart on paper on a tray; fendus and tabatières groove or flap down on a floured towel. Cover. Write the time.
4. **Épi piece:** shape a baguette of about 38 cm (lesson 08.3) and lay it seam down on baking paper. Cover. Preheat the oven to 250 °C with a baking tray on the middle shelf and the metal steam tray on the lowest, at least 45 minutes.
5. **Bake the rolls** when the poke test says ready (from about 35 minutes): turn the fendus groove up onto the paper, one cut or a cross on the ronds, one long cut on the longs. Slide onto the hot tray, 100-150 mL of hot water into the steam tray, close, step back. Vent at 10 minutes. About 14-16 minutes in total, until evenly golden.
6. **Cut and bake the épi** at the end of its apprêt: dust with flour, five cuts with the scissors about every 7 cm, three quarters through, leaves alternately left and right. Load at once with steam as above; vent at 10 minutes; about 20-22 minutes, until the stalk is deep golden.
7. **Cool** on a rack. After 1 hour weigh every roll and the épi. Calculate the average roll weight, the loss and the salt per 100 g of the lightest roll (60 × 1.076 % ÷ its weight × 100; for PD-01 use 1.070 %).

### Targets

- Rolls 60 g ± 2 g before baking; within each shape, the same size and outline by eye.
- Épi: 5 leaves, evenly spaced, alternating, still joined by the stalk; no leaf torn off.
- Rolls baked 14-16 minutes to an even golden colour; salt per 100 g of the lightest roll at or below 1.4 g.

### How you know it worked

The épi lies flat with leaves of even size pointing alternately left and right, each leaf with a crisp edge, and it breaks apart into pieces by hand. On the roll tray you could not tell one rond from another; the longs are open along their single cut; the fendus have opened along their groove. The lightest roll has lost no more than about 23-24 % of its weight.

### Self-check

- [ ] I cut the épi with one quick stroke per leaf, about three quarters through, alternating sides.
- [ ] My nine rolls were within ± 2 g, and I rounded them with tension before shaping.
- [ ] I proofed and baked the rolls first because they were ready first.
- [ ] I weighed the rolls after cooling and checked salt per 100 g of the lightest one.
- [ ] I handled scissors and lame with the blade moving away from my other hand.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Épi leaves fell off | Cut right through; stalk too thin; épi moved after cutting | Bake the pieces as they lie | Stop at three quarters; cut on the paper or loader it bakes on |
| Leaves closed up, épi looks like a bumpy baguette | Cuts too shallow (half through); scissors held upright | — | Low angle, three quarters through; lay each leaf well to the side |
| Ragged, torn leaf edges | Scissors dull or sticky; hesitant cut | Flour or oil the blades | One swift stroke; sharp, clean scissors |
| Leaf tips burnt, stalk pale | Leaves very thin and long; oven too hot at the top | Move down a shelf; cover the tips with foil for the last minutes | Cuts every 6-8 cm, not further apart; bake 240 °C if your top heat is strong |
| Rolls of one shape uneven in size | Pieces outside ± 2 g; uneven rounding | — | Weigh every piece; same rounding movement for all |
| Rolls flat and spread | Over-proofed while the épi or the oven was waited for | Bake at once | Proof rolls cooler or shape them last |
| Rolls dry, salt above 1.4 g per 100 g | Over-baked; tray in a hot corner; loss above 23 % | — | 14-16 minutes; turn the tray; weigh after cooling |

## Review

- An épi is a baguette cut with scissors held low, about three quarters through, every 6-8 cm, leaves laid alternately left and right; the uncut quarter is the stalk.
- Cut at the end of apprêt and load at once (sharp leaves), or before apprêt (rounder leaves); either way, one swift cut per leaf.
- Rolls: divide to ± 2 g, round two at a time with the bench gripping the dough, shape after a short détente; within each shape all rolls must look alike.
- Rolls proof and bake faster and lose more weight than baguettes, so their salt per 100 g is higher: a PC-02 roll reaches 1.4 g at about 23 % loss.
- The épi and rolls in several shapes come from the production-test dough: see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md).

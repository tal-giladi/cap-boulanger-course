# 10.1 · Shaped Pieces: Boule, Bâtard, Fendu, Tabatière, Couronne

The same dough that makes baguettes also makes the round, oval, split, lidded and ring-shaped loaves on a bakery shelf. This lesson explains the five classic pièces façonnées, what each shaping movement does, and how each shape proofs, is scored (or not) and is baked. You shape two different 400 g pieces from one home batch of pain courant dough.

## Why it matters

A bakery sells more than baguettes from its pain courant or tradition dough: boules, bâtards, fendus, tabatières and couronnes give the shelf variety at no extra cost in ingredients. The référentiel's production competency (C2.3) covers these doughs from mixing to baking, and shaped pieces are among what the production test asks you to make from them; exactly what and how it is marked is in [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md). Each shape is a small technical problem: a boule needs tension in every direction, a fendu must open along its groove and not elsewhere, a tabatière's lid must lift and not glue itself back, a couronne's hole must stay open. Getting them regular, one like the other, is what makes a shelf look professional.

> [!NOTE]
> This is the first lesson of a practical module, so the usual reminder: watching someone shape bread does not make you able to shape it. Do the practice with your hands. The Academy cannot grade photos, so you compare your loaves with the targets and the [quality rubric](../../templates/quality-rubric.md) yourself, and that self-assessment does not replace the official practical exam.

## Key terms

| French | Say it | English meaning |

| pièce façonnée | *pyess fah-soh-NAY* | shaped piece: a loaf shaped by hand from the day's dough, other than a baguette |

| tabatière | *tah-bah-TYAIR* | "snuff box": a round loaf with a thin flap of dough folded over it like a lid |
| couronne | *koo-RONN* | crown: a ring-shaped loaf |
| boulage | *boo-LAHZH* | rounding: shaping a piece into a tight ball |
| rouleau (à pâtisserie) | *roo-LOH* | rolling pin, used here to press the fendu's groove and roll the tabatière's flap |

## How it works

### One dough, two families of shapes

Every shape starts from one of two pre-shapes (lesson [08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)), so decide at dividing which pieces become what:

- **Round family** (pre-shape: a loose ball): boule, tabatière, couronne.
- **Long family** (pre-shape: a loose cylinder): bâtard, fendu (a fendu can also be made from a boule).

After a détente of 20-30 minutes (longer if the pre-shape was tight), the pieces are shaped. The physics is the same as for the baguette (lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)): remove the largest bubbles, stretch the outer skin over the inside and lock it with a sealed seam. That skin holds the gas in apprêt and steers the oven spring. What changes is the geometry, and with it, where the bread is allowed to open.

### The five shapes

**Boule.** Flatten the relaxed ball lightly, fold the edges into the centre like an envelope, turn it seam down, then cup both hands around it and drag it towards you across an unfloured patch of bench, turning it a quarter each time. The bench must grip the dough slightly: that drag is what tightens the skin (King Arthur Baking). Too much flour and it slides without tightening; too much force and the skin tears. Scored with a cross, a square or a "chequerboard": the cuts must be symmetrical so the boule opens evenly in every direction.

**Bâtard.** Flatten the relaxed cylinder, fold the two long sides to the middle to make a rectangle, then roll it up from the far edge, pressing in with the thumbs at each turn, and seal the seam with the heel of the hand. Roll it gently to an even oval: a 400 g bâtard is about 25 cm long. One long cut along the middle (blade low, like a baguette cut, lesson [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)), or three oblique cuts.

**Fendu.** Shape a bâtard (or a boule) with a little less tension, dust the top with flour, then press a rolling pin (or a thin wooden rod) along the middle down almost to the bench, rolling it slightly to widen the groove to about a third of the width. Bring the two halves back together, groove closed, and set the piece **groove down** on a floured couche. At loading turn it over, groove up. The flour in the groove keeps the two lips from fusing, so the loaf opens along the groove in the oven: that is its "cut". It is not scored.

**Tabatière.** Shape a tight boule. Flour one side, and with the rolling pin roll about a third of it into a flap 3-4 mm thick, still attached to the ball. Brush the edge of the flap with a very little oil (or dust it with flour) so it does not stick back, and fold it over the top of the ball like a lid. Proof **flap down** on a floured couche; turn it over at loading so the flap is on top. In the oven the body rises and pushes the lid open like a snuff box. A flap thicker than about 5 mm does not lift; one thinner than about 2 mm tears or burns. It is not scored.

**Couronne.** From a relaxed ball: push your fingers (or elbow, for a large piece) through the centre and widen the hole by rotating the ring on the bench between both hands until it is about one third of the total width. It looks too big; it closes in apprêt and in the oven. A second way is a long cylinder joined end to end, the joint overlapped and pressed well so it does not open. Proof on a floured couche or paper, seam down. Score with 4-6 short cuts around the top (or snip with scissors). Regional crowns exist, such as the couronne bordelaise (balls of dough around a central disc); they follow the same rules of tension and spacing.

### Proofing, loading and baking the shapes

| Shape | Proofs | At loading | Cut | Bake (400-500 g, 240 °C, steam) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boule | seam up in a floured banneton, or seam down on a couche | turned seam down | cross or square | about 30-35 min |
| Bâtard | seam up in a couche | rolled seam down | 1 long or 3 oblique | about 28-32 min |
| Fendu | groove down | turned groove up | none | about 30 min |
| Tabatière | flap down | turned flap up | none | about 30 min |
| Couronne | seam down on couche or paper | slid onto the loader as it is | short cuts around | about 30-35 min |

A 400 g piece is thicker than a baguette, so it bakes longer at the same temperature (the core must reach at least 93 °C, lesson 08.4) and loses a smaller share of its weight: about 18 % in this lesson's planning, against about 20-21 % for baguettes. In a bakery the shaped pieces usually go on their own deck, so a 30-minute bake does not hold up baguettes that need 22 minutes. Steam at loading is the same as for baguettes, and so is the poke test for the end of apprêt (lesson [04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)); larger pieces take slightly longer to proof than baguettes shaped at the same time.

### Regular means alike

A shelf of twelve fendus is judged as a batch: same weight (pâtons divided to the gram, lesson [06.3](../module-06/lesson-03.md)), same length, same depth of groove, same colour. Shape them one after another with the same movements and the same number of strokes, and check every third piece against the first.

## Worked example

Friday, Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle. Besides the baguettes, the shop wants for Saturday morning: **6 fendus of 400 g, 6 tabatières of 400 g and 4 couronnes of 500 g**, all PC-02 (T55 100, water 64, salt 1.8, fresh yeast 1.5, pâte fermentée 15; total 182.3 %), process losses 2 %, flour rounded up to the next 100 g.

1. **Dough requirement.** Fendus 6 × 400 = 2,400 g; tabatières 6 × 400 = 2,400 g; couronnes 4 × 500 = 2,000 g. Total **6,800 g**.
2. **Batch.** 6,800 × 1.02 = 6,936 g. Flour = 6,936 ÷ 1.823 = 3,805 g → **3,900 g**. Water 2,496 g; salt 70.2 g; fresh yeast 58.5 g; pâte fermentée 585 g. Total 3,900 × 1.823 = 7,109.7 g ≥ 6,936 g (lesson [06.2](../module-06/lesson-02.md)).
3. **Dividing and pre-shaping.** 12 pieces of 400 g pre-shaped as loose balls (tabatières) and loose cylinders (fendus; a bâtard base gives a longer split); 4 pieces of 500 g as balls (couronnes). Détente 25 minutes, covered.
4. **Shaping order.** In division order, one shape at a time so the hands settle into one movement: fendus, then tabatières, then couronnes. Fendus and tabatières go groove or flap **down** on a floured couche; couronnes seam down.
5. **Check.** Every third fendu next to the first: same length (about 25 cm), groove pressed to the same depth. One tabatière's flap is visibly thicker: rolled again before it goes in the couche, not after.
6. **Loading.** At the end of apprêt (poke test), each fendu and tabatière is turned over onto the loader so the groove and flap face up; the couronnes are cut with five short cuts each. Steam, deck at 240 °C, about 30 minutes; couronnes about 33 minutes.
7. **Control after cooling.** One fendu weighs 326 g: a loss of (400 − 326) ÷ 400 = 18.5 %, as planned. Salt: the pâte fermentée carries its own salt, so salt is 1.8 ÷ 167.3 = 1.076 % of the dough (lesson 08.5). 400 × 1.076 % = 4.30 g in 326 g → **1.32 g per 100 g**, under the 1.4 g limit for pain courant (lesson [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)).

## Practice

You make a home batch of pain courant dough, divide two pieces of 400 g, and shape them into two different forms, one from each family: a **fendu** and a **tabatière** (or, as a second round, a boule and a couronne). You bake them together.

> [!WARNING]
> The oven is at 240 °C with steam. Use dry oven gloves and long sleeves; steam only into a metal tray preheated in the oven on the lowest shelf (never pour water into a hot glass or ceramic dish: it can shatter), pour, close the door and step back. Turning a proofed loaf over onto a board and sliding it onto a hot tray is a two-handed move: clear the space in front of the oven first. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers and cover the lame after use.

### You need

- Minimum: scale (1 g) and a 0.1 g pocket scale for yeast and salt, probe thermometer, large bowl, scraper, lidded container for pointage, rolling pin (or a thin wooden rod such as the handle of a wooden spoon for the fendu), two clean tea towels well rubbed with flour on a tray (the couche), baking paper, a flat board or the back of a tray as a peel, baking tray, metal steam tray, oven gloves, lame, wire rack, ruler, [bake log](../../templates/bake-log.md).
- Professional equivalent: divider-rounder or hand division, linen couche on boards, bannetons for boules, planchette and oven loader, deck oven with steam.

### Ingredients

PC-02 on 500 g of flour, with the pâte fermentée of lesson [08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md) (or PD-01 direct: water 65 %, no pâte fermentée):

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Home batch | Professional batch (worked example) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour T55 | 100 | 500 g | 3,900 g |
| Water at the calculated temperature | 64 | 320 g | 2,496 g |
| Fine salt | 1.8 | 9 g | 70.2 g |
| Fresh yeast (or 2.5 g instant dry) | 1.5 | 7.5 g | 58.5 g |
| Pâte fermentée | 15 | 75 g | 585 g |
| **Total** | **182.3** | **911.5 g** | **7,109.7 g** |

Two pâtons of 400 g = 800 g; keep 75 g as your next pâte fermentée; about 36 g covers what sticks to the bowl.

### In Israel

- **Flour:** white flour (קמח לבן, *kemakh lavan*) as the T55, as in [Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md). Strong Israeli white flour makes a tight boule that tears when you drag it: give 10 more minutes of détente rather than more force.
- **Water in a warm kitchen:** four factors with a hand friction factor of 8 (lesson [05.2](../module-05/lesson-02.md) and 08.1). Summer example: flour 29 °C, kitchen 30 °C, pâte fermentée 8 °C: 96 − 29 − 30 − 8 − 8 = **21 °C**; blend tap and fridge water.
- **Apprêt:** at 28-30 °C the pieces can be ready in 40-50 minutes, sooner than the 60-75 minutes of a 24-25 °C kitchen; start the poke test early and preheat the oven before you shape.
- **Bannetons and rolling pins:** bannetons are sold by baking-supply shops and online; a bowl lined with a floured tea towel does the same job. Any smooth rolling pin works; for the fendu a thin rod presses a cleaner groove (checked 2026-10-08).

### Steps

1. **Mix** PC-02 by hand to 24 °C ± 1 °C (lesson 08.1). Pointage about 45-60 minutes with one fold at 30 minutes, judged by the signs (08.2). Keep 75 g of pâte fermentée in the fridge.
2. **Divide** 2 × 400 g ± 2 g. Pre-shape one as a loose cylinder (fendu) and one as a loose ball (tabatière). Cover; détente 20-30 minutes.
3. **Fendu:** shape a bâtard of about 22-25 cm with moderate tension. Dust the top with flour. Press the rod along the middle almost to the bench and roll it a little to widen the groove to about a third of the width. Close the two halves, turn the piece groove down onto the floured towel.
4. **Tabatière:** shape a tight boule (drag on an unfloured patch of bench). Flour one side; roll a third of it into a flap about 3-4 mm thick and as wide as the ball. Dab the edge of the flap with a drop of oil, fold it over the top, set it flap down on the floured towel.
5. Fold the towel up between the two pieces as a wall. Cover. Write the time. Preheat at 240 °C (or your maximum) with the baking tray on the middle shelf and the metal steam tray on the lowest shelf, at least 45 minutes.
6. **Poke test** from 40 minutes. When ready, lay baking paper on the board, turn each piece over onto it (groove up, flap up) with one quick roll of the towel.
7. **Load and steam:** slide the paper onto the hot tray, pour 100-150 mL of hot water into the steam tray, close the door at once and step back. Fan off for the first 10 minutes if your oven allows.
8. After **10 minutes**, remove the steam tray (or vent the door for a few seconds). Bake about **30 minutes** in total, until deep golden-brown; the probe in the centre reads at least 93 °C.
9. **Cool** on the rack. Weigh both loaves after 1 hour and fill in the bake log.

### Targets

- Pâtons 400 g ± 2 g; fendu about 22-25 cm long with a straight groove along the centre; tabatière flap 3-4 mm, covering about a third of the ball.
- Groove and flap facing up in the oven; no extra cuts.
- Core at least 93 °C; baked weight about 320-340 g (a loss of about 15-20 %).

### How you know it worked

The fendu has opened along the whole groove into two even, rounded lips, with no tear at the sides. The tabatière's lid has lifted and stands open over a risen body, with a pale floured edge where it was folded. Both are round in section, not flat, and their crumb is regular with no tunnel under the crust.

### Self-check

- [ ] I decided the shape of each piece at pre-shaping and gave the right pre-shape.
- [ ] I built tension by dragging on the bench, not by tearing the skin.
- [ ] The fendu's groove and the tabatière's flap were floured or oiled so they did not stick back.
- [ ] Both pieces proofed upside down and were turned over at loading.
- [ ] I weighed the pâtons and the cooled loaves and calculated the loss.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fendu closed up, no split | Groove too shallow; no flour in it; piece proofed groove up and the lips fused | — | Press almost to the bench; flour the groove; proof groove down |
| Fendu tore at the side instead of the groove | Over-tight bâtard; under-proofed; groove off-centre | — | Less tension for a fendu; poke test; press along the exact centre |
| Tabatière lid did not lift | Flap too thick (over 5 mm); stuck back to the ball | — | Roll to 3-4 mm; oil or flour the edge before folding |
| Tabatière lid torn or burnt | Flap too thin; flap too long, hanging over the edge | — | 3-4 mm; flap covers about a third, not more |
| Couronne hole closed | Hole too small; no support during apprêt | Bake at once | Open to a third of the width; at home, stand an oiled ramekin in the hole during apprêt |
| Couronne opened at the joint | Cylinder ends not overlapped and pressed | — | Overlap the ends 3-4 cm and seal well |
| Boule flat and wide | Too little tension; dough slack; over-proofed | Bake at once, hotter | Drag on an unfloured bench; banneton for support; poke test earlier |
| Pieces uneven within a batch | Different movements and strokes; weights off | — | Divide to ± 2 g; same sequence for every piece; compare with the first |

## Review

- The day's baguette dough also makes pièces façonnées: decide the family at pre-shaping (ball for boule, tabatière, couronne; cylinder for bâtard, fendu).
- Tension comes from dragging the dough on a bench that grips it; too much flour stops it, too much force tears it.
- Fendu and tabatière proof upside down, are turned over at loading and are not scored: the floured groove and the oiled flap are their "cuts". Boules, bâtards and couronnes are scored.
- A 400-500 g piece bakes about 30-35 minutes at 240 °C with steam, to a core of at least 93 °C; it loses a smaller share of its weight than a baguette.
- Shaped pieces in two forms are part of the production test: see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md).

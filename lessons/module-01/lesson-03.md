# 01.3 · Working Safely and Hygienically from Day One

Hygiene and safety are not a module you study at the end; they are habits you build from your first bake. This lesson sets out the professional clothing, the hand-washing standard used in French bakeries and the injuries that end bakers' careers, then you set up your home fournil and test your own hand washing.

## Why it matters

Your hands touch every piece of dough, and the bread leaves the oven to be touched again. The bakery profession's hygiene guide (CNBPF, 2026) calls hands the first route of contamination in the business. Hygiene and safety also carry their own marks in the production test, and the exam service warns that a candidate without the required protective equipment can get 0 in a practical test (see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md)). On the health side, the INRS (France's occupational health and safety institute) names flour as the leading cause of occupational asthma: a baker who becomes allergic to flour often has to leave the trade.

## How it works

### Professional clothing and why each item exists

| Item | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Jacket (veste) | Clean, light colour, worn only at work | Shows dirt; keeps outdoor dust and hair off the food; protects arms from heat |
| Trousers | Work trousers, changed regularly | Same reasons |
| Apron (tablier) | Washable fabric or disposable; changed when soiled | The dirtiest layer; replace it rather than the jacket |
| Cap or hair net | Covers all hair | Hair falls constantly; a hair in bread is the most common customer complaint |
| Safety shoes | Non-slip sole, toe protection, closed | Floors get slippery with flour and water; 25 kg sacks and trays fall |
| No jewellery or watch | Rings, bracelets and watches off; short nails without varnish | They hold dirt and microbes, can fall into dough and can catch in machines |
| Covered wounds | Waterproof dressing, in a bright colour, plus a glove on top | An infected cut carries *Staphylococcus aureus*; a coloured dressing is easy to find if it falls into dough |

If the employer requires a specific uniform, the employer must provide it and pay for its cleaning (CNBPF guide).

### Washing hands: when

The CNBPF guide lists the moments hand washing is required:

- when starting work and after every break;
- after the toilet;
- after blowing your nose;
- after "dirty" operations: receiving goods, unpacking cartons, breaking eggs, handling raw foods, taking out waste and bins, cleaning;
- before sensitive operations: making sandwiches, handling crème pâtissière after cooking, and similar finished products.

Add the everyday cases the same logic covers: after touching your face, hair, phone or money, and after coughing or sneezing (into your elbow, never over the food).

### Washing hands: how

The bakery standard, from the CNBPF guide:

1. Wet hands, wrists and forearms with lukewarm water.
2. Apply liquid soap and rub for **at least 15 seconds**: between the fingers, thumbs, backs of hands, nails, up to the wrists and forearms.
3. Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water.
4. Dry with a single-use paper towel, hands first, then forearms. Use the towel to turn off a hand-operated tap.

Fifteen seconds is the minimum, not the target; most people need 20-30 seconds to reach every surface. Hand gel can complete washing (for example during an epidemic) but never replaces it, and disposable gloves do not replace hand washing either: a glove that has touched a phone is a dirty glove.

### Illness

Tell your manager before your shift if you have diarrhoea, vomiting, a fever or an infected wound on your hands. A **healthy carrier** (porteur sain) shows no symptoms but still spreads germs, which is why the rules apply to everyone every time, not only to people who feel ill.

### The occupational risks of the trade

| Risk | How it happens | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Flour dust: rhinitis, asthma | Dust released when tipping flour, mixing, dusting the bench, sweeping | Pour flour low and slowly; never shake the sack; put water in the mixer bowl before flour; dust the bench lightly; clean with a vacuum suited to flour, never a broom or compressed air (INRS) |
| Burns | Oven doors, trays, steam, hot sugar | Heat-resistant gloves, long sleeves, tell people "chaud" when you carry a hot tray, stand back when steam is released |
| Cuts | Lame, knife, dough cutter, broken glass | Cut away from your hand; keep blades in a holder, never loose in a bowl or under dough |
| Back and joint injuries | Lifting 25 kg sacks, bending to low ovens, repetitive shaping | Bend the knees, keep the load close, use trolleys; ask for help |
| Falls | Flour and water on the floor, stairs to cellars, poor lighting | Clean spills at once; safety shoes; keep passages clear |
| Machines | Mixer hooks, dividers, sheeters | Never put a hand in a running machine; wait for a full stop; use the guards; only use a machine you have been trained on |

The INRS lists back pain, falls, burns, and allergies and asthma among the main risks of the bakery trade. In a business, the employer records these risks and the measures against them in the **Document unique**; your job is to follow those measures.

## Worked example

Inès arrives at 6:00 for a pain courant shift. Here is her first hour. Where must she wash her hands?

| Time | Action | Wash? |
|---|---|---|
| 6:00 | Changes in the changing room, puts on clean jacket, cap and shoes | Yes, after changing and before entering the fournil (start of work) |
| 6:05 | Weighs flour, water, salt, yeast | No |
| 6:15 | Starts the mixer | No |
| 6:20 | Answers her phone, which she had left in her apron pocket | Yes, and the phone goes back to the locker |
| 6:25 | Takes the bins out to the yard | Yes, after waste |
| 6:35 | Breaks 12 eggs for the brioche | Yes, after breaking eggs |
| 6:45 | Divides the bread dough | No (already clean) |
| 6:55 | Blows her nose | Yes |
| 7:00 | Shapes baguettes | No (washed at 6:55) |

Five hand washes in an hour, each about 30 seconds including drying: 2.5 minutes. The phone was avoidable: no phone in the fournil saves one wash and removes a source of contamination. That is how professionals reduce washing time: by organising the work, not by skipping washes.

## Practice

You set up your home fournil, dress to the standard, and test how well you really wash your hands.

> [!CAUTION]
> From this lesson on, keep the oven area clear of anything that can burn or fall, keep your knife or lame in a holder or on a marked spot, and keep children and pets out of the kitchen while the oven is on.

### You need

- An apron, a clean cap or hair tie and scarf that covers all your hair, closed shoes with a non-slip sole.
- Liquid soap, paper towels, a timer (phone left outside the work area, or a kitchen timer).
- 1 teaspoon of vegetable oil and 1 teaspoon of ground cinnamon or cocoa powder (for the wash test).
- Professional equivalent: full tenue with safety shoes, a dedicated hand basin with a non-hand-operated tap, soap and towel dispensers.

### Steps

1. Clear your worktop of everything not used for baking (post, keys, phone, plants). Wipe it with hot soapy water and dry it.
2. Choose one sink or a basin as your hand basin for baking sessions; put soap and paper towels beside it.
3. Pick a fixed place for your blade and knife, out of the dough area.
4. Dress: apron, hair fully covered, rings and watch off, nails short, any cut covered with a waterproof dressing and a glove.
5. Wash test: rub the oil over both hands like hand cream, including between the fingers and around the nails. Sprinkle the cinnamon on and rub it in. It stands in for invisible dirt.
6. Wash with the 4-step method, timing at least 15 seconds of rubbing.
7. Look at your hands in good light. Brown traces show the places you missed: usually thumbs, fingertips, nails, between the fingers and the wrists.
8. Wash again, concentrating on those places. Note how many seconds you needed for clean hands.

### Targets

- All hair covered; no jewellery or watch.
- No visible traces after washing.
- Rubbing time for a clean result written down (expect 20-30 seconds).

### How you know it worked

After the second wash there is no colour on thumbs, nails, fingertips or wrists, and you can do the same wash again from memory without checking the steps.

### Self-check

- [ ] My worktop holds only baking items and was cleaned before I started.
- [ ] I have a fixed hand-wash point with soap and paper towels.
- [ ] I can list the moments when hand washing is required.
- [ ] My wash test left no traces at the second attempt.
- [ ] I know three ways to reduce flour dust at home (pour low, no shaking, no sweeping dry).

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traces on thumbs and fingertips after the test | Rubbing palms only, too short | Wash again, rub each thumb in the other fist and fingertips in the palm | Count to at least 15 while rubbing; follow a fixed order |
| Sneezing or itchy eyes while weighing flour | Flour dust from pouring high or shaking the bag | Step back, let the dust settle, wipe with a damp cloth | Pour low, scoop rather than tip, never sweep dry |
| A hair in the dough | Hair not fully covered | Remove it and the dough around it; check the batch | Cover all hair before entering the work area |
| Burnt forearm from the oven door or tray | Short sleeves, no oven gloves | Cool under cool running water for about 20 minutes; get medical help for a large or blistered burn | Long sleeves and dry oven gloves; wet gloves conduct heat |
| Phone or money handled during production | Phone kept in an apron pocket | Wash hands before touching dough again | Leave the phone outside the work area |

## Review

- Professional clothing protects the food and you: clean jacket and apron, all hair covered, safety shoes, no jewellery, covered wounds.
- Wash hands at the start of work, after breaks, toilets, nose-blowing and every dirty operation, before sensitive ones; rub at least 15 seconds and dry with single-use paper.
- Gloves and gel never replace hand washing.
- Flour dust causes occupational asthma: pour low, never shake sacks, put water in first, vacuum instead of sweeping.
- Hygiene, safety and protective equipment are marked in the exam; see [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md). [Module 17](../module-17/lesson-01.md) covers food safety in full.

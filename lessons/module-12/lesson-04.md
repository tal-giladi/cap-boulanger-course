# 12.4 · Braids, Navettes and Animals

Pain au lait and pain brioché are sold shaped: a braided loaf for the weekend, small navettes for the school snack, hedgehogs and turtles for children. Shaping a pâte levée is about regularity more than skill tricks: strands of the same weight and length, braids with the same tension end to end, small pieces that all look alike on the tray. This lesson explains the one-, two- and three-strand braids, the navette and a few animals, and you shape a braid and a set of small pieces from your pain au lait dough.

## Why it matters

The référentiel lists the shaping of pâtes levées as large pieces (braids) and small pieces (navettes, braids, animals) under C2.3, and the production test presents a pâte levée in these shapes (which shapes and how many: [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md)). Customers see the shaping before anything else: a braid that is thick at one end and thin at the other, or a tray of hedgehogs in five sizes, looks amateur even if the dough is perfect. And the marks for presentation come from the same thing: identical weights, symmetrical shapes, even colour (C3.2).

## How it works

### The dough for shaping

Shaping needs a dough that is **firm and cold**: the pointage différé of lesson [12.3](lesson-03.md) (a night at +4 °C), or at least a cold rest (the EP1 2019 sheet: 30 minutes at 4 °C after dividing). Warm butter-rich dough sticks to the bench, tears and slumps; cold dough rolls into clean strands that keep their edges. Use almost no flour on the bench: the strand must grip slightly to roll, as for a baguette (lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)).

**Dividing is half the shaping.** Weigh every strand (± 2 g on a small piece, ± 3 g on a strand of a large braid) and roll strands for the same braid to the same length. Pre-shape strands into short cylinders, rest them covered 10-15 minutes in the cold, then roll them out: a strand that springs back is rested again, not forced (King Arthur Baking).

**Rolling a strand.** Start with both hands in the middle, roll outwards with light, even pressure, moving the hands apart; for braids, keep strands slightly thicker in the middle and tapering towards the ends, so the finished braid is plump in the middle and tapered at both ends.

### The three braids

![Three braiding methods: one strand, a long strand looped like a pretzel with one end passed through the loop and the loop given half a turn each time; two strands, laid in a cross and the ends of one strand swapped over the other, then the other, giving a tall square braid; three strands, braided from the centre, left over middle then right over middle, without pulling, then turned to braid the other half. Bottom: a navette, a hedgehog with snipped spines, a snail and a turtle](../../assets/m12-braids.svg)

**Three strands (tresse à trois branches).** The classic and the easiest to keep even. Lay the three strands side by side, close but not touching. Start **in the middle**: left strand over the middle one, then right strand over the new middle one, and repeat to the end without pulling: you are laying strands over each other, not tightening them (King Arthur Baking). Pinch the end, turn the braid round and braid the other half the same way, then pinch and tuck both ends under. Starting from the middle gives two identical halves; starting from one end makes one end tighter than the other. For a 300 g braid: 3 × 100 g, about 35 cm each.

**Two strands (tresse à deux branches).** It looks more complicated than it is. Lay the two strands in a cross (+), centres on top of each other. Take the two ends of the **lower** strand and swap them over the upper one (each end crosses to the other side). Then take the two ends of the other strand and swap them the same way. Repeat, always alternating, until the ends are too short; pinch them and lay the braid on its side. The result is a tall, square braid that looks like four strands. For 300 g: 2 × 150 g, about 40 cm each.

**One strand (tresse à une branche).** A single long strand, about 60-70 cm for 300 g, thicker in the middle. Lay it in a U, cross the two ends to make a loop (like a pretzel), pass one end through the loop from underneath, give the loop half a turn, and pass the end through again; continue until the strand is used up, and tuck the end underneath. Bakeries and schools teach several variants of the one-strand braid: what matters is that the crossings are even and the piece is symmetrical. Practise the movement first with a piece of rope.

All braids: no tension (a tight braid splits along the strands in the oven), even crossings, ends tucked under, placed on the tray at an angle with room to grow, egg-washed gently into the cracks.

### Small pieces

| Piece | Weight (course) | How | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Navette | 50 g | round, then roll to an oval about 10-12 cm with pointed ends, tight on top; optionally snip a line along the top with scissors after the egg wash, or sprinkle pearl sugar | all the same length, both ends equally pointed |
| Petite tresse | 60 g | two strands of 30 g or three of 20 g, braided like the large ones, ends tucked | same number of crossings on every piece |
| Hérisson (hedgehog) | 60 g | tight ball, one side rolled to a point (the nose); after the egg wash, rows of small snips with the scissors' points held low to lift the spines; two chocolate-chip eyes | spines in regular rows, nose centred |
| Escargot (snail) | 60 g | strand of about 30 cm, one end thicker (the head), the rest rolled into a flat spiral, head raised | spiral even, head the same on every piece |
| Tortue (turtle) | 60 g | 45 g body ball, 4 feet of 2.5 g, head of 4 g, tail of 1 g, pressed under the body; shell crossed with the back of a knife | all feet the same size, symmetrical |

The EP1 2019 sheet shapes its animals at 60 g and braids at 300 g; your production sheet fixes the weights. Whatever the animal, the tray must show **one** animal repeated identically, not a zoo of different sizes.

> [!WARNING]
> Scissors and knife: cut with the points away from the hand holding the dough, close the scissors and put them down flat after use. The oven is at about 180 °C: oven gloves, stand back when you open the door.

### Proof, egg wash, bake

Egg wash once after shaping (the skin stays supple), proof at about 25 °C until about doubled and light (EP1: 1 h-1 h 45), egg wash again very gently, especially into the cracks of the braids, where unwashed dough stays pale. Bake at about 180 °C (EP1 sheet): small pieces about 12-15 minutes, a 300 g braid about 20-25 minutes, until evenly deep golden including where the strands cross, core about 90 °C. Large and small pieces need different times: bake them on separate trays, and put them on a rack as soon as they come out.

## Worked example

Wednesday, a school fair orders for Saturday 10:00 from sheet **PB-01** (total 203.5 %): **4 three-strand braids of 300 g, 20 hedgehogs of 60 g, 20 navettes of 50 g**. Process losses 2 %, flour rounded up to the next 100 g. Method as the EP1 sheet: pointage différé, shaping in the morning.

1. **Dough.** 4 × 300 + 20 × 60 + 20 × 50 = 1,200 + 1,200 + 1,000 = 3,400 g; with 2 %: 3,468 g.
2. **Flour.** 3,468 ÷ 2.035 = 1,704 g → **1,800 g**. Milk 756 g; egg 324 g; sugar 234 g; salt 36 g; fresh yeast 63 g; butter 450 g. Total 3,663 g.
3. **Friday 15:00.** Mix (liquids from the TB, lesson 12.3), 30 minutes at the fournil with a fold, flattened in a filmed tray, cold room +4 °C.
4. **Saturday 5:00. Divide cold:** 12 strands of 100 g, 20 × 60 g, 20 × 50 g; about 200 g is spare after the process losses. Pre-shape; 30 minutes in the cold room.
5. **5:40-6:20. Shape:** the braids first (they proof a little longer), then hedgehogs and navettes; first egg wash. Strands checked: 100 g ± 3 g, 35 cm.
6. **Apprêt** at 25 °C, 80 % humidity. 7:20: small pieces ready; second egg wash, snip the hedgehogs' spines, bake 180 °C, 13 minutes. 7:40: braids ready; second egg wash into the cracks, bake 180 °C, 22 minutes.
7. **Check (C3.2).** One hedgehog weighs 53 g after cooling (loss about 12 %); two navettes are 1 cm shorter than the rest: noted on the sheet as a shaping point for next time, still sold. Packed in boxes at 9:00 with the label: wheat (gluten), milk, egg; soy if the chocolate chips for the eyes list it.

## Practice

You shape the **half of PL-01 kept in the fridge in lesson 12.3** (about 490 g), or a fresh **PB-01 on 250 g of flour** (about 509 g) made as in 12.3 and chilled at least 2 hours: one three-strand braid and a set of small pieces.

> [!WARNING]
> Oven at about 180 °C: dry oven gloves, open the door and step back. Scissors and knife: points away from your other hand.

> [!CAUTION]
> Egg wash is raw egg: keep it in the fridge between coats, discard what is left, wash your hands after handling eggs. The pieces contain wheat (gluten), milk and egg; chocolate eyes may add soy and milk.

- Professional equivalent: cold room at +4 °C, proofing cabinet at 25 °C, perforated trays, rack oven at 180 °C.

### In Israel

Checked 2026-10-08.

- **The dough** is your PL-01 or PB-01 from lesson 12.3, made with Israeli white flour, full-fat milk, eggs and block butter ([Flour in Israel](../../references/flour-in-israel.md)). Keep it in the fridge at 5 °C or below until the moment you divide it.
- **Shaping in a 30 °C kitchen:** rich dough softens on the bench within minutes. Work in the air-conditioned room, take out only the strands you are rolling, and put the rest back in the fridge; if strands go sticky, chill them 10 minutes.
- **Braiding:** if you have braided challah, the three-strand braid is the same movement; the difference is the dough (butter, softer) and the rule here of braiding from the middle without pulling.
- **Pearl sugar** (*sukar perlim*, סוכר פנינים) and chocolate chips (*shokolad chips*, שוקולד צ'יפס) are in baking-supply shops; read the chips' label for milk and soy.
- **Proofing** at 25-27 °C, not at 30 °C: the air-conditioned room or the oven with the light on, checked with the probe.

### Steps

1. **Practise the movements** with rope or cloth strips: two-strand cross three times, one-strand loop three times, until your hands know them.
2. **Divide** the cold dough: 3 × 80 g, 2 × 30 g, 60 g, 50 g, 60 g (± 2 g). Pre-shape strands as short cylinders and the others as balls. Cover, fridge 15 minutes.
3. **Three-strand braid:** roll the three 80 g pieces to 30 cm, slightly thicker in the middle. Braid from the middle without pulling, turn, braid the other half; tuck the ends. Place it at an angle on the tray.
4. **Small braids:** two 30 g strands of 20 cm crossed and braided by the two-strand method; one 60 g strand of about 40 cm for the one-strand braid.
5. **Navette:** round the 50 g, roll to 10-12 cm with pointed ends.
6. **Hedgehog:** tight ball, roll one side to a point, press in two chocolate-chip eyes.
7. **First egg wash** on everything. Proof at 25-27 °C until about doubled and light (about 1 h-1 h 30). Preheat to 180 °C conventional (165-170 °C fan).
8. **Second egg wash** (into the braid's cracks); snip the hedgehog's spines in rows and a line along the navette. Pearl sugar if you like.
9. **Bake:** small pieces 12-15 minutes, braid 20-25 minutes, until deep golden even in the crossings. Cool on a rack.
10. **Check:** weigh each piece after cooling, measure the braid, and score the tray with the quality rubric: shape, regularity, colour.

### Targets

- Pieces within ± 2 g of their target before baking; braid strands the same length.
- Braid symmetrical, both halves alike, plump in the middle, tapered ends tucked under, no split along the strands.
- Even, deep golden colour including the crossings; no pale cracks.

### How you know it worked

The braid looks the same from both ends, with regular crossings and a glossy, even colour, and it has risen without tearing between the strands. The navette is a neat spindle with two equal points; the hedgehog has a centred nose and spines that stand up in rows. Put next to the shapes in the référentiel's list, your pieces are recognisable at a glance, and a second set made the same way would look identical.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix now | Prevent next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Braid thick at one end, thin at the other | Started braiding from one end; strands of different lengths | Re-tuck the thin end tighter | Braid from the middle; strands weighed and the same length |
| Braid split along the strands in the oven | Braided tight; under-proofed | — | Lay strands without pulling; full apprêt |
| Strands shrink back while rolling | Dough not rested, too elastic | Rest 10 minutes and roll again | Pre-shape and rest strands in the cold |
| Strands sticky, flattened shapes | Dough too warm; butter soft | Chill 10-15 min | Shape cold dough from the fridge, little flour |
| Pale lines in the crossings | Egg wash not brushed into the cracks | — | Second egg wash with a soft brush into the cracks |
| Small pieces of different sizes | Divided by eye | — | Weigh every piece; ± 2 g |
| Hedgehog spines flat or burnt at the tips | Snipped before the egg wash or too deep; oven too hot | Lower the oven 10 °C | Snip after the second egg wash, small and shallow; 180 °C |

## Review

- Shape pâte levée cold and firm (night at +4 °C or a cold rest), on a bench with almost no flour.
- Weigh every strand and piece; strands of a braid have the same weight and length.
- Three strands from the middle without pulling; two strands by swapping the ends in a cross; one strand by passing the end through a turning loop.
- Egg wash twice, into the cracks; about 25 °C apprêt; about 180 °C bake, small and large pieces on separate trays.
- The shapes the production test asks for: [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../../references/cap-exam.md).

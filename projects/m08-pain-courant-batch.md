# Project: Pain Courant Batch

Your first full bread from order to evaluation. In Part A you prepare the paperwork for a bakery order of PC-02 baguettes and rolls: the technical sheet, the water temperature, tomorrow's pâte fermentée, the salt check and the timeline. In Part B you make a 1 kg batch of PC-02 at home, shape six baguettes, bake them in two loads and evaluate them as a batch control. The Academy cannot grade your bread or your photos: compare your work honestly with the rubric and the reference answers; this self-assessment does not replace the official practical exam.

## Brief

> **Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle — Commande de production, mercredi**
>
> | Produit | Quantité | Poids pâton | Pour |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Baguette (pain courant, PC-02) | 30 | 350 g | magasin 7:00 |
> | Petit pain rond (PC-02) | 24 | 60 g | livraison restaurant 7:30 |
>
> Fiche PC-02: farine T55 100 %, eau 64 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,5 %, pâte fermentée 15 %. TPV 24 °C. Pertes de fabrication 2 %; farine arrondie à la centaine de grammes supérieure.
> **Garder 950 g de pâte fermentée pour jeudi**, prise sur ce lot. Pâte fermentée en stock pour aujourd'hui: 1 100 g.
> Relevés à 2:30: farine 23 °C, fournil 25 °C, pâte fermentée 6 °C. Facteur de friction PC-02 (4 facteurs, pétrissage amélioré): 26.
> Temps: pesée 15 min, pétrissage 10 min, pointage 45 min, division et préfaçonnage 20 min, détente 20 min, façonnage 30 min, apprêt 1 h 15, cuisson baguettes 22 min (four 3 soles, 12 baguettes par sole), petits pains 15 min, ressuage 30 min minimum.
> Pertes à la cuisson mesurées la semaine dernière: baguettes 21 %, petits pains 23 %.

In English: 30 baguettes at 350 g for the shop at 7:00 and 24 rolls at 60 g for a 7:30 delivery, both PC-02. This batch must also provide Thursday's 950 g of pâte fermentée. Readings, friction factor, stage times and last week's baking losses are given.

## You need

- Part A: calculator, the [production sheet template](../templates/production-sheet.md), the [organigramme template](../templates/organigramme.md) and the [temperature log](../templates/temperature-log.md).
- Part B: the home equipment of lessons [08.1](../lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md) to [08.4](../lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md) (scales, probe thermometer, bowl, scraper, lidded containers, couche or tea towels, two trays, metal steam tray, lame, oven gloves, wire rack, bread knife), the [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md), the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md) and the [non-conformity report](../templates/non-conformity-report.md).
- Time for Part B: an evening (15 minutes) and about 5 hours the next day, of which about 1 h 30 hands-on.

> [!WARNING]
> Part B uses a 250 °C oven and steam. Dry oven gloves and long sleeves; steam only in a metal tray preheated in the oven, never water into a hot glass dish; pour and step back. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers, keep the guard on the lame between uses, and cut bread with a serrated knife on a stable board.

## Steps

### Part A — the bakery order (about 60 minutes)

1. **Dough requirement.** Baguettes and rolls, then the total including Thursday's 950 g of pâte fermentée.
2. **Batch.** Add 2 % process losses, calculate the flour (round up to the next 100 g) and every ingredient. Check that today's 1,100 g of pâte fermentée covers 15 %. Check the batch total covers the requirement.
3. **What if?** Show what would happen to the order if the batch were calculated without Thursday's pâte fermentée and the 950 g were then taken from it.
4. **Water temperature** with the pâte fermentée as fourth factor.
5. **Salt check.** Salt per 100 g of bread for a baguette and a roll with last week's measured losses.
6. **Timeline.** Work backwards from 7:00: when must weighing start, when must the oven be ready, and when are the rolls out and cooled?
7. **Fill in** one production sheet (PC-02 for this batch, with temperatures and process) and one organigramme.

### Part B — the home batch (about 5 hours)

8. **The evening before:** make 167 g of pâte fermentée (flour 100 g, water 64 g, salt 1.8 g, fresh yeast 1.5 g or 0.5 g instant); 1 hour at room temperature (20 minutes in a hot summer kitchen), then the fridge.
9. **Plan:** PC-02 on 1 kg of flour: flour 1,000 g, water 640 g, salt 18 g, fresh yeast 15 g (or 5 g instant), pâte fermentée 150 g; total 1,823 g. Six pâtons of 270 g (1,620 g) and 150 g kept as your next pâte fermentée. Measure your tray and set your baguette length (about 38 cm).
10. **Mix** by hand to 24 °C ± 1 °C with the four-factor water calculation (lesson [08.1](../lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md); in a warm kitchen the In Israel notes there). Record all temperatures.
11. **Pointage** about 45-60 minutes with a fold at 30 minutes, judged by the signs; keep 150 g of pâte fermentée, labelled (lesson [08.2](../lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md)).
12. **Divide** 6 × 270 g ± 2 g, pre-shape loose cylinders, détente until they stretch easily.
13. **Shape** set 1 (three baguettes), then set 2 about 20-25 minutes later; measure every baguette (lesson [08.3](../lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md)).
14. **Score and bake** each set when the poke test says ready: three overlapping cuts, steam, 250 °C, 20-25 minutes, core at least 93 °C (lesson [08.4](../lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)).
15. **Evaluate** as a batch control after 1 hour of cooling: weights, loss, salt per 100 g, rubric for one baguette of each set, the main defect traced to its stage, a three-line report (lesson [08.5](../lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md)).
16. **Write three lines:** what matched the plan, what did not, the one change for the next bake.

## Deliverables

- Part A: the calculations for steps 1-6, the completed production sheet and organigramme, and a one-line note to the chef about Thursday's pâte fermentée.
- Part B: the temperature log (inputs, calculated water, dough temperature), the bake log with every time and weight (pâtons, lengths, cooled weights), losses and salt per 100 g, two rubric scores, the defect analysis, the report and your three lines.
- Optional: a photo of the six baguettes and a cut section, kept with your log for your own comparison.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Batch calculation | Thursday's pâte fermentée counted in the requirement; 2 % added; flour rounded up; every ingredient correct; total ≥ requirement | Pâte fermentée taken from the order's dough; flour rounded down; loss added to the flour only |
| Water temperature | Four factors, all four inputs and the four-factor friction factor | Three factors, or the pâte fermentée left out |
| Salt check | Both products checked with their own measured loss | Planning figure used for all products |
| Timeline | Worked back from 7:00 including baking and cooling; start time within 10 minutes of the reference | Cooling or oven readiness forgotten |
| Home dough | 23-25 °C after mixing; pointage decided by signs; pâte fermentée kept and labelled | Temperature not measured; pointage by the clock only |
| Dividing and shaping | Six pâtons within ± 2 g; lengths within ± 2 cm of the target; seams closed | Weights or lengths not recorded; seams opened |
| Scoring and baking | Apprêt ended on the poke test; overlapping cuts along the midline; steam safely made; core ≥ 93 °C | Cuts across the loaf; no steam; pale bread |
| Evaluation | Sample weighed after cooling; loss and salt calculated; rubric complete; defect traced to a stage with evidence; factual report | Bread judged warm; several changes planned at once; report without facts |
| Safety and hygiene | Oven, steam and blade handled as in the warning; station cleaned | Steps skipped |

1. Baguettes 30 × 350 = 10,500 g; rolls 24 × 60 = 1,440 g; order 11,940 g; with Thursday's pâte fermentée **12,890 g**.
2. With 2 %: 12,890 × 1.02 = 13,147.8 g. Flour 13,147.8 ÷ 1.823 = 7,212 g → **7,300 g**. Water 4,672 g; salt 131.4 g; fresh yeast 109.5 g; pâte fermentée 1,095 g (the 1,100 g in stock covers it). Total 7,300 × 1.823 = **13,307.9 g** ≥ 13,147.8 g.
3. Without Thursday's 950 g: 11,940 × 1.02 = 12,178.8 g → flour 6,681 g → 6,700 g → batch 12,214.1 g. Only about 274 g would be spare before losses: taking 950 g would leave the order about 680 g short, more after process losses, two baguettes or more missing at 7:00.
4. 4 × 24 = 96; 96 − 23 − 25 − 6 − 26 = **16 °C**.
5. Salt is 1.076 % of the dough. Baguette: 350 × 1.076 % = 3.77 g in 350 × 0.79 = 276.5 g → **1.36 g per 100 g** (conforms). Roll: 60 × 1.076 % = 0.646 g in 60 × 0.77 = 46.2 g → **1.40 g per 100 g** (1.397 g: conforms, just; a longer bake would take it over).
6. 30 baguettes fit in one load on three decks. Out of the oven by 6:30 (30 min cooling) → in at 6:08 → apprêt from 4:53 → shaping from 4:23 → détente from 4:03 → dividing from 3:43 → pointage from 2:58 → mixing from 2:48 → **weighing from 2:33** (about 2:30). Oven ready by 6:08 (switch on about an hour before). Rolls: second load at about 6:30, out at 6:45, cooled by 7:15 for the 7:30 delivery.

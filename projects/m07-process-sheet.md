# Project: The Process Sheet

This project brings module 7 together. You turn a realistic Saturday order into a complete process sheet: all 18 stages for two doughs, with times, targets and checks, the water temperatures, the oven-loading plan, the cooling and storage of each product and the cleaning slots. Then you write the same sheet for a home order, bake it from the sheet alone, and compare what you planned with what happened.

## Brief

You are the ouvrier boulanger at Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle. On Friday afternoon the chef leaves you this order and asks for your fiche de fabrication before you go home.

> **Commande de production — samedi 21 novembre 2026**
>
> | Produit | Quantité | Poids | Fiche | Pour |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Baguette (pain courant) | 36 | pâton 350 g | PC-02 | magasin 7:00 |
> | Petit pain rond (pain courant) | 30 | pâton 60 g | PC-02 | livraison hôtel 7:45 |
> | Pain de campagne (boule) | 10 | pâton 590 g | CA-01 | restaurant, dimanche 11:00 |
>
> PC-02: farine T55 100 %, eau 64 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,5 %, pâte fermentée 15 %. TPV 24 °C. Pétrissage amélioré (échauffement environ 5 °C). Pointage 45 min; détente 20 min; apprêt 1 h 15 à 25 °C; four 250 °C, buée; baguettes 22 min, petits pains 15 min.
> CA-01 (from the [module 6](../lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md) [workbook](m06-calculation-workbook.md)): lot de 3 600 g de farine (T65 3 060 g, seigle 540 g), eau 2 520 g au total, sel 64,8 g, levain liquide 1 440 g (préparé vendredi soir). TPV 24 °C. Pétrissage lent (échauffement environ 3 °C). Pointage 1 h 30 à 2 h; apprêt 1 h 45 à 2 h en bannetons; four 230 °C, buée, 40 min.
>
> Pertes de fabrication 2 %; farine arrondie à la centaine de grammes supérieure.
> Relevés prévus samedi 1:30: fournil 22 °C, farine 20 °C, pâte fermentée 6 °C, levain 26 °C, eau du réseau 16 °C.
> Matériel: un pétrin à spirale; four à sole deux étages, chaque étage: 12 baguettes ou 20 petits pains ou 6 boules; préchauffage 1 h.

Part A is the paperwork for this order. Part B is a home order you plan, bake and review:

> **Commande maison:** 4 bâtards de 270 g pour un déjeuner à 13:00, et 2 bâtards de 270 g à congeler. PD-01 sur 1 kg de farine (eau 65 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,5 %), pétrissage à la main. Four ménager, deux fournées.

## You need

- Calculator; the [production sheet template](../templates/production-sheet.md) (one per dough), the [organigramme template](../templates/organigramme.md), the [temperature log](../templates/temperature-log.md), the [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md) and the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md).
- For Part B: the equipment of lessons [07.3](../lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md) to [07.5](../lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md), including the metal steam tray, couche, lame, wire rack, paper and freezer bags.

> [!WARNING]
> Part B uses a 240 °C oven with steam. Preheated metal tray on the lowest shelf only (never a glass dish), 100-150 mL of hot water poured with a long, dry glove, arm extended, face away; close the door and stand back. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers and cover the lame after use.

## Steps

### Part A — the bakery process sheet (about 2 hours)

1. **Order and sheet (stages 1-2).** Calculate the PC-02 dough needed with 2 % losses, the flour (rounded up), every ingredient and the total. Check that the CA-01 batch covers 10 × 590 g with 2 %.
2. **Water (stage 6).** Calculate the water temperature for each dough with the Module 5 convention (lesson [05.2](../lessons/module-05/lesson-02.md)): both doughs have a pre-ferment, so 4 factors; friction factor = heating × 4. Say how you get that water from a 16 °C tap.
3. **Oven plan (stages 14-16).** Plan every load: which product, which deck, what time in and out. Check that the baguettes are cooled (30 minutes) by 7:00 and the rolls by 7:45, and say when the decks must be at 230 °C for the campagne.
4. **Organigramme (stage 3).** Work back from the oven plan to the start time of each dough, with one mixer. Fill in the organigramme template in 15-minute slots: weighing, mixing, pointage, dividing, détente, shaping, apprêt, preheating, loads, cooling, cleaning.
5. **Requisition (stage 4).** List what you take from store on Friday evening, with the checks (lot numbers, dates, pre-ferments).
6. **The 18-stage process sheet.** For each dough, one line per stage: time, target and check (as in lesson [07.1](../lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)). Include the end-of-mixing check, the shaping targets, the scoring (cuts, angle, depth), the steam and the défournement checks.
7. **Finish (stages 17-18).** Write how each product is cooled, controlled and stored or packed until it leaves (the campagne stays overnight), and when and how the station is cleaned.
8. **Two "what if" lines.** (a) The oven is 20 minutes late in heating: what do you do with the PC-02 dough, and when? (b) The baguettes tear in the façonneuse: diagnosis and correction.

### Part B — the home order (about 6 hours, of which 2 hours hands-on)

9. Write the 18-stage process sheet for the home order: work back from 13:00 (bâtards cooled at least 1 hour), with two oven loads of three bâtards, your real preheat time, your water calculation from the morning's readings, cleaning slots and the storage of the two frozen bâtards.
10. Bake from your sheet alone. Keep it beside you, tick each check and write the real time beside the planned one.
11. At each control point, record: dough temperature after mixing, piece weights, bâtard lengths, oven temperature at loading, core temperature, weight after 1 hour of cooling.
12. Score one bâtard with the quality rubric. Freeze two (cooled, sliced, wrapped airtight), labelled with the date.
13. Under the sheet, write the three stages where plan and reality differed most, why, and what you will change on the sheet.

## Deliverables

- Part A: the two production sheets with weights and water temperatures, the oven plan, the organigramme, the requisition, the 18-stage process sheet for each dough, the finish and cleaning plan, and the two "what if" lines.
- Part B: your home process sheet with planned and real times, the temperature and bake logs, the rubric score, and your three lines of review.
- Optional: a photo of the six bâtards on the rack, kept with your log.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Calculations | PC-02 flour rounded up and every weight correct; CA-01 checked against the order | Losses forgotten; weights from the dough instead of the flour |
| Water | 4 factors, friction factor = heating × 4, pre-ferment temperature used; source of the water stated | 3 factors with a pre-ferment; friction counted once as degrees |
| Oven plan | Every load placed; baguettes and rolls cooled by their deadline; temperatures programmed product by product | A deadline missed; two products on one deck at different temperatures |
| Organigramme | Worked back from the oven; one mixer never needed twice; preheat and cleaning placed | Forward plan; overlaps; no preheat |
| 18 stages | Every stage, with time, target and check, for each dough | Stages missing (often ressuage or cleaning); checks vague |
| Finish | Ressuage on racks; campagne in paper overnight, dated; rolls bagged only when cold | Bread bagged warm, refrigerated or not dated |
| Home bake: control | Real times within 15 minutes of the plan, or explained; all control readings recorded | Readings missing; sheet abandoned during the bake |
| Home bake: result | Bâtards 270 g ±2 g, regular, opened cuts; rubric completed honestly | No weights or rubric |
| Safety | Steam, oven and blade handled as in the warning | Glass dish, water on the door, blade towards the hand |

The Academy cannot grade your bread or your photos. Compare your work honestly with this rubric and the reference answers below; this self-assessment does not replace the official practical exam.

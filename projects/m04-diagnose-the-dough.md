# Project: Diagnose the Dough

This project brings module 4 together. You first diagnose three real-looking fermentation problems from a bakery's records, then you make a reference dough and a deliberately faulty dough at home, follow the faulty one both "by the clock" and "by the signs", and write the diagnoses, a non-conformity report and a corrected production sheet. The aim is the habit every professional needs: judge the dough, not the timer, and turn every fault into a record that prevents the next one.

## Brief

You are the ouvrier boulanger at Boulangerie du Marché. The owner leaves you this note on Saturday:

> **Note de la patronne — semaine difficile**
>
> Trois problèmes cette semaine, je veux comprendre avant lundi. Les fiches de production sont dans le classeur (relevés ci-dessous).
> Pour chacun : ce que tu vois, la cause probable, ce qu'il fallait faire sur le moment, et comment on évite que ça recommence.
> Et refais l'essai chez toi : une pâte normale et une pâte « à problème », pour montrer aux apprentis la différence entre suivre la fiche à la minute et regarder la pâte.

In English: three problems this week; for each one, what you see, the probable cause, what should have been done at the time and how to prevent it. Then repeat the test at home with a normal dough and a "problem" dough, to show the apprentices the difference between following the sheet to the minute and watching the dough.

### The three records

| | Case 1 — Monday, tradition on poolish | Case 2 — Wednesday, PC-02 pain courant | Case 3 — Friday, pain de mie (direct) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sheet says | poolish 100/100, fresh yeast 0.1 % on its flour, made 18:00 at 20 °C for 6:00 | TPV 24 °C, pointage 45 min, apprêt 1 h 15 at 25 °C | TPV 25 °C, pointage 30 min, apprêt 1 h 30 at 28 °C, salt 1.8 % |
| Record shows | poolish made 15:00, yeast 0.5 %, fournil 26 °C | water 12 °C (new cold water tank), dough 20 °C; times as the sheet | dough 25 °C; salt line on the weighing sheet not ticked; dough "very soft" at dividing |
| Product | dough sticky and weak; baguettes flat, pale, sour | baguettes small, dark reddish crust, burst along the side, tight crumb | rose fast, loaves slack, bland taste |

<details><summary>Reference diagnoses (open after you have written yours)</summary>

1. **Case 1: over-ripe poolish.** Made 3 hours early, five times the yeast, 6 °C warmer: by 6:00 it had collapsed and turned acid, weakening the dough and using up sugars (flat, pale, sour). At the time: use less of it, mix cooler, shorten pointage, firm shaping. Prevention: respect the time, yeast and temperature on the sheet; write the poolish state (domed, bubbles, smell) before use.
2. **Case 2: under-fermented by a cold dough.** 4 °C under target is about 1.07⁴ ≈ 1.3 times slower; following the sheet's times left the dough young. Pointage should have been about 45 × 1.3 ≈ 60 min; apprêt up to about 75 × 1.3 ≈ 100 min, in practice somewhat less because the pieces warm up in the 25 °C cabinet, so the poke test decides. At the time: longer pointage and apprêt in a warmer place, poke test before loading. Prevention: recalculate the water temperature when the water supply changes ([Module 5](../lessons/module-05/lesson-02.md)); always compare dough temperature with the TPV and adapt.
3. **Case 3: salt forgotten.** Without salt the yeast rushes, the gluten is weak and the bread tastes flat. At the time: if noticed before dividing, salt can sometimes be worked in; otherwise the batch is reported. Prevention: tick every ingredient at weighing; weigh salt and yeast in separate containers.

</details>

## You need

- For Part B (home bake): scale (1 g, ideally 0.1 g), probe thermometer, two bowls, scraper, two straight-sided clear containers with lids, tape and ruler, baking paper, trays, a metal steam tray, oven gloves, lame, bread knife.
- In Israel: in summer the reference dough needs fridge-blended water and the coolest room; see the In Israel notes of lessons [04.2](../lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md) and [04.7](../lessons/module-04/lesson-07.md).
- Templates: [production sheet](../templates/production-sheet.md), [temperature log](../templates/temperature-log.md), [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md), [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md), [non-conformity report](../templates/non-conformity-report.md).
- Time: about 45 minutes for Part A; about 5 hours for Part B (mostly waiting); about 45 minutes to write Part C.

> [!WARNING]
> You bake at 240 °C with steam, several loads in a row. Use dry oven gloves; make steam only in a preheated metal tray (never a glass dish), pour and step back. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers and cover the lame after use. Water for the warm dough must not be above 40 °C.

## Steps

### Part A — diagnose the records (about 45 minutes)

1. For each case, write four lines: **what you see → probable cause (with the evidence from the record) → what to do at the time → prevention**. Use lessons [04.5](../lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md) and [04.7](../lessons/module-04/lesson-07.md).
2. For case 2, calculate how much slower the dough fermented with the 7 %-per-degree rule of thumb, and how long pointage and apprêt should have been.
3. Only then open the reference diagnoses and mark your own.

### Part B — reference dough and problem dough (about 5 hours)

4. **Choose one fault** for your problem dough and write it on a production sheet:
   - **Warm:** water warmer so the dough is about 28 °C (4 °C over target), or
   - **Too much yeast:** fresh yeast 3 % instead of 1.5 % (dough at 24 °C), or
   - **Cold:** water colder so the dough is about 20 °C (4 °C under target).
5. **Reference dough R:** PD-01 (flour 500 g, water 325 g, salt 9 g, fresh yeast 7.5 g), dough at 24 °C ± 1 °C. **Problem dough P:** the same formula with your fault. Mix R first, P 20 minutes later. Record each dough temperature in the temperature log.
6. Put each in a marked container. Record height every 15 minutes. Give R its pointage judged by the signs (lesson [04.2](../lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md)), with a fold at 40 minutes.
7. **Split P after mixing** into two equal halves, P-clock and P-signs, in two marked containers side by side. **P-clock** follows the PD-01 sheet to the minute: pointage 1 h 15, détente 15 min, apprêt 40 min, whatever it looks like. **P-signs** is adapted: you decide each stage by volume, feel and poke test, and write down every time and the reason.
8. Divide R into two pieces of 410 g; P-clock and P-signs are each one piece of about 410 g. Pre-shape, détente, shape bâtards of about 25 cm (lesson [04.3](../lessons/module-04/lesson-03.md)).
9. Before each bake, poke-test and write the result (lesson [04.4](../lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md)). Bake at 240 °C with steam, about 25 minutes, in the order the pieces are ready.
10. Cool 1 hour. Measure height and width, cut each lengthwise, photograph the crumbs side by side, taste. Score each with the quality rubric.

### Part C — the write-up (about 45 minutes)

11. Write a diagnosis for P-clock and P-signs: symptoms, fermentation state, evidence (temperatures, times, poke results), compared with R.
12. Fill in a non-conformity report for P-clock as if it were a production batch of 30 bâtards: facts and measurements, immediate action, probable cause, prevention.
13. Write a corrected production sheet for the fault you chose: either the corrected input (water temperature, yeast) or, if the fault cannot be avoided, the adapted times and the signs to check.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Record diagnoses (Part A) | Each case names the cause from the evidence in the record and gives an action at the time and a prevention; case 2 calculation within 5 minutes of the reference | Causes guessed from the symptom only, or no prevention |
| Measurements | Dough temperatures, heights every 15 min, times and poke results recorded for all three doughs | Missing temperatures, times or poke results |
| Fault control | Fault applied as planned (temperature within ±1 °C, or yeast weighed to 0.1 g); R on target | Fault and reference not clearly different, or R off target |
| Judging by the signs | P-signs decisions written with the sign that triggered them; P-signs visibly better than P-clock | Decisions not written, or taken by the clock |
| Bread evaluation | Volume, scoring, crust, crumb and taste compared across the three loaves with the rubric | Loaves described vaguely ("not great") |
| Report and corrected sheet | Non-conformity report complete (facts, action, cause, prevention); corrected sheet changes the right input | Report blames or is incomplete; sheet unchanged or changes the wrong input |
| Safety and hygiene | Oven, steam and lame used safely; clean station; dough covered at every rest | Unsafe steam method; dough left uncovered |

Remember: the Academy cannot grade your bread. Compare your results honestly with these criteria and the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md); self-assessment helps you learn but does not replace the official practical exam.

# Project: Job-Ready File

A baker who arrives at an interview knowing the business, with a clean CV, and who can read the contract and the first payslip without help, starts the job on equal terms. In this project you build that file for a realistic job offer: you profile the employer, apply, check the contract and the first payslip against the law and the convention collective, write yourself a one-page rights card, and read the bakery's monthly figures as an owner would. The Academy cannot grade your file: compare your work honestly with the rubric and the reference answers; this self-assessment does not replace the official exam, and the course does not award the CAP. Legal figures were checked on 2026-10-09; check them again on the day you use the file.

## Brief

> **Boulangerie Les Blés d'Or** — SAS au capital de 20 000 € — président: M. Garnier — code APE 10.71C — Tours (37) — 9 salariés: 3 ouvriers boulangers, 1 tourier, 1 pâtissière, 1 apprentie, 3 vendeuses. Activités: pains (tradition, levain), viennoiseries, pâtisseries; sandwiches et quiches chaudes à emporter le midi.
>
> **Offre d'emploi (France Travail, 5 octobre):** Ouvrier boulanger H/F, CDI. CAP Boulanger exigé. 39 h de travail effectif par semaine, du mercredi au dimanche: 3 h 00 – 11 h 00 du mercredi au samedi, 3 h 00 – 10 h 00 le dimanche (pauses non comprises). Pain courant, tradition, levain; participation au tourage. Candidature: CV + lettre.
>
> **Extrait du contrat proposé:**

Convention collective: boulangerie-pâtisserie (entreprises artisanales). Les heures effectuées entre 5 h et 6 h ne donnent pas lieu à majoration de nuit. Le travail du dimanche est rémunéré au taux normal, la rémunération ayant été fixée en conséquence."

>
> **Chiffres d'octobre de la boulangerie (€ HT):** chiffre d'affaires 52 000; matières premières et emballages 15 600; énergie 14 000 kWh à 0,20 €; loyer 2 600; autres charges externes 2 000; salaires bruts 18 500; cotisations patronales 5 000; impôts et taxes 250; amortissements 2 000; intérêts 400.

> **Nouveaux produits à chiffrer:** pain au levain 500 g, coût de revient 1,95 €; quiche individuelle chaude à emporter, coût de revient 2,40 €. Marge visée: 25 % du prix HT. Prix arrondis aux 0,05 € supérieurs.

In English: a craft bakery (SAS, 9 employees) advertises a CDI for an ouvrier boulanger with nights from 3:00 and Sunday work. It sends a contract extract with several clauses to check, then a first payslip; you also receive its October figures and two products to price. You are the candidate, a CAP holder.

## You need

- Lessons [20.1](../lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md) to [20.5](../lessons/module-20/lesson-05.md) and [06.5](../lessons/module-06/lesson-05.md); a calculator or spreadsheet; a word processor for the CV and letter.
- The official pages cited in 20.3 and 20.4 (service-public, Légifrance convention collective), to re-check the SMIC and the grid on the day you work.
- For Part B, someone to play the employer (in person or by video call) and a phone to record yourself.
- Time: about 5-6 hours over a week (Part A 45 min, Part B 2 h, Part C 1 h 30, Part D 45 min, Part E 1 h).

## Steps

### Part A — know the employer

1. From the identity card: legal form and what it means (owners, liability, who runs it, how profit is taxed); main and secondary activities; the VAT rates the bakery will use; whether the bakery must have a CSE.
2. List the jobs in the team and where you would fit; name two outside bodies you could turn to about your working conditions.

### Part B — apply

3. Write a one-page CV in French for this offer (lesson 20.3), with facts and numbers from your practice and any work. If your experience is from Israel, translate job titles into the French trade terms and give dates and contact details in the French way.
4. Write a cover letter of 200-250 words in three paragraphs (vous / moi / nous), naming the bakery, its products and the offer.
5. Prepare and rehearse the interview: a 2-minute presentation, answers to six likely questions, and three questions of your own about hours, coefficient and training. Record one rehearsal and note what to improve.

### Part C — check the contract and the first payslip

6. Check each clause of the contract extract: legal or not, which text decides (law, convention collective, contract), and what you would ask to change. Write the corrected clauses.
7. From the offer's timetable, calculate per week and per month (× 4.333): overtime hours, night hours between 20:00 and 6:00, Sunday hours. Say whether you would be a night worker under the convention, and how many extra rest days a year that brings (about 45 working weeks).
8. Recalculate the first payslip with the right coefficient and every premium due. Find the difference with the payslip received.
9. Write the short, polite message you would send to the employer about the payslip (in French, 5-8 lines), with the figures.

### Part D — your rights card

10. On one page, write the rules you need on the job, each with its figure, its source and the date you checked it: legal week and maximums; overtime rates; night period, premium and night-worker status; Sunday and holiday premiums; SMIC; your coefficient and its minimum; paid leave; trial period; minors' night rule (for the apprentice beside you); the three end-of-contract documents; who to contact (inspection du travail, prud'hommes, occupational health).

### Part E — read the business

11. From the October figures: energy cost, intermediate consumption, value added, staff costs, result. Say who receives the value added (in € and %).
12. Price the two new products: HT with a 25 % margin on HT, the right VAT rate, TTC rounded to the next €0.05.
13. If October's result were repeated every month for a year, what corporate tax would the SAS pay (small-company conditions met)?
14. Write three lines: what these figures mean for you as an employee (waste, energy, punctuality, accuracy of weights).

## Deliverables

- Part A: a half-page business profile.
- Part B: CV, cover letter, interview notes and a note on your recorded rehearsal.
- Part C: the clause-by-clause check with corrected clauses; the hours calculation; the corrected payslip and the difference; the message to the employer.
- Part D: the one-page rights card with sources and dates.
- Part E: the monthly figures, the value-added split, the two prices, the corporate tax and your three lines.

<details><summary>Reference answers for Parts A, C and E</summary>

**Part A.** SAS: at least two partners (an SASU has one), liability limited to contributions (€20,000 capital), run by a président (M. Garnier), profit taxed at corporate tax (IS). APE 10.71C: craft bread and pastry making with retail sale is the main activity; sandwiches and hot quiches to take away are a secondary activity at **10 %** VAT; bread, viennoiserie and pastries at **5.5 %**. With 9 employees the bakery is **under 11**: no CSE is required. Outside bodies: the inspection du travail, the occupational health service, a union; in a dispute, the conseil de prud'hommes.

**Part C — clauses.**
- "Coefficient 155, €12.41": a CAP holder is placed at **coefficient 160** (article 9), minimum **€12.53** since 1 January 2026, above the SMIC of €12.31. Ask for 160 at €12.53.
- "Période d'essai 3 mois, renouvelable": for ouvriers the law caps the initial trial period at **2 months** (renewal only if a branch agreement allows it), and the bakery convention sets **30 days**. Ask for 30 days.
- "Hours 5:00-6:00 without night premium": the convention gives **+25 % for every hour between 20:00 and 6:00**; a contract cannot give less. Invalid.
- "Sunday at the normal rate": the convention gives **+20 %** for Sunday work. Invalid for the same reason.

**Part C — hours.** Per week: 4 × 8 h + 7 h = 39 h: **4 h overtime** at +25 %. Night: 3:00-6:00 = 3 h × 5 days = **15 h**. Sunday: **7 h**. Per month: overtime 17.33 h; night 65.00 h; Sunday 30.33 h. Night worker: yes (at least 3 night hours on at least 2 days a week); 15 h × 45 weeks ≈ 675 night hours a year, above 600: **2 extra rest days**. Before starting, an information and prevention visit with occupational health.

**Part C — payslip.**

| Overtime 17.33 h × 15.6625 | 271.43 | 268.79 |

| Sunday 30.33 h × 2.506 | 76.01 | 0.00 |
| **Gross** | **2,451.48** | **2,285.33** |

Difference: **€166.15** gross for the month.

**Part E.** Energy 14,000 × 0.20 = **€2,800**. Intermediate consumption 15,600 + 2,800 + 2,600 + 2,000 = **€23,000**. Value added 52,000 − 23,000 = **€29,000**. Staff costs 18,500 + 5,000 = €23,500. All charges 23,000 + 23,500 + 250 + 2,000 + 400 = €49,150; **result €2,850**. Split of VA: staff and social bodies €23,500 (81 %), State €250 (1 %), bank €400 (1 %), business 2,000 + 2,850 = €4,850 (17 %). Prices: levain 1.95 ÷ 0.75 = €2.60 HT × 1.055 = €2.743 → **€2.75**; hot quiche (food for immediate consumption) 2.40 ÷ 0.75 = €3.20 HT × 1.10 = €3.52 → **€3.55**. Year: 2,850 × 12 = €34,200, under €42,500: IS 15 % = **€5,130**. For the employee: a result of about 5 % of turnover means waste, overweight pâtons and energy left on decide whether the business is profitable.

# Project: Equipment File

Every bakery keeps, or should keep, an equipment file: for each machine, what it is for, how it is set for the house products, how it is used and cleaned safely, how it is checked and what it costs to run. In Part A you build that file for a small bakery's Saturday from its equipment list, rating plates and production order, with the calculations behind each decision. In Part B you build the same file for your own kitchen from the cards you made in this module, and run a full test bake that uses every card. The Academy cannot grade your file or your bread: compare your work honestly with the rubric and the reference answers; this self-assessment does not replace the official practical exam.

## Brief

> **Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle — Dossier matériel et commande du samedi**
>
> | Matériel | Plaque / fiche technique |
> |---|---|
> | Pétrin à spirale | 400 V 3~ 50 Hz; charge maximale 30 kg de pâte |
> | Diviseuse hydraulique | 20 pièces |
> | Laminoir | 230 V ~ 50 Hz 550 W; protecteurs à arrêt; arrêt d'urgence |
> | Armoire de pousse contrôlée | 230 V ~ 50 Hz 2 300 W |
> | Batteur 20 L | 230 V ~ 50 Hz 1 840 W, classe I |
> | Chambre froide positive | consigne +3 °C |
> | Four à soles électrique, 3 étages, 5 m² | 400 V 3~ 50 Hz; générateur de buée par étage |
> | Four ventilé 10 plaques | 400 V 3~ 50 Hz |
>
> Commande: 160 baguettes PC-02, pâton 350 g (farine 100 %, eau 64 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,5 %, pâte fermentée 15 % = 182,3 %); 60 croissants CR-01 (tourage la veille, apprêt samedi en armoire).
> Relevés samedi 3:30: farine 19 °C, fournil 21 °C, pâte fermentée sortie de chambre froide 6 °C. Carte de frottement: "PC-02, amélioré, 28 kg: FF 26 (4 facteurs)". TPV 24 °C.
> Four: premier enfournement 5:30; temps réel de chauffe (carte du four) 1 h 30; jusqu'ici le four est allumé à 2:00. Prix de l'électricité environ 0,21 € le kWh.
> Les prises du fournil sont sur des circuits de 16 A.

In English: the bakery's equipment with its rating plates; Saturday's order (160 PC-02 baguettes of 350 g, 60 croissants proofed in the cabinet); the morning temperature readings and the friction-factor card; the oven's real preheat time and the habit of switching it on at 2:00; the electricity price; 16 A socket circuits.

## You need

- Part A: calculator; the [production sheet template](../templates/production-sheet.md) for the baguette dough; the [temperature log](../templates/temperature-log.md); the [non-conformity report](../templates/non-conformity-report.md); one operating manual of a bakery sheeter or mixer found online; paper for the machine cards.
- Part B: the cards and logs from lessons [13.1](../lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md) to [13.6](../lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md); ingredients for 1 kg of PD-01; your home baking setup (scale, probe and oven thermometers, proofing box, steam tray or cover, lame, rack); the [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md) and the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md).
- Time: Part A about 2-3 hours; Part B one baking day (about 5 hours, 1 h 30 of it hands-on) plus the clean-down.

> [!WARNING]
> Part B uses a 240 °C oven, steam, a lame and possibly a stand mixer. Steam only with a preheated metal tray and a long dry glove, face turned away, door shut at once (lesson [07.4](../lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)); never a hand in a turning mixer bowl; lame covered when not in your hand. Do not open or modify any electrical or gas appliance while you inspect them: read the plates only.

## Steps

### Part A — the bakery's equipment file

1. **Mixer.** Dough for the baguettes; number and size of batches; flour and every ingredient for one batch; water temperature for one batch (4 factors). Which friction factor applies, and why.
2. **Divider.** Load for one division; number of divisions; how and when the pieces are checked.
3. **Sheeter.** A gap plan for the final sheeting of the CR-01 block to about 3.5 mm, and a one-page **fiche de poste** for the sheeter from a real manual (as in lesson [13.2](../lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)), every line traced to a page.
4. **Cabinet and cold.** A settings table for: the croissant apprêt in the armoire, the cold room, and what the baker checks to know each is really at its setting.
5. **Oven.** Which product bakes where and in what order (deck, convection oven, temperatures, steam on or off per deck); when the preheat should start; the energy and money saved per year by starting at the right time instead of 2:00 (use the CNBPF figure of about 12 kWh a day for 2 hours of empty running on a 5 m² deck, about 3,300 kWh a year).
6. **Electricity.** The current of each 230 V appliance; whether the armoire and the batteur may share one 16 A circuit; why you do not calculate the three-phase machines with P = U × I but read their current on the plate.
7. **Cleaning and maintenance.** A weekly plan: which machine or tool, how (state of the machine, dry before wet, what is never used), and which check (scale, probes, oven thermometer, guards) on which day.
8. **Three incidents.** Write the response, in order, to: a gas smell in the back room (if the bakery had a gas appliance); the mixer cable cut by a trolley; a colleague who stays in the cold room for 40 minutes to tidy it in a T-shirt. Fill in one non-conformity report for one of them.

### Part B — your home equipment file and test bake

9. **Assemble your file**: the friction factors from [13.1](../lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md); your sheeting drill results and sheeter fiche de poste from [13.2](../lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md); your proofing box card from [13.3](../lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md); your oven card from [13.4](../lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md); your safety card from [13.5](../lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md); your care sheet and maintenance log from [13.6](../lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md).
10. **Maintenance check before baking**: scale and probe checked and logged.
11. **Bake 1 kg of PD-01** as 6 bâtards of 270 g using only your cards: water temperature from your own friction factor; pointage and apprêt in your proofing box (its card says how); oven preheated to its real temperature by your oven card; loaves placed away from your hot zones; your best steam method.
12. **Log everything** in the bake log: every temperature against its target, times, weights, the oven thermometer reading at loading.
13. **Clean down** with your care sheet and time it.
14. **Evaluate** the bâtards with the quality rubric and write three lines: which card was right, which one needs correcting, and what you change.

## Deliverables

- Part A: calculations for steps 1, 2, 5 and 6; the production sheet for one baguette batch; the gap plan; the sheeter fiche de poste with page references; the settings table; the oven plan; the weekly cleaning and maintenance plan; the three incident responses and one non-conformity report.
- Part B: your home equipment file (six cards and the maintenance log), the bake log of the test day, the timed clean-down, the rubric scores and your three lines.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Mixer batches | Every batch at or under 30 kg; friction factor tied to the batch size; water from 4 factors | One 56 kg batch; full-bowl factor reused without a word; 3-factor formula with a pre-ferment |
| Divider | Load = piece weight × 20; number of divisions right; pieces checked on the scale | Baking loss taken off the load; no piece check |
| Sheeter fiche de poste | Guards, STOP, never under a guard, never bypass, unplug to clean, no compressed air or hose, faulty-guard procedure; every line traced to the manual | Generic advice with no source; faulty guard "used carefully" |
| Cabinet and cold | Croissants 24-26 °C, below about 27 °C, 75-80 %; cold room about 0-4 °C; checked with a probe, not only the display | Croissants at bread temperature; display trusted alone |
| Oven and energy | Hottest to coolest; steam off where not needed; preheat from the real time; saving calculated from kWh × price | Preheat at 2:00 kept; price applied to one day only or with a misplaced decimal |
| Electricity | I = P ÷ U for 230 V appliances; 10 + 8 = 18 A > 16 A, so separate circuits; three-phase read on the plate | Currents divided the wrong way; appliances chained on one circuit |
| Safety responses | Gas: valves, no switches or phone, out, alert from outside; cable: stop, label, report; cold room: time limited, warm clothing, release and alarm | Light switched on to look; cable taped; cold-room risk ignored |
| Home file and test bake | All six cards used; dough within 1 °C of target; oven at its real temperature at loading; rubric filled; three lines specific | Cards made but not used; temperatures not measured; "it went fine" |

<details><summary>Reference answers for Part A</summary>

1. **Mixer.** 160 × 350 g = 56,000 g of dough, too much for one 30 kg bowl: **two batches of 28 kg**. Flour per batch 28,000 ÷ 1.823 = 15,359 g → 15.4 kg; water 64 % = 9,856 g; salt 277 g; fresh yeast 231 g; pâte fermentée 2,310 g (check: 15,400 × 1.823 = 28,074 g). The card's FF 26 was measured for 28 kg, so it applies. Water = 4 × 24 − 19 − 21 − 6 − 26 = **24 °C**.
2. **Divider.** 20 × 350 g = **7,000 g** per division; 56,000 ÷ 7,000 = **8 divisions** (4 per batch). Spread each load evenly; check several pieces of every division on the scale and adjust the next load.
3. **Sheeter.** For example 20 → 14 → 10 → 7 → 5 → 3.5 mm, with a quarter turn once to keep the sheet square; never one jump from 20 to 7 mm. The fiche de poste lists the manual's rules: trained users only, close-fitting clothes, guards down and tested before starting, STOP before reaching in, never reach under a closed guard, safety elements never adjusted or bridged, plug pulled before cleaning or service, no compressed air, hose or high-pressure cleaner, a faulty guard puts the machine out of service with a written report.
4. **Settings.** Croissant apprêt 24-26 °C, never above about 27 °C, 75-80 % humidity, judged by the poke test; cold room about 0-4 °C (+3 °C set point). Each checked with a probe in a glass of water (or the cold-room thermometer) and written in the temperature log, not read from the display only.
5. **Oven.** Baguettes on the decks at 250 °C with steam, loaded from 5:30; croissants in the convection oven at about 175-180 °C, no steam; steam generators off on any deck not baking bread; decks not used later switched off. Preheat starts at **4:00** (5:30 − 1 h 30), not 2:00: 2 hours of empty running saved, about 12 kWh a day, about 3,300 kWh a year; 3,300 × 0.21 ≈ **690 € a year**.
6. **Electricity.** Armoire 2,300 ÷ 230 = **10 A**; batteur 1,840 ÷ 230 = **8 A**; laminoir 550 ÷ 230 ≈ **2.4 A**. Armoire and batteur together draw 18 A, more than a 16 A circuit: separate circuits. The spiral mixer and the ovens are three-phase (400 V 3~), where P = U × I does not apply as written; their current is read on the plate or given by the electrician.
7. **Weekly plan (example).** Daily: mixer, divider, sheeter stopped and unplugged, scraped and vacuumed dry, wiped as the manuals allow; floors vacuumed then washed; bannetons and couches shaken and dried. Before each session: guard and emergency-stop test on the sheeter and mixer. Weekly: scale with a known weight, probes in iced water. Monthly: oven thermometer against each deck's display; steam generators descaled as the maker says; condenser of the armoire and cold room checked (by the technician).
8. **Incidents.** Gas smell: close the valve if you can, touch no switch, no flame, no phone inside, open up on the way out, everyone out, alert the gas distributor or emergency services from outside, nobody back in until a professional has checked. Mixer cable: do not use or tape it; unplug by the plug with dry hands; label "out of service"; report; electrician repairs; use the other mixer. Cold room: limit time inside, warm clothes and gloves for longer jobs, check the inside release and alarm work, never block or lock the door with someone inside.

</details>

# Project: Mixing Study: One Dough, Three Ways

This project brings module 3 together. You make the same lean dough three ways (slow, improved, and autolyse followed by improved mixing), measure what each method does to development, temperature, handling and the bread, correct the consistency on the way, and write a short technical note that tells a bakery which mixing line to put on its sheets for pain courant and for tradition.

## Brief

You are the ouvrier boulanger at Boulangerie du Marché. The head baker wants to stop the white, bland baguettes a former employee used to make and leaves you this note:

> **Note du chef — essai de pétrissage**
>
> Même pâte, trois pétrissages : lent, amélioré, autolyse + amélioré. Formule PD-01 : farine T55 100 %, eau 65 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,5 % (à adapter si le pointage change). Pâte à 24 °C.
> Garde 2 % d'eau en réserve sur chaque pâte et ajoute-la en bassinage seulement si la pâte est ferme.
> Note pour chaque pâte : temps de pétrissage, test de la fenêtre, température, couleur, comportement au façonnage, volume, mie, goût, et le lendemain.
> Dis-moi quelle ligne de pétrissage tu mettrais sur la fiche pain courant et sur la fiche tradition, et pourquoi.

In English: the same dough mixed three ways; keep 2 % of the water in reserve and add it by bassinage only if needed; record everything; recommend a mixing line for the pain courant and tradition sheets.

At home you mix by hand, which simulates the methods (lesson [03.3](../lessons/module-03/lesson-03.md)). If you own a stand mixer you may add a fourth dough mixed intensively, for comparison. In the CAP practical test mixing is done by machine ([exam reference](../references/cap-exam.md)), so note in your report what you would expect on a spiral mixer.

## You need

- Flour T55 (or plain/all-purpose), about 1.1 kg; salt; fresh yeast (or instant dry, one-third of the weight).
- In Israel: white flour as the T55 ([Flour in Israel](../references/flour-in-israel.md)); in a warm kitchen follow the In Israel notes of lessons [03.3](../lessons/module-03/lesson-03.md) and [03.4](../lessons/module-03/lesson-04.md) for the water and the long pointage.
- Scale (1 g, and 0.1 g for salt and yeast if you have one), probe thermometer, three bowls, three identical clear containers with the level marked, scraper, timer, ruler, baking trays, metal tray for steam, oven gloves, lame or sharp knife, a phone camera.
- [Production sheet](../templates/production-sheet.md) (one per dough), [temperature log](../templates/temperature-log.md), [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md), [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md), [non-conformity report](../templates/non-conformity-report.md) if anything goes wrong.
- Time: about 5 hours on the day (about 1 h 30 hands-on), plus 15 minutes the next day and 30 minutes to write the note.

> [!WARNING]
> You bake at 240 °C with steam: use dry oven gloves, pour water only into a preheated metal tray on the lowest shelf, close the door at once and stand back; never pour water into a hot glass dish. If you use a stand mixer, never put a hand or scraper in the bowl while the hook turns, and stop if the motor gets hot.

## Steps

### Part A: plan (about 20 minutes)

1. Fill one production sheet per dough on 350 g of flour. Water 65 % = 227.5 g, of which 7 g (2 % of the flour) in reserve; salt 6.3 g. Yeast: 1.5 % (5.3 g) for doughs B and C; 1.0 % (3.5 g) for dough A, because its pointage is twice as long.
2. Write each mixing line:
   - **A, slow:** frasage 4 min, gentle kneading 4 min (lift and fold, no slapping); pointage 2 h with folds at 30, 60 and 90 min.
   - **B, improved:** frasage 4 min, vigorous kneading (slap and fold) to the windowpane; pointage 1 h, one fold at 30 min.
   - **C, autolyse + improved:** flour and water (minus the reserve) 2 min, rest 30 min covered; add yeast, then salt; vigorous kneading to the windowpane; pointage 1 h, one fold at 30 min.
3. Calculate the water temperature for a 24 °C dough with the hand-mix estimate from lesson [01.7](../lessons/module-01/lesson-07.md); use water about 2 °C warmer for dough C (less kneading, less heat). Draw a timeline so the three doughs are divided within about 15 minutes of each other: start A first, C about 35 minutes later, B about 55 minutes after A.

### Part B: mix, ferment, bake (about 4 hours 30)

4. Mix each dough as planned. At the end of frasage judge consistency; add the reserve water by bassinage only if the dough is firm, and record how much (lesson [03.5](../lessons/module-03/lesson-05.md)).
5. For B and C, do the windowpane test every 2 minutes and record the minute it passes; for A, record the windowpane at the end of mixing and again before dividing (lesson [03.1](../lessons/module-03/lesson-01.md)).
6. Record the dough temperature of each at the end of mixing and its colour (creamy or whiter) next to a pinch of plain flour-and-water paste (lesson [03.4](../lessons/module-03/lesson-04.md)).
7. Ferment in the marked containers. Record the height every 30 minutes and the rise at the end of pointage (× 1.5, × 2…).
8. Divide each dough into two pieces of 280 g ± 5 g, pre-shape, rest 15 minutes, shape into bâtards of about 22 cm. Note how each one lengthens: tears, springs back, or stretches easily.
9. Proof until a finger press springs back slowly; record each apprêt time. Preheat the oven to 240 °C with the steam tray for at least 45 minutes.
10. Score each loaf once and bake 24–27 minutes with steam; bake the three doughs in the same load if your oven allows (label the paper).
11. Cool at least 1 hour. Weigh, measure height and width, cut one loaf of each, photograph the crumb next to a ruler. Taste, blind if someone can label the slices.
12. Keep the second loaf of each in a bag; taste and press the crumb again the next day.

### Part C: technical note (about 30 minutes)

13. Fill in the results table below and write the note to the head baker.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Three sheets correct to the gram; yeast adjusted for the long pointage; timeline lets the doughs reach the oven together | Same yeast for all, or doughs ready hours apart |
| Measurement | Kneading times, windowpane results, temperatures (to 0.5 °C), heights and weights all recorded | Estimated or missing values |
| Temperature control | All doughs 23–25 °C at the end of mixing, or deviations corrected through fermentation time and explained | A dough far off target with no correction or explanation |
| Consistency correction | Reserve water added only when needed, amount recorded, final hydration calculated | Water added by guesswork or not recorded |
| Analysis | Differences in colour, volume, crumb, taste and keeping linked to development and oxidation | Differences described without causes |
| Recommendation | A clear mixing line for each sheet with numbers and reasons a professional would accept | Vague advice ("C is best") without a method or times |
| Safety and hygiene | Oven and steam handled safely; clean bench; flour dust wiped damp, not swept dry | Unsafe steam method or dusty work area |

Remember: the Academy cannot grade your bread. Compare your results honestly with these criteria and the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md); self-assessment helps you learn but does not replace the official practical exam.

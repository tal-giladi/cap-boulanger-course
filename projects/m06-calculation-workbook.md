# Project: Calculation Workbook

This project brings module 6 together. You turn a realistic Saturday order for three doughs into complete technical sheets, deal with a stock shortage, split a poolish and a levain formula, work back from baked weights, check salt and the levain yeast rule, cost a baguette, and then carry out a small home order on poolish whose pâtons you calculate from your own measured baking loss.

## Brief

You are the ouvrier boulanger at Boulangerie Au Pain de la Halle. On Friday afternoon the chef leaves you this order for Saturday morning.

> **Commande de production — samedi 28 novembre 2026**
>
> | Produit | Quantité | Poids | Fiche |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Baguette (pain courant) | 48 | pâton 350 g | PC-02 |
> | Petit pain rond (pain courant) | 40 | pâton 60 g | PC-02 |
> | Baguette sur poolish | 24 | pâton 350 g | PO-01 |
> | Pain de campagne (boule) | 10 | **poids cuit 500 g** | CA-01 |
>
> PC-02: farine T55 100 %, eau 64 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,5 %, pâte fermentée 15 %.
> PO-01 (formule globale): farine T65 100 %, eau 68 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 0,8 % au total; 30 % de la farine en poolish (eau 100 %, levure 0,2 % de la farine du poolish).
> CA-01 (formule globale): farine T65 85 %, farine de seigle T130 15 %, eau 70 %, sel 1,8 %; 20 % de la farine en levain liquide (100 %) à la T65; perte à la cuisson mesurée 15 %. Levain de tout point: ensemencement 20 %, marge 60 g.
>
> Pertes de fabrication: 2 %. Arrondir la farine à la centaine de grammes supérieure, les pâtons aux 5 g supérieurs.
> Stock: pâte fermentée au froid **1 500 g** seulement. Pétrin à spirale: 25 kg de pâte maximum.
> Prix d'achat HT (exemple): T55 21,50 € le sac de 25 kg; sel 15,00 € les 25 kg; levure 1,70 € les 500 g; eau 4,20 € le m³. Coûts par baguette PC-02 (comptabilité, exemple): main-d'œuvre 0,34 €, énergie 0,06 €, frais généraux 0,31 €. Prix affiché baguette: 1,35 € TTC.

Part A is the paperwork for this order. Part B is a home order you calculate, bake and cost:

> **Commande maison:** 3 bâtards sur poolish, **poids cuit 300 g**. Formule globale: farine T55 100 %, eau 66 %, sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,0 %; 30 % de la farine en poolish (100 %, levure 0,2 %). Pertes 2 %. Arrondir la farine aux 10 g supérieurs.

## You need

- Calculator, paper or a spreadsheet; the [production sheet template](../templates/production-sheet.md) (one per dough), the [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md) and the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md).
- For Part B: the equipment of lesson [06.3](../lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)'s practice and the poolish container of lesson [06.4](../lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md); your measured baking loss from [06.3](../lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md) (if you have none yet, use 18 % and measure it in this bake); your shopping receipt and electricity price.

> [!WARNING]
> Part B uses a 240 °C oven and steam. Dry oven gloves, steam only in a preheated metal tray (never glass), pour and step back. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers and cover the lame after use.

## Steps

### Part A — the bakery order (about 90 minutes)

1. **Dough requirements.** For each dough, calculate pieces × pâton weight. For the campagne, first work back from the 500 g baked weight to the pâton.
2. **PC-02 with the stock shortage.** Add 2 % losses. Check whether 1,500 g of pâte fermentée covers 15 %. If not, recalculate the flour with the pâte fermentée fixed in grams, round up, and give every ingredient, its real % and the total. Write the one-line note for the chef.
3. **Capacity.** Check each dough against the 25 kg mixer.
4. **PO-01.** From the overall formula, calculate the overall batch, then the poolish and the final dough. Check that they add up.
5. **CA-01.** Calculate the overall batch, the levain (flour and water), the final dough, and the levain de tout point to build tonight (seed, flour, water, with the 60 g margin).
6. **Levain rule.** If the chef later wanted to sell the campagne "au levain" with a little yeast, what is the most fresh yeast allowed at final mixing?
7. **Hidden flour and hydration.** How much flour is inside the 1,500 g of pâte fermentée? What is PC-02's true hydration?
8. **Salt.** Check salt per 100 g of bread for the PC-02 rolls with a measured baking loss of 22.7 %, and for the PO-01 baguettes with 20 %.
9. **Requisition.** Total flour by type (overall formulas), total salt, total fresh yeast for the three doughs.
10. **Cost.** Material cost of the PC-02 batch (pâte fermentée at €0.553/kg, lesson [06.5](../lessons/module-06/lesson-05.md)), cost of one baguette's materials (divide by the dough that becomes products), full cost, price HT, VAT and margin.

### Part B — the home order (about 16 hours, of which 2 hours hands-on)

11. Work back from 300 g baked to the pâton with your measured loss; round up to the next 5 g. Calculate the dough with 2 % losses, the flour (rounded up to the next 10 g), the poolish and the final dough.
12. Make the poolish in the evening (lesson [04.5](../lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md)'s method and yeast solution; in a warm kitchen use the In Israel notes of lesson [06.4](../lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md)).
13. Next morning mix the final dough (target 24 °C), ferment, divide three pâtons to your calculated weight (± 2 g), shape bâtards, proof and bake as in lesson 06.3.
14. Weigh each bâtard 2 minutes out of the oven and after 1 hour. Compare with the 300 g ordered; calculate this bake's loss.
15. Cost the batch from your receipt (and energy) and give the cost per bâtard.
16. Score one bâtard with the quality rubric and write three lines: what matched the calculation, what did not, what you will change in the pâton weight next time.

## Deliverables

- Part A: four production sheets (PC-02, poolish, PO-01 final dough, CA-01 with levain build), the note to the chef, the capacity check, answers to steps 6-8, the requisition and the costing of one baguette.
- Part B: the calculation sheet, the bake log with temperatures, times and all weights, the measured loss, the cost per bâtard, the rubric score and your three lines.
- Optional: a photo of the three bâtards on the scale, kept with your log.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Dough requirements | Every product correct; campagne pâton found by dividing by (1 − loss) and rounded up | Loss added as × 1.15, or a product forgotten |
| PC-02 with shortage | Pâte fermentée fixed in grams; flour rounded up; batch ≥ need + 2 %; real % written | 15 % kept with too little flour; batch short |
| Pre-ferment splits | Poolish and levain flour, water and yeast subtracted correctly; parts add up to the overall batch | Pre-ferment water added on top; salt put in the poolish |
| Levain build and rule | F = levain ÷ (1 + h + s); yeast limit on final-mixing flour only | Seed forgotten in the total; 0.2 % of all flour |
| Checks | Capacity, salt per 100 g with measured losses, hidden flour in the pâte fermentée | Checks missing |
| Cost | Pre-ferment costed; cost per piece on sold dough; HT = TTC ÷ 1.055; margin on HT | VAT counted as income; losses ignored |
| Home order: control | Poolish ripe; dough 23-25 °C; pâtons to ± 2 g of the calculated weight | Temperatures or weights not recorded |
| Home order: result | Baked weights compared with 300 g and the loss recalculated; rubric completed honestly | No weights after baking; no rubric |
| Safety | Oven, steam and blade handled as in the warning | Steps skipped |

The Academy cannot grade your bread or your photos. Compare your work honestly with this rubric and the reference answers below; this self-assessment does not replace the official practical exam.

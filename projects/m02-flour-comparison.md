# Project: Flour Comparison: One Dough, Three Flours

This project brings module 2 together. You take three flours of different types, test them the way a miller and a baker would (label, Pekar, gluten, water absorption), bake the same lean dough with each, and write a short technical report that tells a bakery which flour to use for which product and how to adjust the formula.

## Brief

You are the ouvrier boulanger at Boulangerie du Marché. The owner is thinking of changing mill and has received three sample bags. She leaves you this note:

> **Note de la patronne — essai de farines**
>
> Trois échantillons du nouveau moulin : farine A (T55), farine B (T65 « tradition française »), farine C (T80 ou T110).
> Fais le même pain avec les trois, compare, et dis-moi pour chaque farine : pour quel produit, combien d'eau, quel comportement en fermentation, et ce que tu en penses au goût. Vérifie aussi que la B peut vraiment servir pour la tradition (étiquette).
> Formule de base : farine 100 %, eau à ajuster (départ 64 %), sel 1,8 %, levure fraîche 1,5 %. Pâte à 24 °C. Pâtons de 300 g.

At home use what you can buy: if French types are not available, use a plain/all-purpose flour (A), a strong bread flour (B) and a wholemeal or high-extraction flour (C), and say so in your report.

## You need

- Three flours, about 600 g each; salt; fresh yeast (or instant dry, one-third of the weight).
- Scale (1 g, and 0.1 g for salt and yeast if you have one), probe thermometer, three bowls, three clear marked containers, scraper, baking trays, metal tray for steam, oven gloves, lame or sharp knife, ruler, a Pekar board (smooth board and spatula), a bowl of water.
- [Production sheet](../templates/production-sheet.md), [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md), [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md), [temperature log](../templates/temperature-log.md).
- Time: about 1 hour of tests, then about 4 hours for the bake (mostly waiting), and 30 minutes to write the report the next day.

> [!WARNING]
> You bake at 240 °C with steam. Use oven gloves; steam goes into a preheated metal tray on the lowest shelf, poured standing back, never into a hot glass dish. Wash hands after handling raw dough and clean flour dust from surfaces with a damp cloth, not by blowing or sweeping dry.

## Steps

### Part A — read and test the flours (about 60 minutes)

1. **Labels.** For each bag copy the name, type, ingredients, protein per 100 g, best-before date (DDM), batch number and storage advice. Decide whether flour B may be used for pain de tradition française (no additives; only bean flour ≤ 2 %, soy flour ≤ 0.5 %, wheat malt flour ≤ 0.3 %) — lessons [02.2](../lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md) and [02.9](../lessons/module-02/lesson-09.md).
2. **Sieve** 50 g of each through a fine sieve and weigh what stays (bran) — lesson [02.1](../lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md).
3. **Pekar test** with the three flours side by side; photograph before and after wetting — lesson [02.2](../lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md).
4. **Gluten washing** (optional but recommended): 50 g of each flour + 29 g water; wash and weigh the wet gluten; stretch-test it — lesson [02.3](../lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md).

### Part B — the same dough, three flours (about 4 hours)

5. **Fill one production sheet per flour** for 500 g of flour: water 64 % to start (320 g), salt 1.8 % (9 g), fresh yeast 1.5 % (7.5 g). Calculate the water temperature for a 24 °C dough (water ≈ 70 − flour temperature − room temperature for hand mixing).
6. **Mix A first.** Hold back 20 g of the water. Mix, rest 10 minutes, add salt, knead 8–10 minutes. Add held-back water only if needed to reach a medium (bâtarde) consistency. Record the total water used: that is the flour's practical absorption.
7. **Mix B and C the same way,** aiming for the same consistency as A by feel. Record the water for each (expect C to need more) — lesson [02.4](../lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md).
8. Record each dough temperature (target 24 °C ± 1 °C) in the temperature log.
9. **Bulk fermentation** in marked containers for about 75 minutes with one fold at 30 minutes. Record the rise of each (× 1.5, × 2…) and the time.
10. **Divide** each dough into 300 g pieces, pre-shape, rest 15 minutes, shape into bâtards of about 20 cm.
11. **Proof** until a finger press springs back slowly; record each proof time (C is often faster).
12. **Bake** at 240 °C with steam, 25–28 minutes, one score per piece. Record the bake time and colour.
13. **Cool** at least 1 hour. Weigh each loaf (bake loss %), measure height and width, cut one of each, photograph the crumb.
14. **Taste** blind if you can (ask someone to label the slices 1-2-3). Keep one loaf of each in a bag and taste again after 24 hours.

### Part C — the technical report (about 30 minutes)

15. Write one page in the format below, with your numbers.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Label reading | All label fields copied; tradition decision correct and justified by the decree's limits | Missing fields, or tradition decision based on the type number only |
| Measurements | Weights to the gram (salt and yeast to 0.1 g if possible); dough temperatures within 24 ± 1 °C; all times recorded | Estimated quantities, missing temperatures or times |
| Water adjustment | Water used recorded for each flour; the higher-type flour took more water and you explain why (bran, protein, damaged starch) | Same water used for all without checking consistency, or no explanation |
| Comparison | Results table complete; differences in volume, crumb, crust, fermentation speed and staling linked to flour type and strength | Table incomplete or differences described without causes |
| Recommendation | Each flour matched to a product with a hydration figure and a reason a professional would accept | Vague advice ("B is best") without numbers |
| Safety and hygiene | Oven and steam handled safely; clean, dust-free work area; bake log kept | Unsafe steam method, or no log |

Remember: the Academy cannot grade your bread. Compare your results honestly with these criteria and the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md); self-assessment helps you learn but does not replace the official practical exam.

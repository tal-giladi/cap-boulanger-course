# Project: Quality Panel

A bakery checks every batch before it reaches the shop: it counts against the order, weighs a sample, looks at the outside, cuts one piece, tastes, and decides what goes on the shelf and how. In Part A you run that quality control on paper for a Saturday morning at a bakery, from its records: counts, weight-control sheets, cut tests, tasting notes and a display plan with mistakes in it. In Part B you bake one batch at home and put it through a full quality panel of your own, with blind tasters, a weight-control sheet and a display. The Academy cannot grade your bread or your photos: compare your work honestly with the rubric and the reference answers; this self-assessment does not replace the official practical exam.

In English: the bakery's counts, sample weights, cut tests, a proofing cabinet reading, a coded tasting and the apprentice's display plan for Saturday morning. You check the batch, decide, report and correct the display before 7:00.

## You need

- Part A: a calculator, the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md), the [non-conformity report](../templates/non-conformity-report.md), the [weight-control sheet](../templates/weight-control-sheet.md) (lesson [15.4](../lessons/module-15/lesson-04.md)).
- Part B: the home equipment of lessons [08.1](../lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md) to [08.4](../lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md) (scale, thermometer, bowl, scraper, couche or cloth, trays, metal steam tray, lame, oven gloves, rack), a serrated bread knife and board, a ruler, a 4 × 4 cm card window, two bought breads of different types, coded paper squares, one to three tasters, the [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md) and the rubric.
- Time: Part A about 90 minutes; Part B one baking day (about 5 hours, 1 hour hands-on, plus the pâte fermentée the evening before) and about 1 hour of evaluation.

> [!WARNING]
> Part B uses a 240-250 °C oven with steam, a razor blade and a bread knife. Dry oven gloves; steam only from a preheated metal tray, never a glass dish; pour the water and stand back. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers and cover the lame after use. Ask tasters about allergies: the breads contain wheat (gluten), and bought breads may contain sesame, milk or egg.

## Steps

### Part A — Saturday quality control on paper (about 90 minutes)

1. **Count against the order.** For each product: ordered, baked, set aside, available, short. Who do you tell, and what?
2. **Weight-control sheets.** For baguettes, traditions and croissants: average, lightest and heaviest, pieces outside ± 3 % of the announced or target weight, loss from the pâton, and salt per 100 g of the lightest baguette and tradition. For the pain de mie: the prepackage check (average against 500 g, any bag under 485 g).
3. **Decisions** for each product from the decision table of lesson 15.4.
4. **Crumb and taste.** From the cut tests and the tasting, score the crumb lines and the taste line of the rubric for the baguette and the tradition, and name the stage of the baguette's crumb fault.
5. **Non-conformity reports.** Write the croissant report (with the proofing cabinet reading) and a short note on the burnt traditions.
6. **Display.** Find what is wrong in the apprentice's plan and write the corrected plan.
7. **Hand-over brief** to the sales staff at 6:55, in five lines.

### Part B — your home quality panel (one baking day)

8. **Bake** PC-02 on 500 g of flour (lesson 08.1, with the home pâte fermentée made the evening before): two baguettes of 270 g and four rolls of 60 g, pâtons weighed and written down. Or TR-01 (lesson [09.2](../lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)) if you prefer tradition.
9. **Exterior** (lesson [15.1](../lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)) after 30-60 minutes: count, lengths, colour on the 1-5 scale, the six exterior lines of the rubric, any pattern.
10. **Weight-control sheet** (lesson 15.4) after 1 hour: every piece, average, spread, loss, salt per 100 g of the lightest piece, decision.
11. **Crumb** (lesson [15.2](../lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)) after full cooling: cut one baguette, window count, press test, tracing; crumb lines of the rubric.
12. **Blind tasting** (lesson [15.3](../lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)): your baguette and two bought breads, coded, with the tasting sheet; intensities, then liking; reveal and compare with the salt you calculated and the labels.
13. **Display** (lesson [15.5](../lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)): set the products out with placards (name, invented price, allergens) and a written brief for a helper; check after 2 hours.
14. **Report and change.** One non-conformity report or diagnosis for your weakest point, and three lines: what met the standard, what did not, the one change for your next bake.

## Deliverables

- Part A: the count table, four weight-control sheets with decisions, the rubric lines scored, the croissant report and the tradition note, the corrected display plan and the brief.
- Part B: the bake log, the exterior record, the weight-control sheet, the crumb record (count, press test, tracing or photo), the tasting sheets, the placards and brief, the full rubric score out of 20, one report or diagnosis and your three lines.
- Optional: photos of the display and of the cut section from a fixed spot, kept with your log for comparison with your next panel.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Counting | Every product counted against the order; shortfalls found and passed on at once | Count skipped; shortfall noticed only when a customer asks |
| Weight-control sheets | Average, spread, pieces outside tolerance, loss and salt of the lightest piece correct; decisions follow from the numbers | Average only; no tolerance; salt on the average piece |
| Prepackage check | Average compared with the nominal quantity and each bag with nominal minus the tolerated shortfall | Only individual bags checked; or every bag must reach 500 g |
| Exterior | Fixed order, lengths and weights measured, colour scored on the scale, pattern looked for | Impressions; judged warm or in changing light |
| Crumb | Cut after full cooling; window count and press test; faults traced to a stage with evidence | Cut warm; one ideal crumb for every product |
| Sensory analysis | Blind, coded, changing order; intensities and liking apart; one note linked to the process | Open tasting of your own bread; "good" or "bad" only |
| Display | Cooled products, by family, placards correct, each product held the right way, out of the sun; brief written | Warm or stacked products; missing or wrong placards |
| Reporting | Facts, measurements, action and proposal; no blame | Vague or personal; no numbers |
| Safety and hygiene | Oven, blades and allergens handled as in the warning; tongs or paper for serving | Steps skipped |

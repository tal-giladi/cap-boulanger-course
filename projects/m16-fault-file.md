# Project: Fault File

This project brings Module 16 together. You diagnose six faulty batches from a bakery's records, build your own fault file from the bakes you have made since Module 4, reproduce one fault on purpose beside a control and report it, and finish with a one-page bench card that tells an apprentice what to check first for the ten most common faults. The aim is the professional habit behind competency C4.4: every fault becomes a record with a cause, an action and a prevention.

## Brief

You are the ouvrier boulanger at Boulangerie du Marché. The owner leaves you this note:

> **Note de la patronne — le classeur des défauts**
>
> Les apprentis refont toujours les mêmes erreurs. Je veux un classeur des défauts pour le fournil.
> 1. Analyse les six fiches ci-dessous : constat, étape, preuves, cause probable, action, prévention.
> 2. Ajoute tes propres défauts, avec tes relevés.
> 3. Refais un défaut exprès à côté d'un témoin, pour montrer la différence, et fais la fiche de non-conformité.
> 4. Une fiche d'une page à afficher au fournil : les dix défauts les plus courants et ce qu'on vérifie d'abord.

In English: build a fault binder for the bakehouse. (1) Analyse the six records below: facts, stage, evidence, probable cause, action, prevention. (2) Add your own faults with your records. (3) Reproduce one fault on purpose beside a control piece and write the non-conformity report. (4) A one-page sheet for the wall: the ten most common faults and what to check first.

### The six records

| | Product | What the shop or the baker saw | Records |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baguettes PC-02, Tuesday | All 48: small, tall, dark reddish crust, torn along the side, tight crumb | Water from the new tank at 9 °C; dough 19 °C (TPV 24 °C); pointage and apprêt as the sheet |
| 2 | Bâtards, Wednesday | 10 of 30 flat, pale on top, cuts closed, holes under the crust | Load 1 (20 pieces) fine at 6:10; load 2 (10 pieces) at 6:40, same cabinet at 25 °C; poke test of load 2 not written |
| 3 | Pain de mie PM-01, Thursday | Sides caved in, loaves pale on the sides | Tins left in the oven's entrance 25 minutes before unmoulding; core not recorded |
| 4 | Tradition TR-01, Friday | White, bland, regular crumb; volume normal | Mixed by a new apprentice: 2nd speed 7 min, salt at the end, no autolyse |
| 5 | Croissants CR-01, Saturday | Bready interior, few layers, no butter on the tray | Room during tourage 27 °C; butter 17 °C at lock-in; "pâte grasse" written at turn 2 |
| 6 | Pains aux raisins, Sunday | Pale, wet bases; spirals unrolled | Baked 14 min (sheet 18-20 min); cream spread about 5 mm thick; tails on top |

<details><summary>Reference diagnoses (open after you have written yours)</summary>

1. **Under-fermented by a cold dough** (lessons 04.4, 05.2). Every piece → a dough cause; 5 °C under target is about 1.07⁵ ≈ 1.4 times slower, so the sheet's times left it young. Action: longer, warmer pointage and apprêt judged by the poke test. Prevention: calculate the water from the base temperature whenever the supply changes; measure every dough.
2. **Second load over-proofed** (lessons 14.1, 16.1). Only load 2, which waited 30 minutes longer at the same temperature. Prevention: proof group 2 about 3-4 °C cooler or shape it later; write the poke test for each load.
3. **Left in the tin, probably under-baked** (lesson 10.5). Condensation softens the walls of a loaf left in its tin; pale sides suggest the bake was also short. Prevention: unmould at once; bake until golden on the sides and at least about 90 °C at the core, recorded.
4. **Over-oxidised dough** (lessons 03.4, 09.2). Intensive second speed and late salt bleach the crumb and destroy aroma. Prevention: the TR-01 mixing method posted on the mixer; apprentice trained.
5. **Butter melted into the dough** (lessons 11.3, 11.4). Butter above about 15 °C in a 27 °C room smears into the détrempe and the layers fuse. Prevention: butter at about 13 °C, laminate in the coolest window, chill as soon as the dough feels greasy.
6. **Under-baked, cream too thick, tails not tucked** (lesson 12.2). Two shaping faults and one bake fault. Prevention: an even layer of about 2 mm of cream, tail underneath, bake until golden underneath.

</details>

## You need

- Your bake logs, temperature logs and photos from Modules 4-12.
- For Part C: the equipment and PD-01 ingredients of lesson [16.2](../lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md), [16.3](../lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md) or [16.4](../lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md), depending on the fault you choose: scale, probe and oven thermometers, bowl, scraper, lidded container, baking paper, tray, metal steam tray, oven gloves, lame, serrated knife, ruler.
- Templates: [bake log](../templates/bake-log.md), [non-conformity report](../templates/non-conformity-report.md), [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md), [production sheet](../templates/production-sheet.md).
- Reference: [Troubleshooting](../references/troubleshooting.md) and, if you practise in Israel, [Flour in Israel](../references/flour-in-israel.md).
- Time: about 1 hour for Part A, 1-2 hours for Part B, about 4 hours for Part C (mostly waiting), 30 minutes for Part D.

> [!WARNING]
> Part C uses a 240-250 °C oven with steam. Use dry oven gloves; make steam only in a metal tray preheated on the lowest shelf, never in a glass dish; pour 100-150 mL of hot water quickly, close the door and step back. Score with the blade moving away from your fingers, cover the lame after use, and probe hot bread with an oven glove on.

## Steps

### Part A — the six records (about 1 hour)

1. For each record, write six short lines: **constat** (what, where, how many), **stage**, **evidence** (the record that proves it), **probable cause**, **action now**, **prevention**. Use the five-step method of lesson [16.1](../lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md).
2. For record 1, calculate how much slower the dough fermented with the 7 % rule.
3. Only then open the reference diagnoses and mark your own.

### Part B — your own fault file (1-2 hours)

4. Go through your logs from Module 4 onwards. Choose **at least six faults**, from at least three of the four families (volume and shape, crust and colour, crumb and taste, viennoiserie). If you have not baked viennoiserie yet, use the three croissants you cut in lesson [16.5](../lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md).
5. Write each as one fault-file entry:

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Product, date, lesson | |
| Constat (what, where, how many, when seen) | |
| Family and stage | |
| Evidence from my records (or "no record") | |
| Probable cause, and the cause I ruled out with what evidence | |
| Action taken at the time | |
| Prevention: the one change, with a number | |
| Did the change work? (next bake of that product) | |
| Lesson that teaches the cause | |

6. Where your log had no record to decide, write that down: missing evidence is a finding.

### Part C — one fault on purpose (about 4 hours)

7. Choose **one** fault and its control, from the practices of lessons 16.2-16.4:
   - **Pain plat by loose shaping** (16.2): bâtard A tight, B loose.
   - **Pain cintré by under-proofing** (16.2): A at full proof, C loaded at about 20 minutes.
   - **Croûte terne** (16.3): A covered and steamed, B skinned or C without steam.
   - **Gummy crumb** (16.4): A baked to at least 93 °C, B taken out early; both cut at 15 and 90 minutes.
8. Write a short [production sheet](../templates/production-sheet.md) first, with the one difference between control and fault marked.
9. Bake, cool, measure (height, width, cut opening or core temperature as relevant), cut and score both pieces with the rubric.
10. Write the non-conformity report for the faulty piece as if it were a batch of 30: facts and measurements, action, probable cause, prevention.

### Part D — the bench card (30 minutes)

11. On one page, list the **ten faults** you think an apprentice meets most often. For each, one line: **fault → the first thing to check → the lesson**. Example: "Pâle, plat, grignes fermées → température de pâte et test du doigt → 04.7".
12. Check every line against the [troubleshooting reference](../references/troubleshooting.md) and the lessons; correct any number that does not match the course (temperatures, proof range, steam method).

## Deliverables

- Part A: six six-line diagnoses with the record 1 calculation, marked against the reference.
- Part B: at least six fault-file entries from your own bakes, in at least three families.
- Part C: production sheet, measurements and rubric scores of control and fault, one non-conformity report, photos of both cut pieces (for yourself; the Academy does not grade them).
- Part D: the one-page bench card with ten faults.

## Self-evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Meets standard | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Record diagnoses (Part A) | Each case names the stage and the record that proves the cause; action and prevention given; record 1 calculation about 1.4 | Causes guessed from the symptom; no evidence named |
| Method | Constat written before the cause; "how many pieces" used; competing causes ruled out with evidence | Cause named first; alternatives not considered |
| Own fault file (Part B) | Six or more entries, three or more families, each with evidence or "no record", one numbered change and the lesson | Fewer entries; vague causes ("bad dough"); several changes at once |
| Controlled fault (Part C) | Control and fault differ in one variable only; measured and scored; the difference is visible and explained | Several variables changed; no measurement; no control |
| Non-conformity report | Facts and quantity, action, probable cause, prevention; no blame | Impressions, blame or missing prevention |
| Bench card (Part D) | Ten faults, each with a first check and a lesson; numbers match the course | Fewer than ten; numbers wrong or missing |
| Safety and hygiene | Oven, steam, lame and knife used safely; station clean; dough covered except where the fault requires it | Unsafe steam method or cutting technique |

Remember: the Academy cannot grade your bread. Compare your results honestly with these criteria and the [quality rubric](../templates/quality-rubric.md); self-assessment helps you learn but does not replace the official practical exam. How production faults are assessed in the exam is in [The CAP Boulanger Exam](../references/cap-exam.md).
